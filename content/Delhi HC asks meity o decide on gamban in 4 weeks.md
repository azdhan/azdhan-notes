 **# Delhi HC directs MeitY to decide on Gamban's Section 69A blocking within four weeks

The Delhi High Court directed the Ministry of Electronics and IT (MeitY) to decide within four weeks on the petition filed by UK-based anti-gambling-addiction app Gamban challenging the Section 69A blocking order of its website and app in India. 

The HC said MeitY should decide whether to lift or continue the blocking order based on the Information Technology (Procedure and Safeguards for Blocking for Access of Information by Public) Rules, 2009 (Blocking Rules), including by giving Gamban a hearing.

Justice Amit Mahajan declined to grant MeitY further time and said the Ministry has to take the decision itself, irrespective of whether the concerned nodal agency, I4C responds. The court also gave Gamban liberty to approach it again if MeitY does not act in accordance with the rules, or if the company still has a grievance after the decision.

A brief background of the case: Gamban is a UK-based, nine-year-old app that helps people recovering from gambling addiction by blocking their access to betting, casino and online money-gaming apps and websites. It does not offer any gambling services itself. Its website and app became inaccessible in India on February 7, 2023, after [MeitY issued blocking orders under Section 69A of the IT Act](https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-meitys-section-69a-blocked-anti-gambling-app-gamban-3-years-no-reasons-shared/) against a batch of gambling applications. Gamban's petition claims it was blocked by mistake.

Gamban says it wrote to MeitY five times before receiving the May 6, 2025 email, which cited concerns about user data privacy, "super app" access to banking apps and browser history, and increased gambling ads after a subscription ends. MeitY also asked Gamban to undergo a security audit by a government-approved agency, without sharing a copy of the blocking order. [Gamban filed its writ petition](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-inside-petition-anti-gambling-addiction-app-gamban-challenges-meitys-section-69a-blocking-order/) on July 9, 2026, naming the Union of India as the sole respondent.

Five months, no decision on Gamban's representation: The Delhi HC noted that after MeitY's email of May 6, 2025, which was Gamban's first response from the Ministry in more than three years, Gamban submitted a detailed representation on April 21, 2026. Even after about five months, MeitY had not taken any decision on it.

Blocked without notice or a hearing, argued Gamban: Appearing for Gamban, Advocate Arshdeep Singh Khurana, along with Advocates Akshat Gupta, Jay Sayta and Sakshi Tikmany, argued that the [Information Technology (Procedure and Safeguards for Blocking for Access of Information by Public) Rules, 2009](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/10/91f628cb778f94e76df356bc3fd3ac60.pdf) require the affected party to be heard, and that this was not done. He also referred to MeitY's later email saying that Gamban's request for unblocking had been forwarded to the "Concerned Nodal Agency" for further examination, under the Rule 6 of Blocking Rules. During the hearing the Delhi HC also noted that Gamban's website and mobile application were blocked without a prior show-cause notice and reasons, contrary to the Blocking Rules. 

MeitY says inputs from I4C and MHA are pending: MeitY's counsel told the court that the Ministry was still waiting for inputs from nodal agencies, namely the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) and the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA).When the court asked to clarify which is the nodal agency that is involved in the blocking order, she said I4C and also added that the blocking order was served under the MHA’s direction.

MeitY’s lawyer also submitted that I4C, Google and Apple, where the Gamban application is hosted on respective playstores, should be added as parties to the petition, since the Ministry was waiting for their inputs. This is similar to the preliminary objection MeitY raised at the [first hearing in July](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-urgency-delhi-hc-gamban-blocking-chearing/), when it argued that Gamban had named only the Union of India and not the intermediaries that are actually enforcing the block.

MeitY has to decide, says the HC: The bench did not accept the delay as a reason for further time. It said the decision rests with MeitY, and that it does not matter if the nodal agencies do not respond. The court also indicated that MeitY should, at the least, give Gamban reasons for the block. It declined to give the Ministry more time.

This is in line with what the Delhi HC said at the previous hearing, when Justice Swarana Kanta Sharma told MeitY's counsel, "Please, just decide," and [suggested](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-urgency-delhi-hc-gamban-blocking-chearing/) that the Ministry give Gamban a personal hearing. “I’m not saying that you do this or you do that. Please, just decide. Let people do whatever they want to. They are always in confusion about ho kya raha hai (what is happening)”, said Justice Sharma in July 2026.
