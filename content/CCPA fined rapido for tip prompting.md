# CCPA fines Rapido Rs. 10 lakh, orders it to stop ‘dark pattern’ tipping prompts

Download a copy of the order here: [pdf]

  

You might have seen these prompts on the Rapido app while booking a bike-taxi or an auto or a cab: 

- "Captains aren't accepting at Rs. X price” 
    
- “Try adding more +10, +20, +30"
    
- Or, something similar to: "Higher the price, higher the chance of getting a ride"
    

The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) has ruled such design as a dark patten and fined Rs. 10 lakh. It also ordered the raid-hailing company to immediately discontinue such prompts, according to CCPA’s order on August 31, 2026.  

“Although the opposite party [Rapido] has contended that the tip is voluntary and that the algorithm continues to function irrespective of whether any additional amount is offered, the CCPA finds that the design, sequencing and language of the impugned prompts, particularly when triggered during periods projected by the platform itself as high-demand or low-availability scenarios, amount to a form of behavioural nudging that induces a sense of urgency and apprehension of denial of service in the mind of the consumer. Such nudging …, through interface design, to subvert the consumer's autonomy and free decision-making by creating an apprehension that declining to pay more will result in denial or delay of an already-booked service. The CCPA is, accordingly, prima facie satisfied that the impugned prompts constitute a "dark pattern" within the meaning of Clause 2(e) of the said Guidelines (2023).” – CCPA Order. 

The CCPA askes Rapido to submit a compliance report on the above within 15 days of receiving the order.

Some background: The CCPA took up the matter after receiving a complaint dated May 16, 2025, alleging unfair trade practices through a pre-ride tipping feature and "exploitative" dynamic pricing on an app-based taxi aggregator, without naming a specific platform. Examining multiple ride-hailing and bike-taxi platforms in response, the CCPA found that:

When wait times run longer, sometimes due to factors like driver unavailability or traffic, the app displays prompts nudging riders to add more money through something similar to the above-mentioned prompts

What is Rapido's defence: In its reply to CCPA, and during hearings, Rapido defended itself through these arguments:

- Rapido described the prompts as akin to a real-time fare negotiation, comparable to bargaining offline, and said its terms and conditions disclose that displayed fares are estimates only, not guaranteed.
    
- The prompts for asking for higher pay appear only if a ride request goes unaccepted for some time, is not mandatory to act on, and the matching algorithm keeps running regardless of whether a rider adds money.
    
- It denied "confirm shaming," saying the prompts state facts and create no fear, shame or guilt in riders.
    

What the investigation found: The CCPA’s Investigation Report, submitted on January 21, 2026, found that:

- Although tipping is technically voluntary and the full amount goes to the driver, the design puts non-tipping riders at an indirect disadvantage, since drivers are incentivized to prioritize tipped rides, which inturn affects acceptance rates for normal users and possibly higher wait times too..
    
- The earlier prompt "Captains aren't accepting at ₹33" had since been replaced with a new one, "Skip the wait! Get a ride faster by adding extra," which the report found now appears immediately and every time a booking search starts, which is reportedly contrary to Rapido's claim that such prompts show up only in limited situations.
    

The order said that this is in violation of:

- [Consumer Protection Act, 2019](https://ncdrc.nic.in/bare_acts/CPA2019.pdf), 
    
- [Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023,](https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/admin/cmsfiles/whatsnews/%E0%A4%A1%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%95_%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%88%E0%A4%9F%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A8_%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%87_%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%A3_%E0%A4%94%E0%A4%B0_%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A8_%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%87_%E0%A4%B2%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%8F_%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%97%E0%A4%A6%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%95_%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A7%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%A4_2023_whatsnews.pdf) 
    
- [Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements, 2022,](http://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/CCPA_Notification_1732707665.pdf) and 
    
- [Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020.](http://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/E%20commerce%20rules_1732703966.pdf)
    

Rapido’s official emails weren’t working, says CCPA: The CCPA found that Rapido had allowed one of its official email addresses, captaincare@rapido.bike, to lapse into inactivity and CCPA’s notices to that address were reportedly bounced back on both occasions as undeliverable, with an automated "non-existent account" error. 

However, notices sent to Rapido's other two official addresses, nodalofficer@rapido.bike and shoutout@rapido.bike, were delivered but went unanswered. The CCPA said this meant Rapido could not claim it never received the notices, and that companies are required under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, to keep official contact details functional and reachable.
