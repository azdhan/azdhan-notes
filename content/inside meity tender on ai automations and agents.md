**# Inside MeitY's plan for AI adoption across Digital India platforms

Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), has released a [](https://www.negd.gov.in)tender inviting AI-capable agencies to supply AI/ML professionals for a "Unified AI Team" that will design, develop, and deploy AI-driven services across Digital India platforms like UMANG, DigiLocker, and other government systems.

MeitY issued this tender on 7 August 2026 for the central e-governance division, National e-Governance Division (NeGD). The tender outlines the AI services the seletected AI-cabale agencies are supposed to handle, and the technical infrastructure they are ought to implement. Therefore, this gives a peek into how MeitY intends to ride the wave of AI adoption.

The selected agencies are supposed to work across these seven domains:

- Setting Up Inference Server: Setting up an inference server for NeGD using open-source LLMs on NeGD-provided infrastructure
    
- Cognitive Analytics & Decision Intelligence: Building models for predictive analytics, optimization, and governance dashboards.
    
- Computer Vision & Document Intelligence: OCR, object detection, and automated form processing for administrative systems.
    
- Conversational & Assistive AI: Developing multilingual chatbots and voice assistants for government platforms
    
- Generative AI & LLM Engineering: Fine-tuning Large Language Models and designing RAG pipelines.
    
- Data Engineering & MLOps: Designing data pipelines, deploying models, and managing lifecycle through CI/CD automation. 
    
- Responsible AI & Governance: Ensuring AI policy compliance, explainability, and ethical risk mitigation.
    

NeGD intends to establish an in-house server to deploy and serve open-source models (LLMs, SLMs, etc.) behind stable APIs for use by any NeGD or DIC project. The infrastructure will run on approved government cloud services like NIC or MeghRaj.

RFE states that the system architecture should support switching between two model categories:

- Commercial models: Including enterprise-grade AI/ML models from companies like OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, AWS, and Azure.
    
- Open-source models: Open source LLMs like LLaMA and Mistral to ensure security, compliance, and adherence to data localization norms.
    

The RFE notes that the listed activities are illustrative and additional tasks may include othe technical workload like mitigating vulnerabilities remediation or evidence generation for compliance etc

The RFE also lists the following six AI services that deployed AI-agencies are supposed to work: 

1. Document Summarization and Classification: This service is supposed to generate concise summaries and structured insights from large documents like cabinet notes, tender abstracts, policy documents, circulars, scheme documents, notifications, and unstructured notices. The goal is to make these documents more accessible and actionable for citizens and call-center staff.
    
2. Multilingual conversational AI  Chatbots: These can be used for conversing with the government documents (annual reports, FAQs, PDFs) as a knowledge base to answer user queries. It states that these can be integrated on platforms like UMANG, DigiLocker, and citizen query systems, processing and retrieving information to provide accurate, context-aware responses in multiple languages.
    
3. Voice-based form fillingnand web navigation: A multilingual, voice-driven service enabling citizens and field workers to complete structured forms using speech. Additionally, the government intends to use speech to execute navigation actions on government portals possibly in multiple Indian languages. 
    
4. OCR processing of documents: This service intends to automate the extraction and validation of information from certificates, IDs, forms, and scanned PDFs.
    
5. Live face detection for ID verifiaation: This AI service is for image matching with liveness detection to strengthen identity verification, reduce fraud, and minimize reliance on manual KYC checks. Use cases include attendance tracking, eKYC, and digital onboarding.
    
6. AI Agents for Government Applications: AI agents will be built for government applications like DigiLocker, using tools such as LangGraph or CrewAI. These agents are supposed to do the following functions:
    

- Securely invoke approved APIs and tools to complete transactions while maintaining audit trails and human checkpoints for irreversible actions.
    
- Build agent-based AI capabilities to automate and assist government workflows, starting with DigiLocker.
    
- Develop modular, reusable agent frameworks (e.g., LangGraph, CrewAI) for adoption across ministries and platforms.
    
- Design agents to be discoverable and integrable by third-party developers.
    
- Incorporate human-in-the-loop validation for sensitive actions to ensure accountability.
    
- Maintain comprehensive audit trails, access logs, and traceability for all agent decisions and API calls.
    
- Demonstrate end-to-end reference implementations, such as agent-driven document retrieval and verification via DigiLocker APIs.
    
- Develop specialized agent capabilities for querying large databases or structured data tables, enabling natural language query processing and analytical insights.
    

According to the REF document, these agents are supposed to function as secure, policy-aligned digital assistants capable of understanding user intent, orchestrating tasks, and being responsible.
