The judge held that the material on record made out "a very strong prima facie case," finding that the unauthorised AI content violated Zinta's personality rights, publicity rights, and moral rights under Copyright Act, 1957, framing personality rights  as flowing from the right to freedom of speech and expression under Article 19(1)(a) and the right to life and dignity under Article 21 of the Constitution.

The Bombay High Court has granted actress Preity Zinta interim injunctive relief in a suit against Google  and 16 other over AI-generated deepfakes, chatbot personas, and merchandise that allegedly misappropriate her personality and publicity rights. 

The order, passed by Justice Madhav J. Jamdar on July 8, follows a leave petition filed under Clause XII of the Letters Patent that was allowed by the same court on June 16.

 
 


## What the court found

According to the court order, Zinta's advocates submitted an extensive range of unauthorised AI-generated content: deepfake videos, morphed and superimposed photographs, memes, AI voice simulations, and chatbot personas built in her likeness without consent. Lawyers representing the actress submitted a list of over 275 infringing YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, and X links, along with supporting exhibits documenting AI-generated images and chatbot content across several platforms.

The court noted that some of the platforms named as defendants host AI chatbot services and AI image-generation tools that "commercially exploit different facets" of Zinta's personality. The court order noted Kash Collective as the accused of selling merchandise using her name and image without her permission. Other respondent in the case includes, domain registrars to seek information of the anonymous owners of these websites. 



## The reliefs granted

The court's ad-interim order, which will hold until further orders, does several things:

-  Restrains AI chatbot and image-generation platforms) from using Zinta's name, nicknames ("PZ" and/or "PGZ"), photograph, likeness, caricature, voice, mannerisms, or persona — through any means including AI, generative AI, deepfakes, or face-morphing — for commercial or personal gain without her consent.
- **Directs Google, X, and Meta** (Defendants 1–3) to take down or block access to the roughly 275 URLs listed in the plaint's Exhibit K within 72 hours of the order being uploaded to the court's website — with one entry excluded after Zinta's counsel flagged it as wrongly included.
- **Creates a standing takedown mechanism**: once notified in writing of further URLs hosting similar infringing content, Google, Instagram and X must act within 72 hours, though they retain liberty to approach the court if they dispute that flagged content is actually infringing.
- **Orders specific platforms** to remove identified chatbot personas, AI-generated characters, GIFs, and images bearing Zinta's likeness, with some defendants permitted to seek clearer identifying details if they can't locate the content from the exhibits alone.
- **Requires one AI platform (Defendant No. 5)** to add "Preity Zinta" and "Preity G Zinta" to its character-creation and search blocklists, and to take steps to prevent new AI characters from being built using her image and voice.
- **Restrains the merchandise seller and unknown-defendant class** from passing off goods or services as endorsed by Zinta.
- **Sets a disclosure mechanism** requiring domain registrars to hand over registrant details for infringing domains within seven days of a written request backed by an affidavit.

Counsel for the defendants did not oppose the reliefs sought, though several — appearing for Google, Meta, and others — flagged that future takedown requests involving content they consider "genuine" (i.e., not actually infringing) may need to be contested, with liberty granted to approach the court if disputes arise. Counsel for Google also confirmed that Tenor Inc. (Defendant No. 9, the GIF platform), being wholly controlled by Google, would have any order against it complied with by Google itself.

The court also referenced the intermediary due-diligence obligations under Rule 3(1)(b) of the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, and cited the Bombay High Court's earlier order in _Akshay Hari Om Bhatia v. John Doe & Ors._ (2025 SCC OnLine Bom 4044) as precedent on the point.

Defendants have six weeks to file their reply affidavits, with two weeks for any rejoinder. The matter is next listed for September 3, 2026.

## Why it matters

Zinta's case is the latest in a growing line of Indian personality-rights suits targeting AI-generated impersonation — following similar dynamic/"John Doe"-style injunctions won by other celebrities and public figures against deepfakes and unauthorised AI likeness use, including the Delhi High Court's recent personality rights ruling involving Allu Arjun. What distinguishes this order is its explicit reach into _generative_ AI infrastructure itself — not just hosting platforms but AI chatbot services and character-creation tools, which are directed to pre-emptively blocklist a named individual's identity rather than simply respond to takedown notices after the fact.



# Bombay HC grants Preity Zinta interim relief against AI deepfakes, chatbots, and merchandise misusing her Personality

Download the court order [here](https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/2026/07/08/preity-g-zinta-vs-google-llc--685264.pdf) 

Hindi actress Preity Zinta has secured an ad-interim injunction from the Bombay High Court against Google, X, Meta, and several AI platforms over deepfakes, chatbot personas, and merchandise that misuse her name, image, and voice without consent. Justice Madhav J. Jamdar passed the order in, directing Google, X, and Meta to take down or block access to roughly 275 infringing URLs within 72 hours.

Zinta had first approached the High Court in June seeking and then Justice Abhay Ahuja granted agreed to sue intermediaries and others. Read MediaNama's previous reporting [here](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-preity-zinta-deepfake-lawsuit-google/). 

**"'Personality Rights' and 'Publicity Rights'" are protected by Article 19(1)(a). noted Bombay HC:** Justice Jamdar held that the material on record made out "a very strong prima facie case," finding that AI-generated content violated Zinta's personality rights, publicity rights, and moral rights under the Copyright Act, 1957. The court also noted these personality rights flows from the right to freedom of speech and expression under Article 19(1)(a) and the right to life and dignity under Article 21 of the Constitution.

According to the court order, actress' lawyers also submitted the deepfake videos, morphed and superimposed photographs, memes, AI voice simulations, and chatbot personas built in Zinta's likeness. Senior Advocate Venkatesh Dhond, appearing for Zinta, told the court that certain content was also "misleading, defamatory, explicit or otherwise objectionable."

**Bombay HC's ruling:** 
- An unnamed AI platform was directed to add "Preity Zinta" and "Preity G Zinta" to its character-creation and search blocklists, and to take steps preventing new AI characters from being built using her image and voice.
- The court ordered the unnamed defendants not to use Zinta's name, nicknames, photo, likeness (like her dimpled smile), voice, mannerisms, or character without her permission, including AI, deepfakes, chatbots and face-morphing.  
- Bombay HC told that the accused  cannot use Zinta's name, nicknames, photo, smile, voice, mannerisms, or any part of her likeness without her permission. This includes using AI, deepfakes, or face-morphing. They are also not allowed to do this for money or personal benefit.
- If Google, X, or Meta get a written notice about new infringing content similar to what was already listed, they must act within 72 hours and they have the right to challenge such takedown requests. 

Kash Collective, named as Defendant int he case, was accused of selling merchandise bearing Zinta's name, image, and likeness without authorisation. It is important to note that the same entity (via kashcollectiveco.com)  was also found selling unauthorised Aishwarya Rai Bachchan merchandise in a Delhi High Court order last year. Domain name registrars named as defendants were separately directed to disclose the identities of owners of the alleged privacy violating websites behind infringing domains within seven days of a written request backed by an affidavit.
