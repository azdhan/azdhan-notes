**# Government directs ride-hailing apps to remove pre-ride tipping prompts

Access the original advisory here: [ [Original link](https://morth.gov.in/backend/documents/uploaded/1786532826_y7Cm8mbmmL.pdf) | [Archived at MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/1786532826_y7Cm8mbmmL.pdf) ]

The Ministry of Road Transport & Highways (MoRTH) has directed all motor vehicle aggregators to immediately comply with Clause 14.15 of the Motor Vehicle Aggregator Guidelines, 2025, which prohibits the display of tipping prompts before or during a ride.

The advisory stated the following prompts as an example: 

- "Advance Tip",
    
- "Choose an Add-on", or
    
- "Improve your chances of quicker ride confirmation" .
    

In this advisory letter dated August 11, addressing to all ride hailing aggregators, the Ministry stated that tipping must remain entirely voluntary and only visible after journey completion. The letter states that Motar Vehicle Guielines guidelines explicitly ban any feature, prompt, or interface element that "directly or indirectly encourages, induces, or creates an impression that payment of any additional amount may improve ride confirmation, driver acceptance, driver allocation, waiting time, or quality of service."

Therefore, the MoRTH has ordered aggregators to review their mobile applications and digital platforms and remove such non-compliant features. 

If the platform wishes to have a tipping feature, it should prompt the user for the tips at the end of ride and 100% of the tip amount must be credited to the driver without platform deductions.

In November 2025, MediaNama [reported](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-advance-tipping-indias-ride-hailing-experience/), based on LocalCircles survey, that 78% of app-based taxi users continue to face intrusive prompts on ride-hailing apps, including nudges to add an advance tip, despite months of scrutiny from the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA).

“Riders who tip in advance often get picked up faster in busy hours. It is not clear whether the algorithm gives them priority or if drivers themselves accept those trips faster. Either way, riders who pay extra get better service and that defeats the idea of a fair platform.” – [Shaik Salauddin, TGPWU (November 2025)](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-advance-tipping-indias-ride-hailing-experience/)

In May 2025, The Minister of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution of India, Pralhad Joshi, [flagged](https://www.medianama.com/2025/05/223-ccpa-sends-notice-uber-advance-tip-feature/) the “unethical and exploitative” practice of ‘Advance Tip’ on Uber app. Following this, CCPA also sent the ride-hailing app a notice and sought an explanation.
