**# TRAI allows AI agents in telecom support, mandates 24x7 

Telecom operators in India can now use artificial intelligence (AI) agents to handle consumer complaints, says Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI), according to the [press release](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-11/PR_No146of2026.pdf) it released after the initial consulataions in May 2026.

TRAI brought this mandate to make customer 24/7 service available by making the amendments to [Telecom Consumers Complaint Redressal (Fourth Amendment) Regulations, 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-11/Regulation_02112026.pdf), on November 2. Before this, the customer complaint centers have to run atleast 16 hours, as per the previous regulations. 

What has changed:

- Round-the-clock support: Complaint Centres must run 24x7 instead of 16 hours. Operators can use human staff, automated systems, AI agents or a mix, but humans must be available for at least 16 hours daily.
    
- Feedback and board reporting: Operators must run an online feedback survey after every resolved complaint or appeal and publish the results quarterly. They must also place a quarterly report on complaints, appeals and survey results before their CEO or board.
    
- Oversight committee: Each operator must set up a Consumer Grievance Redressal Oversight Committee with consumer-organisation representatives. It must meet at least once a year.
    
- Appeals down to 15 days: The window to appeal a decision shrinks from 30 days to 15. The Advisory Committee stage is scrapped. Additionally, consumers can file complaints, appeals and service requests through the operator's mobile app.
    
- Enforcement: TRAI can audit records and review performance reports. It can impose financial disincentives for non-compliant disposal of complaints and for late or missing quarterly reports.
    

These rules apply to Internet Service Providers with more than 10,000 subscribers at the end of the previous financial year. The earlier alternative test, annual turnover above Rs. 5 crore, is now dropped, as per the new amended regulations.

AI agentic customer support with ‘appropriate human oversight’: “Consumer support through Complaint Centres will be available round the clock on all days of the week, instead of the existing 16-hour arrangement. Such support may be provided through human representatives, automated systems, Al agents, other customer-centric technological solutions, or a combination thereof. Human assistance shall be available for at least sixteen hours daily. Technology-based solutions shall function with appropriate human oversight. This will ensure ease and convenience to consumers and gives flexibility to service providers in manning the Complaint Centres.”

Who is to be held accountable for the AI agentic actions? However, the press release does not define "appropriate human oversight". Therefore, it is not clear whether it means that a human must review each AI decision, sample conversations, or only provide mandatory human intervention in case of escalations. Additonally, the perpetual liability for hte AI agentic actions is also unclear: Who is to be held accountable when an AI agent mishandles a complaint, the telco operator or the AI service provoder/vendor?

In an attempt to bring an accountability framework to the AI agentic decisions in telecom operations, TRAI has separately released a new draft consultation. The draft  proposes that telecom operators will be held accountable for the outcomes of every artificial intelligence (AI) system they use, including if they are depending on the third-party service providers or vendors. The deadline to submit the responses is October 30. Read more about it [here](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-telcos-third-party-trais-anthropic/). 

“What we definitely need is a very light-touch progressive enabling regulatory system. We need an accountability system which does not seek transparency into systems but seeks transparency and demands the highest quality of service and excellence to what we deliver to our subscribers,” – Ambika Khurana, Chief Regulatory & Corporate Affairs Officer, Vodafone Idea. 

Read Medianama’s coverage of the COAI discussion [here](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-telcos-dot-risk-based-ai-regulations/), where they discussed the accountability framework and the feasibility fo human in the loop for telecom operations.

Why does the 24x7 mandate matter for AI companies? Nikhil Pahwa, founder and editor of MediaNama, said telecom operators "probably receive the highest number of support calls of any business in the country." He said AI voice technology has been rising, and that a 24x7 expectation makes telecom a large market for AI customer-support firms. 

A round-the-clock requirement also raises costs for operators. AI agents are the obvious way to cover the hours when human staffing is optional. This links to MediaNama's earlier reporting on AI agents in telecom [link: earlier MediaNama story on AI agents in telecom].

Pahwa also said the mandate could spread beyond the telecom sector if other regulators copy the requirement of making customer service a mandatory requirement, especially by allowing AI agents for the support team. ,

"This could pave the way for other regulators to also put in a requirement for 24-hour customer support." – Nikhil Pahwa
