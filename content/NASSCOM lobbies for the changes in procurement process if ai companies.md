**# NASSCOM asks government to review public procurement processes for AI to counter Big Tech pricing

Access a copy of the parliamentary report here: [[pdf](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/INDIAS-ROLE-IN-GLOBAL-TECH-DIPLOMACY-DIGITAL.pdf)]

The industry body National Association of Software and Service Companies (NASSCOM) has urged the government to review its public procurement processes to give domestic AI foundation models a preferential treatment against global Big Tech giants like Google and Meta.

This was revealed in the 17th report of the Committee on External Affairs, titled “India’s Role in Global Tech Diplomacy, Digital Governance, Outer Space Diplomacy, AI and Emerging Innovations,” tabled in September 2026. NASSCOM is a trade association over 3,500 companies as members including the popular IT companies like Tata Consultancy Services (TCS), Cognizant, Accenture India and various Global Capability Centres (GCCs) and startups. The full list can be explored [here](https://nasscom.in/members-listing).

While the government has heavily invested in building indigenous AI capabilities, NASSCOM warned that a lack of a clear domestic adoption pathway could render these efforts futile if local startups are forced to compete purely on price with heavily subsidized global models. Some of the issues it had highlighted are:

The lowest bidder tender’ trap and the competitive advantage of big tech: During its oral evidence before the committee on April 30, 2026, NASSCOM highlighted a critical structural flaw in how government entities, including state governments, procure AI services that might be acting against the government-funded AI projects or companies like Sarvam:

“We do not have an AI inclusion roadmap right now... We are investing in foundational companies like Sarvam AI and BharatGen, who are building India’s AI models, but we are not talking about how they will get consumed by Indians. Government procurement for these models still has to follow the L1 [lowest bidder] tender process.” – [Parliamentary Report](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/INDIAS-ROLE-IN-GLOBAL-TECH-DIPLOMACY-DIGITAL.pdf)

NASSCOM brings in the China context to encourage domestic companies: NASSCOM’s submission drew a comparison with China’s approach, where their government apparently created a protected domestic market for its homegrown AI models, allowing them to grow and compete against global Big Tech competition.

“China gave real preference to its own companies for their models to be adopted; if we still compete purely on price with Meta and Google and others, who have spent hundreds of billions of dollars, we will never have a level playing field. We need to look at our procurement processes to ensure that what we are investing in gets some disproportionate preference.” -  [Parliamentary Report](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/INDIAS-ROLE-IN-GLOBAL-TECH-DIPLOMACY-DIGITAL.pdf)

The Mercator Institute for China Studies (MERICS), a European think tank's research paper [argued](https://merics.org/en/report/chinas-drive-toward-self-reliance-artificial-intelligence-chips-large-language-models) that the "protected home market" of China has helped the  large language model (LLM) developers like DeepSeek as well as the AI ecosystem, including the microprocessing chips.

The Committee’s Recommendations: While the parliamentary committee did not issue a direct directive to overhaul the General Financial Rules governing the L1 (lowest bidder) tender process, it strongly acknowledged the structural bottleneck highlighted by NASSCOM

“The Committee are concerned that India still lacks several of the basic foundations that would make it a genuine AI power…The Committee are of the view that the gap between India's demonstrated capability and its realised leadership is not primarily a gap of resources or talent, but of follow-through, adoption pathways, strategic clarity, and the pace at which these foundations are being built. The Government should ensure that the opportunity AI presents for India's development is not left to be realised by default, but is pursued as deliberately.”

What are the companies under the IndiaAI mission? In March 2024, the Indian government had approved the budget of Rs. 10,371.92 crore for the IndiaAI Mission. As MediaNama [reported](https://www.medianama.com/2024/03/223-indian-govt-indiaai-mission-rs-10000-crore/) then, this budget was supposed to be used for building “programs and partnerships across the public and private sectors.” These includes activities like: 

- Providing compute capacity
    
- Developing and deploying indigenous Large Multimodal Models (LMMs)
    
- streamlining access to quality non-personal datasets for AI innovation
    
- development of AI apps and AI based solutions
    
- Increasing accessibility to AI programs and courses
    
- Financing  deep-tech AI startups
    
- Implementing responsible AI projects for "trust and safety"
