# Rs. 300 Cr NBFC AI cyberattack: Why did the public hear it from media, not regulators?

  

Because of AI-powered cyberattacks, about Rs. 300 crore has been siphoned from mostly smaller non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) across India in roughly about two months time period that resulted in over 150 first information reports (FIRs) filed, [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/banking/finance/ai-powered-cyberattacks-siphon-300-crore-from-small-nbfcs-in-two-months/articleshow/135278215.cms?from=mdr) reported based on the “highly placed sources.” 

According to ET:

- The cyberattackers mostly hit smaller NBFCs with a turnover of about Rs. 1,000 crore or below. 
    
- In one reported case, a Delhi-based NBFC lost Rs. 7.5 crore in one attack and Rs. 7.25 crore a few days later.
    
- Cybersecurity agencies have reportedly traced some completed investigations to CyberStrike, a Chinese hacking platform. 
    
- Experts, as quoted in ET report, believe attackers are combining two Chinese AI toolkits, Artex and CyberStrike, with open-source models such as DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi and GLM.
    

 “Cyberattacks used to happen earlier also but for the past one and a half months, they have become rampant, with some companies witnessing repeated attacks.” – an anonymous source quoted in ET.

Why this matters: How should the public come to know about the incidents of cyber attacks, data or financial breaches? Definitely not through the news media. Disclosure should’ve ideally come from the regulators responsible for cybersecurity in India, like the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), CERT-In and CSIRT-Fin, the financial security arm of Cert-In. Neither CERT-In's public advisories on these attacks nor any RBI notices carries a loss figure that the ET had reported.

Because of the generic and vague public advisoris, this leaves customers, borrowers and other stakeholders to find out from the media reports, if they are reported, or not at all.

Moreover, the concerning aspect that raises doubts about the responsible behavioour of the financial instituions is that the official acknwoledgement comes only after the public disclosures via non-official routers. For instnace: 

- The Central Bank of India's Rs. 13.97 crore loss became public through a [Hindustan Times report on its police complaint](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-central-bank-of-india-rs-13-97-crore-fraud/).
    
- SBI disclosed its data exposure to the stock exchanges on October 21 only two days after a report that a threat actor had [claimed the data on a dark web](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-sbi-data-breach-3897-customers-dark-web/).
    
- CoinDCX's chief executive called the reported breach a false alarm on October 22, only to admit [a Rs. 152 crore loss](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-coindcx-cyberattack-rs-152-crore-loss/) three days later.
    

ET also reported that CERT-In has advised organisations to prioritise alerts linked to Artex and CyberStrike. However, till now, the public notices the CERT-IN has issued doesn’t refer to any company or entity particularly. 

What Cert-In has said about cyberattacks in recent times: 

On October 9, CERT-In and CSIRT-Fin, its finance-sector arm, issued a [“Critical” advisory](https://www.cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?VLCODE=CIAD-2026-0046&pageid=PUBVLNOTES02) on October 9 to NBFCs, digital lenders, fintechs, payment service providers and wallet operators. It describes attackers who steal payment gateway and partner-bank API credentials, then call disbursal APIs directly. This bypasses KYC checks and per-transaction limits.

However, the advisory names no affected entity or the particular threat actor or the specific modus operandi. It does not say how many incidents CERT-In has seen or how much money has been stolen, if any, as per Cert-In’s records and acknowledgements. 

On October 15, a second [advisory](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CERT-In-Advisory-Notes.pdf) on AI agents accessing Indian systems, is similarly technical and stated that AI agents have accessed the Indian information and communication technology (ICT) systems without authorisation. 

Without naming the tools particularly, the advisory stated that attackers with financial motivations ran cyber campaigns against Indian entities using publicly available, AI-native offensive security frameworks. 

On October 27, it [held a full-day drill](https://x.com/IndianCERT/status/2115815527318974974) for over 300 officials from more than 55 financial institutions, predominantly regional rural banks and cooperative banks. Its post does not mention NBFCs or any losses.

What are the reporting timelines in data breaches? 

- CERT-In’s 2022 directions [require](https://www.medianama.com/2022/04/223-summary-cert-in-cybersecurity-directions-2/) organisations to report incidents within six hours. 
    
- RBI’s July 2026 [directions](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13592&Mode=0) require NBFCs to report cyber incidents on its DAKSH platform within six hours. 
    
- SEBI mandates that market entities should report cyber incidents to SEBI and CERT-In within 6 hours of detection, followed by expanded disclosures and the listed companies also have exchange-reporting obligations, typically within 12 hours.
    
- DPDP Act mandates that the Data Protection Board of India be informed [within 72 hours](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-data-breach-reporting-timeline-of-dpdp-rules-2025-explained/) and the Data Principals should be notified “without delay.”
    

What are the disclosure obligations of data fiduciaries in case of a data breach? 

The Section 8(6) of the DPDP Act requires a data fiduciary to give both the Data Protection Board of India, which is yet to be fully established, and each affected individual notice of a personal data breach. Rule 7(1) of the DPDP Rules, 2025 says this must happen “without delay”, in plain language, and must tell the individual:

- a description of the breach, including its nature, extent and timing;
    
- the consequences likely to arise for her;
    
- the measures taken to mitigate risk;
    
- the safety steps she can take; and
    
- contact details of a person who can answer queries.
    

Later, Rule 7(2) requires the DPB to be told without delay, with a detailed report within 72 hours. That includes findings on who caused the breach and a report on the notices sent to individuals.This rule will [take effect only on May 13, 2027](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-data-breach-reporting-timeline-of-dpdp-rules-2025-explained/). And it is not clear whether these attacks exposed personal data, since CERT-In describes thefts of funds through payment APIs.

MediaNama has written to the RBI, SEBI, CERT-In and CSIRT-Fin, and will update this story with their responses.

To RBI:

- Whether it can confirm ET’s estimate of Rs. 300 crore in losses and nearly 150 complaints, and how many NBFCs have reported cyber incidents since August.
    
- Whether its July 31, 2026 cybersecurity directions, or any other directions with a six-hour reporting window, apply to NBFCs.
    
- Whether NBFCs are required to tell customers about cyber incidents that affect their funds or data, and if not, whether the RBI will issue such a direction pending the DPDP Rules.
    
- Whether it has taken or is considering supervisory action against any NBFC, and whether it will publish aggregate incident and loss data.
    

To SEBI:

- Whether any listed NBFC has disclosed these losses under Regulation 30 of the SEBI Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements Regulations, 2015.
    
- Whether SEBI considers cyber-fraud losses of this scale material for disclosure, and whether it will set timelines for disclosing cyber incidents.
    
- Whether it has asked listed financial entities to confirm that affected customers were informed.
    

To CERT-In and CSIRT-Fin:

- How many NBFCs and fintechs have reported incidents under the October 9 advisory or earlier, and whether it can confirm ET’s figures.
    
- When and how it alerted NBFCs about Artex and CyberStrike, as reported by ET, and whether alerts went beyond the published public advisories.
    
- Whether it will publish aggregate, non-attributed data on incidents, losses and remediation status.
    
- Whether it tracks if affected entities have informed their customers, and what it does after receiving an incident report.
