**Nandan Nilekani hints at instant AI generated exam to prevent paper leaks

At Bloomberg New Economy Forum 2026 in New Delhi, the Bloomberg's host John Fraher, asked Nandan Nilekani whether AI could "spontaneously create the exam paper on the day that it's being taken," Respondening to this, Nandan Nilekani, who has been asked by PM Narendra Modi to lead the India’s examination reforms, said “Yeah, Sure.” He then reasoned: 

“I think the issue that led to this committee and so on is that the question paper leak. This is all public information, as you know, the NEET exam paper leak. So I think the question then becomes, why do we need to have something called a question paper? Because the moment you have a question paper, then that creates an economic incentive for that to be sold or whatever." – Nandan Nilekani

Nilekani, co-founder of Infosys and the architect of India's Aadhaar and UPI digital infrastructure, was [asked by PM Modi in July](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-pm-modi-nandan-nilekani-task-force-exam-reforms/) to chair a high-power task force on examination reforms after hte controversy of  NEET paper leak that led to [widespread protests at Jantar Mantar](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-digital-developments-second-day-internet-shutdown-jantar-mantar/) and resignation of then Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan. "I was asked to do this report on July 28th, and I was given three months," he said at the forum. 

Nandan idetaed these approaches: 

1. AI generates the paper at only the last minute from a massive question bank: "The way to do that is to really have a massive bank of questions, and only at the last minute generate the question paper for that day. And therefore, then the question paper goes away, and there's no economic incentive for that.”
    
2. Computer-based testing at scale. "The other thing is, of course, how to make sure all exams are done on a computer-based testing. And that requires a network of locations. We have 150,000 locations where a young boy or girl can take a test. That needs to be expanded."
    

In India, the computer-based public examinations often rely on the private service providers to conduct these examinations. Among them, Infosys rival Tara Consultancy Services (TCS) is one of the largest player with widespread physical examination centers through their [TCS iON National Qualifier Test.](https://www.tcsion.com/hub/national-qualifier-test-institutions/) DEXIT Global, which was previously run as government-owned NSEIT, Sify technologies are among the competitors. 

IIT Kanpur recommends a dynamic question paper based on student performance at a Minsitry’s IIT Council: Nilekani is not the only one such tech-enabled solutions to the examinations. According to the [minutes of the 56th meeting of the Council of IITs](https://www.iitsystem.ac.in/sites/default/files/councilminutes/minutes/56/Minutes-of-the-56th-Meeting-of-the-Council-of-IITs.pdf), held on 25 August 2025 at IIT Delhi, Prof Manindra Agrawal, Director of IIT Kanpur, recommendted that for quantitative and reasoning skills, there could be a mechanism that “dynamically generates questions based on a student's performance.” He reasoned that this dynamic method could “reduce coaching dependency, enhance fairness, and allow flexible, secure testing environments.”

The IIT Kanpur later recommended to the Council to “create a robust tool to generate questions of varying difficulty levels dynamically, ensuring fairness and scalability” and prepare a white paper within six months. 

Zoho’s Sridhar Vembu also made a presentation at the Council regarding the education reforms in India broadly calling for the pedagogical changes. Explore more about the council [here](https://www.iitsystem.ac.in/sites/default/files/councilminutes/minutes/56/Minutes-of-the-56th-Meeting-of-the-Council-of-IITs.pdf).

Medianama take: Nilekani again reaffirmed that the ‘systemtic issues’ like education reforms are solvable issues with the technology. “If you look at it systematically and go to the root causes of why it's happening and use technology to solve it, they are solvable issues," he said. However, without addressing the non-technical real life problems like corruption and accountability, the tech solutions could go less/no good to India's existing education system, let alone a “reform.”

According to an Economic Times report based on Frost & Sullivan's 2025 candidate estimates, a 2025 Parliamentary Committee report, India's exam system is run by a fragmented private contractor network that is broken. Some of the issues with the current system are:

- No single entity to oversee the examination process: The report says that oftentimes, one firm may run centres, meanwhile another supplies computers and/or software, separate agencies deploy invigilators, security and technical support.   
    
- Supervision has shifted from teachers to private-agency hires, and undertrained invigilators remain the core vulnerability. As per the report, Abhay Jere, former AICTE vice-chairman and Rajat Moona, Director, IIT Gandhinagar, held this view.
    
- What about the insider leaks?: The reexamination of NEET 2026 leak at Bihar's Lakhisarai was an [inside job](https://www.newindianexpress.com/thesundaystandard/2026/Oct/11/neet-re-exam-scam-case-key-accused-held-from-bihar) by the alleged biometric staff, supervisors and operators were arrested alongside dummy candidates whose biometrics were defeated by human collusion, a classic case of tech manipulationby the humans. 
    
- No national vendor database or common accountability framework: Confisec, Eduquity, Satvat Infosol and Aptech, the exam service providers are reportedly blacklisted by some states, and still continue to win contracts elsewhere– [an allegation raised earlier too](https://www.newslaundry.com/amp/story/2024%2F07%2F08%2Fyears-before-neet-row-nta-contracted-2-exam-firms-with-murky-record). The Parliamentary Committee's call for a centralised blacklisted-firm register is still pending, noted the report.
    

The tech, sometimes powered by AI, can offer some legitimate solutions to real life problems, including examinations. Nonetheless, what is the point of the dream/vision created by the tech-enabled solutions, like AI-generated instant question papers, if the existing system and the public/employees mindset is not fixed/reformed? Just like any other system, tech is also breakable and malleable. Therefore, India's examination reforms require a holistic approach from the ground up. Otherwise, we could end up finding tech-solutions like banning Telegram to prevent the spread of NEET paper leak instead of probing and finding out the exact leak. 

Moreover, as the AI advancements keep increasing, there is a requirement for overhaul of the education system and the curriculum. Anyway, that could be a “tech solution” for another day.
