# From PSIR to Policy: A Complete Career-Contingency Report for a Two-Time-Prelims UPSC Aspirant (July 2026)

## TL;DR

- **Your PSIR-heavy UPSC preparation is a high-value asset in India's fast-growing public-policy economy, not a sunk cost** — the same syllabus that covers Political Theory, Indian Government, Comparative Politics and International Relations maps almost one-to-one onto entry roles at think tanks (PRS/LAMP, ORF, CPR-successors, Takshashila, Vidhi), CM fellowships, political consultancies (I-PAC), regulators (RBI Grade B, SEBI Grade A) and academia (UGC-NET/JRF).
- **The single best "insurance" master's for your profile is a Master of Public Policy (MPP) or MA Political Science/IR from a brand-strong Indian institution** — prioritise, in order, (1) a funded/low-cost, high-brand route (JNU MA, TISS, Azim Premji University with need-based aid), then (2) a premium policy-school route (NLSIU MPP, Jindal JSGP, Kautilya, ISPP) if you can fund it — because in India's policy hiring market brand and network matter more than the MPP-vs-MA label.
- **Run a two-track plan: give a maximum of 1–2 more disciplined UPSC attempts while simultaneously converting to a parallel career** by registering now for UGC-NET (December 2026 cycle), RBI Grade B and SEBI Grade A, applying to LAMP (deadline ~21 December) and CM fellowships, and building a public-writing/RTI/data portfolio — the highest-probability domains for the next five years are technology/AI governance, data-protection (DPDP), climate/energy, and urban and health policy.

## Key Findings

1. **The transferable-skills case is strong and market-recognised.** Rebounce, founded in October 2023 by ex-aspirants Jigyasu and Akshat Agarwal, has already helped over 300 former UPSC aspirants — in co-founder Jigyasu Agarwal's words to The Better India, "many of whom are now working in public policy, impact consulting, NGOs and other corporates" — explicitly reframing "a 4–5 year gap" as resilience, holistic thinking and policy fluency.
2. **PSIR is the single most policy-adjacent optional.** Its Paper I (Political Theory, Indian Government & Politics) and Paper II (Comparative Politics, International Relations, India & the World) overlap directly with GS-II (governance, constitution, IR) and are the exact conceptual vocabulary used by think tanks, legislative-research bodies and IR institutes (ICWA, MP-IDSA).
3. **Salaries in the policy ecosystem are modest at entry but scale steeply.** Entry policy analyst/associate roles pay roughly ₹6–9 LPA (Vidhi ~₹9 LPA for freshers per practitioner accounts; general think-tank associates ~₹40k–60k/month), rising to ₹15–25 LPA mid-career and ₹40–80L+ at director level; regulator jobs (RBI Grade B) start at a gross of ₹1,54,936/month (without HRA).
4. **Regulator and allied exams are a natural hedge** because they reuse your economy/polity/current-affairs base: RBI Grade B (basic pay ₹78,450; 60 vacancies notified 29 April 2026), SEBI Grade A, NABARD Grade A, and UPSC EPFO APFC (80 vacancies expected 2026 after EO/AO withdrawal).
5. **Real precedents exist of people who never cleared UPSC yet built meaningful, preparation-linked careers** — including India's most famous UPSC educator Avadh Ojha (failed Mains 2005), journalist Ravish Kumar, and numerous policy-sector professionals.
6. **The five-year demand curve favours tech/AI governance, data protection, climate and urban/health policy** — per the Competition Commission of India market study cited by PIB (12 February 2026), India's AI market is expected to grow to USD 131.31 billion by 2032 at a 42.2% CAGR (from USD 7.63 billion in 2024), and per the NASSCOM–Deloitte report "Advancing India's AI Skills" (August 2024) AI talent is set to grow from 600,000–650,000 professionals to more than 1.25 million by 2027 (15% CAGR); the government has stood up the IndiaAI Mission, an AI governance framework and an AI Economic Council.

## Details

### SECTION 1 — The Complete Current UPSC Civil Services Examination Syllabus

The UPSC Civil Services Examination (CSE) has three stages: Preliminary (screening), Main (written), and Personality Test (Interview). Prelims 2026 was held on 24 May 2026; Mains 2026 begins 21 August 2026; Prelims 2027 is scheduled for 23 May 2027.

**STAGE 1 — PRELIMINARY EXAMINATION (objective, screening only; marks not counted in final merit).** Two papers, each 2 hours, with negative marking of one-third per wrong answer.

_GS Paper I (200 marks, 100 questions, merit-deciding for the Prelims cutoff):_

- Current events of national and international importance.
- History of India and the Indian National Movement.
- Indian and World Geography — physical, social, economic geography of India and the World.
- Indian Polity and Governance — Constitution, Political System, Panchayati Raj, Public Policy, Rights Issues, etc.
- Economic and Social Development — Sustainable Development, Poverty, Inclusion, Demographics, Social Sector initiatives.
- General issues on Environmental Ecology, Biodiversity and Climate Change (no specialisation required).
- General Science.

_GS Paper II — CSAT (200 marks, 80 questions, qualifying only; must score at least 33% / 66 marks):_

- Comprehension; Interpersonal skills including communication skills; Logical reasoning and analytical ability; Decision-making and problem-solving; General mental ability; Basic numeracy (Class X level); Data interpretation (charts, graphs, tables, data sufficiency — Class X level).

**STAGE 2 — MAIN EXAMINATION (nine papers; two qualifying, seven counted for merit; total merit marks 1750).**

_Paper A — Indian Language (300 marks, qualifying; choose a language from the Eighth Schedule; minimum ~25%)._ _Paper B — English (300 marks, qualifying; minimum ~25%)._ Both test the ability to read and understand serious discursive prose and to express ideas clearly.

_Essay (Paper I, 250 marks):_ Two essays on topics spanning society, governance, economy, philosophy, environment; marks for coherent, orderly argument.

_GS Paper I (250 marks):_ Indian Heritage and Culture; History and Geography of the World and Society (Indian culture; modern Indian history and the freedom struggle; post-independence consolidation; world history; Indian society, diversity, role of women, population, urbanisation, globalisation, social empowerment, communalism, regionalism, secularism; physical geography, resource distribution, factors for industrial location, geophysical phenomena).

_GS Paper II (250 marks) — the paper with maximum PSIR overlap:_ Indian Constitution (evolution, features, amendments, basic structure); functions and responsibilities of the Union and States, federalism, devolution; separation of powers, dispute-redressal; comparison of the Indian constitutional scheme with other countries; Parliament and State legislatures; executive and judiciary structure, organisation and functioning; representation of people; statutory, regulatory and quasi-judicial bodies; governance, transparency, accountability, e-governance, citizens' charters; welfare schemes and social sector (health, education, human resources); poverty and hunger; role of civil services in a democracy; **International Relations** — India and its neighbourhood; bilateral, regional and global groupings involving India; effect of policies of developed and developing countries on India's interests; the Indian diaspora; important international institutions, agencies and their mandate.

_GS Paper III (250 marks):_ Indian economy, planning, resource mobilisation, growth, development and employment; inclusive growth; budgeting; agriculture, MSP, PDS, food security, farm subsidies; food processing; land reforms; liberalisation effects; infrastructure; investment models; science and technology, IT, space, computers, robotics, nano-tech, biotech, IPR; environment, conservation, pollution, EIA; disaster management; security — extremism, internal security, cyber security, money laundering, border management, organised crime.

_GS Paper IV — Ethics, Integrity and Aptitude (250 marks):_ Ethics and human interface; attitude; aptitude and foundational values for civil service; emotional intelligence; contributions of moral thinkers/philosophers from India and the world; public/civil service values and ethics in public administration; probity in governance; case studies.

_Optional Subject — Papers VI and VII (250 marks each; 500 total):_ Candidates choose one optional from UPSC's list of 48 optional subjects.

**PSIR (Political Science and International Relations) Optional — full syllabus (the user's optional):**

_Paper I — Part A (Political Theory and Political Thought):_ Political theory (meaning and approaches); Theories of the state (Liberal, Neo-liberal, Marxist, Pluralist, Post-colonial, Feminist); Justice (Rawls and communitarian critiques); Equality (social, political, economic; equality–freedom relationship; affirmative action); Rights (meaning, theories, kinds; human rights); Democracy (classical and contemporary theories; representative, participatory, deliberative models); Concept of power (hegemony, ideology, legitimacy); Political ideologies (Liberalism, Socialism, Marxism, Fascism, Gandhism, Feminism); Indian Political Thought (Dharamshastra, Arthashastra, Buddhist traditions; Sir Syed Ahmed Khan, Sri Aurobindo, M.K. Gandhi, B.R. Ambedkar, M.N. Roy); Western Political Thought (Plato, Aristotle, Machiavelli, Hobbes, Locke, John S. Mill, Marx, Gramsci, Hannah Arendt).

_Paper I — Part B (Indian Government and Politics):_ Indian Nationalism — political strategies of the freedom struggle (constitutionalism to mass Satyagraha, Non-cooperation, Civil Disobedience, militant/revolutionary, peasant/workers' movements); perspectives on the national movement (Liberal, Socialist, Marxist, Radical Humanist, Dalit); making of the Constitution; salient features (Preamble, Fundamental Rights and Duties, DPSP, Parliamentary system, amendment procedures, judicial review and basic structure); principal organs of the Union and State governments; grassroots democracy (Panchayati Raj and municipal government; 73rd/74th amendments); statutory institutions/commissions (EC, CAG, Finance Commission, UPSC, NCSC, NCST, NCW, NHRC, Minorities Commission, Backward Classes Commission); federalism; planning and economic development (Nehruvian and Gandhian perspectives; role of planning and public sector; Green Revolution, land reforms, liberalisation); caste, religion and ethnicity; party system; social movements (civil liberties, women's, environmental).

_Paper II — Part A (Comparative Politics and International Relations):_ Comparative politics (nature, approaches, political economy and political sociology perspectives, limitations); the State in comparative perspective; politics of representation and participation; globalisation (developed/developing responses); approaches to the study of IR (Idealist, Realist, Marxist, Functionalist, Systems theory); key concepts (national interest, security, balance of power, deterrence, transnational actors, collective security, world capitalist economy, globalisation); changing international political order (superpowers, arms race, Cold War, nuclear threat; non-alignment; end of the Cold War and unipolarity); evolution of the international economic system (Bretton Woods to WTO; NIEO; North–South dimensions); UN and its agencies; regionalisation of world politics (EU, ASEAN, APEC, SAARC, NAFTA); contemporary global concerns (democracy, human rights, environment, gender justice, terrorism, nuclear proliferation).

_Paper II — Part B (India and the World):_ Indian foreign policy (determinants, institutions of policymaking, continuity and change); India's contribution to the Non-Aligned Movement; India and South Asia (regional cooperation, SAARC, "Look East"/"Act East", obstacles, the nuclear question, river-water disputes); India and the Global South; India and the great powers; India and the UN system; India and the nuclear question; recent developments in Indian foreign policy.

**STAGE 3 — Personality Test / Interview (275 marks, merit-counted):** A board assesses mental and social traits — clarity of expression, balance of judgement, intellectual and moral integrity, leadership, and interest in current national/international affairs. Total for the final merit list = 1750 (Mains) + 275 (Interview) = 2025 marks.

_Why this matters for your pivot:_ Note how much of what you have already mastered — the entire Constitution/federalism/IR block of GS-II, the economy of GS-III, the ethics/probity frameworks of GS-IV, and the whole of PSIR — is the working knowledge base of policy analysts, legislative researchers, regulators and professors. That is the foundation of everything below.

### SECTION 2 — Five Strategies If UPSC Does Not Work Out

**Strategy 1 — Convert your knowledge base into a credential-bearing qualification (do a targeted master's or clear UGC-NET) rather than letting it decay.** _Rationale:_ The market does not automatically recognise "3–5 years of UPSC prep"; Rebounce's founders found recruiters see only "a gap" unless the skills are packaged. A master's or NET converts tacit knowledge into a formal signal. _Evidence:_ Prakhyat Mathur, a multi-attempt non-clearer, went to the Indian School of Public Policy and now works as a Research & Communication Associate in a Rajya Sabha MP's office, explicitly saying he "leverages UPSC lessons daily" for policy memos.

**Strategy 2 — Redeploy immediately into an adjacent exam that reuses the same syllabus, so preparation compounds instead of restarting.** _Rationale:_ RBI Grade B (ESI + Finance + English), SEBI Grade A, NABARD Grade A, State PSC and EPFO APFC all overlap heavily with GS economy/polity/current affairs. _Evidence:_ Akand Sitra, who repeatedly reached the CSE interview but never cleared, used the same preparation to clear the Intelligence Bureau (ACIO) and later did an MBA at ISB; Ankit Avasthi, who did not clear UPSC CSE, cleared the Rajasthan Administrative Service on his first attempt.

**Strategy 3 — Build a public policy-writing and research portfolio while still deciding, because the policy sector hires on demonstrated writing, not just degrees.** _Rationale:_ Think tanks weight publications and analytical writing heavily; op-eds, blogs and briefs create a visible track record. _Evidence:_ Ravish Kumar, who pursued history and public affairs at Delhi University and prepared for public life through that route rather than clearing the CSE, built a career on the analytical/current-affairs muscle such preparation develops, and won the 2019 Ramon Magsaysay Award "for harnessing journalism to give voice to the voiceless."

**Strategy 4 — Monetise your subject mastery through the education/content economy.** _Rationale:_ India's UPSC-prep and competitive-exam coaching market is large and rewards subject depth and communication — precisely what years of PSIR/GS study build. _Evidence:_ Avadh Ojha, who failed the UPSC Mains in 2005, became one of India's most-followed UPSC educators (Chanakya IAS Academy, Vajiram & Ravi, Unacademy, then founder of IQRA IAS Academy, Pune); multiple Unacademy PSIR/GS educators (e.g., Arvindsingh Rajpurohit, who scored 296/500 in the PSIR optional; Gajendra Singh Godara, an IIT Bombay four-attempt aspirant now building study material at PadhAI) never became officers but teach the subject professionally.

**Strategy 5 — Set a hard "exit rule" and diversify psychologically, treating a Plan B as a strength, not a betrayal.** _Rationale:_ Aspirants who lack a Plan B suffer the worst outcomes; a pre-committed attempt-cap protects mental health and finances. _Evidence:_ Jigyasu Agarwal decided in advance to give no more than three attempts and pivoted cleanly to found Rebounce; Rushikesh Kulkarni's roughly decade-long, ultimately unsuccessful attempt cycle illustrates the cost of not setting a limit — he eventually succeeded in corporate HR (after a TISS master's) but describes the years of drift.

### SECTION 3 — Alternate Career Paths from UPSC/PSIR Preparation (Indian Context)

**A. Public policy careers (think tanks, legislative research, fellowships, consulting).**

- _Think tanks_ — PRS Legislative Research, Observer Research Foundation (ORF), Takshashila Institution, Vidhi Centre for Legal Policy, Centre for Social and Economic Progress (CSEP, successor ecosystem to the former Brookings India), Carnegie India, Accountability Initiative, IDFC Institute, NCAER, and successors to the Centre for Policy Research (CPR). _Entry:_ a relevant bachelor's/master's, strong writing, and (increasingly) data skills; Vidhi typically wants ~2 years' experience and publications for research roles. _Salary:_ research associate/analyst entry ~₹6–9 LPA (Vidhi ~₹9 LPA / ~₹75–80k/month for freshers per practitioner reports; general think-tank associates commonly ~₹40k–60k/month); mid-level consultant/senior analyst ~₹15–25 LPA; policy director/partner ~₹40–80L+. _PSIR transfer:_ direct — theories of the state, federalism, IR frameworks and Indian government are the daily working vocabulary.
- _LAMP Fellowship (PRS):_ a full-time 10–11 month fellowship placing you with a Member of Parliament; stipend ₹23,000/month; eligibility is a bachelor's degree in any discipline and age 25 or under (born on/after 1 June 1998 for the 2026–27 cycle); application deadline ~21 December each year, with a mandatory online evaluation test (~4 January) and interviews; roughly 75 fellows are selected annually. Alumni go on to Harvard, Yale, Oxford, LSE, Sciences Po, IIMs, ISB, JNU, and into political consulting.
- _CM fellowships:_ Haryana's Chief Minister's Good Governance Associates (CMGGA, with Ashoka University) — one-year, ₹50,000/month (incl. ₹5,000 office expenses) plus travel and accommodation, graduate + 1 year experience or a PG degree, age ≤28; Gujarat's Sardar Patel Good Governance CM Fellowship — ₹1,00,000/month + ₹10,000 LTA, bachelor's (60%) + 3 years' experience, applications ~Nov–15 December; Chhattisgarh's CMGGF (with IIM Raipur, fully sponsored MBA-PPG). _PSIR transfer:_ governance, service delivery, centre-state relations.
- _Government consulting / advisory:_ KPMG, EY, Deloitte, PwC government & public-sector practices, plus MicroSave and development consultancies; NITI Aayog Young Professionals/consultants. _Salary:_ Big-Four government advisory analysts ~₹6–12 LPA at entry, scaling with promotions. _PSIR transfer:_ policy analysis, stakeholder mapping, scheme evaluation.

**B. Other government/regulator exams.**

- _RBI Grade B:_ basic pay ₹78,450/month in the scale 78450-4050(9)-114900-EB-4050(2)-123000-4650(4)-141600 (16 years); initial monthly gross emoluments (without HRA) of ₹1,54,936; in-hand ~₹1.2–1.6 lakh; 60 vacancies (General/DEPR/DSIM) notified 29 April 2026; PG (55%) or graduate with 60% eligibility for the general stream; three stages (Phase 1 objective, Phase 2 with Economic & Social Issues + Finance & Management + English descriptive, Interview). _PSIR transfer:_ ESI and current affairs overlap; the descriptive papers reward the essay/answer-writing skills PSIR builds.
- _SEBI Grade A, NABARD Grade A:_ comparable pay scale and syllabus families; strong overlap with economy/finance current affairs.
- _State PSCs (e.g., UPPSC PCS, BPSC, MPSC, RPSC):_ PSIR is an available optional in most, and the GS overlap is near-total; a PSIR-prepared aspirant is well-placed for deputy collector/DSP-level state services.
- _UPSC EPFO APFC:_ ~80 APFC vacancies expected in 2026 (basic pay Level-10, ₹56,100), graduate eligibility. Note: the 311 EO/AO vacancies were withdrawn per a June 2026 RTI.
- _UGC-NET/JRF (Political Science):_ the gateway to Assistant Professor eligibility and JRF; master's with 55% (50% reserved); JRF stipend ₹37,000 (then ₹42,000 SRF); Assistant Professor Level-10 basic ₹57,700, gross ~₹90,000–1.1 lakh in central universities. _PSIR transfer:_ the NET Political Science syllabus is essentially the PSIR optional plus research methodology — arguably the single highest-overlap exam available to this user.

**C. Journalism and policy writing.** Political/policy reporting, editorial and explainer journalism (The Hindu, Indian Express, ThePrint, Scroll, The Diplomat for IR). _Entry:_ portfolio + often a journalism/mass-comm degree, though many enter on writing merit. _PSIR transfer:_ IR, governance and political-theory literacy are exactly what political desks need. _Evidence:_ Ravish Kumar's trajectory.

**D. Law.** A 3-year LLB (after graduation) or a CLAT-PG/LLM route opens policy-law, constitutional litigation, and legal-research careers (e.g., Vidhi, 39A, NLU centres). _PSIR transfer:_ constitutional law, rights, basic-structure doctrine already studied in Paper I(B).

**E. Teaching/coaching and content creation.** UPSC/State-PSC/NET educators, YouTube and Unacademy content, authoring guidebooks. _Evidence:_ Avadh Ojha, Ankit Avasthi, Arvindsingh Rajpurohit.

**F. Development sector (NGOs, CSR, foundations).** Azim Premji Foundation, Tata Trusts, PRADAN, Teach For India, SBI Youth for India. _Salary:_ entry ~₹4–7 LPA, program managers ~₹8–15 LPA. _PSIR transfer:_ social justice, welfare-scheme design, grassroots democracy. _Evidence:_ Gargi Gupta, who missed Prelims narrowly, secured Teach For India and SBI Youth for India fellowships.

**G. International relations careers in India.** Indian Council of World Affairs (ICWA) Research Fellows (first-class master's in IR/political science with 60%+, PhD preferred; two-year contract); Manohar Parrikar Institute for Defence Studies and Analyses (MP-IDSA) Research Analyst/Associate Fellow/Research Fellow (master's/M.Phil/PhD in IR/strategic affairs, with research experience); MEA-adjacent research and area-studies roles. _PSIR transfer:_ Paper II is literally IR theory + India's foreign policy.

**H. Political consulting (I-PAC-style firms).** I-PAC and its many spin-offs (e.g., Sunil Kanugolu's Mindshare) hire researchers, field coordinators and data analysts. _Salary:_ political consultants broadly ₹3.2–15 LPA, mean ~₹8.5–9 LPA; some I-PAC roles reportedly ~₹1 lakh/month. _Caveat:_ documented downsides per employee reviews — poor work-life balance (Saturday/Sunday working), project-based insecurity, and ethically uncomfortable partisan work. _PSIR transfer:_ electoral behaviour, party systems, pressure groups (Paper I(B)).

**I. Corporate public affairs / government relations / ESG.** Most MNCs (Amazon, Google, Uber, etc.) run public-policy/government-affairs teams managing regulatory risk; ESG and sustainability roles are expanding. _Salary:_ public-affairs managers ~₹12–30 LPA mid-career. _PSIR transfer:_ governance, regulatory understanding, stakeholder analysis.

### SECTION 4 — Which Master's in India Is Ideal

**The recommendation: pursue a Master of Public Policy (MPP) or an MA in Political Science/IR, choosing the specific programme by brand + network + affordability rather than by degree label.** Here is the comparative reasoning.

_Premium policy schools (high fee, strong brand/network, variable placements):_

- **NLSIU Bengaluru MPP** — 2-year, ~50 seats, total fee ~₹10.5 lakh (recently raised from ~₹5.7 lakh in 2021); law-infused curriculum; strong academic rigour and NLSIU brand. _Caveat:_ candid student accounts on Legally India describe weak dedicated placement support (no formal placement cell; a student-run Recruitment Coordination Committee), so the degree works best for self-starters who network independently. Entry-level MPP outcomes ~₹5–10 LPA.
- **Jindal School of Government and Public Policy (JSGP, O.P. Jindal Global University)** — 1-year MA Public Policy, tuition ~₹4 lakh; strong global-university branding and QS-ranked parent; broad recruiter base.
- **Kautilya School of Public Policy (Hyderabad)** — 2-year, 103-credit MPP with specialisations, internship + capstone; newer but aggressively industry-linked.
- **Indian School of Public Policy (ISPP, Delhi)** — 1-year Post Graduate Programme in Policy, Design & Management; practitioner-heavy; growing alumni in MP offices and think tanks (e.g., Prakhyat Mathur).
- **IIM public-policy programmes** (IIM Bangalore PGPPM, IIM Raipur MBA-PPG/CMGGF) and **IIT MPPs** (IIT Delhi School of Public Policy, IIT Madras/Bombay) — strong analytics and brand; IIM/IIT signalling is very high in the Indian market.
- **Ashoka University** — strong liberal-arts/political-science brand and research orientation.

_High-brand, low-cost / funded options (best ROI for this profile):_

- **JNU** — MA in Politics (with specialisation in International Studies)/Political Science; nominal fees; unmatched brand in political science, IR and the policy/academia pipeline; the natural home for a PSIR-prepared candidate and the strongest feeder to UGC-NET/JRF and PhD.
- **TISS (Mumbai)** — MA in Public Policy/Development Studies/Social Work; strong field exposure and development-sector placements, generally better placement support than NLSIU per student comparisons.
- **Azim Premji University (Bengaluru)** — MA Public Policy & Governance / MA Development; substantial need-based scholarships (family income <₹8 lakh eligible for various levels; PG fees modest with aid); explicitly development-focused.
- **Jamia Millia Islamia, Delhi University (Dept. of Political Science), South Asian University** — affordable MA Political Science/IR with solid brand, feeding academia and NET/JRF.

_Comparison logic for a PSIR-prepared, two-time-prelims aspirant unwilling to study abroad:_

- **Admission fit:** Your PSIR base gives you a genuine edge in entrance tests/interviews for JNU, TISS, Jamia, DU, Ashoka and the policy schools — the reading you have already done (Rawls, Gramsci, realism/liberalism, Indian constitutional design) is exactly what these interviews probe.
- **ROI:** JNU/TISS/APU dominate on ROI (low/subsidised fee, strong brand). Premium schools (NLSIU ~₹10.5L, Jindal ~₹4L) carry higher fee risk against modest entry salaries (₹5–10 LPA), so they make sense mainly if funded or if you value the specific alumni network.
- **Brand value in the Indian policy job market:** IIM/IIT/JNU/TISS/Ashoka/NLSIU all carry strong signal; for think tanks and MP offices, JNU (for IR/PSc) and the policy schools (for MPP) are most recognised.
- **Final recommendation:** **First choice — an MA in Political Science/International Relations at JNU (or TISS/APU MA Public Policy/Development if you prefer applied policy and want scholarship support), because it is affordable, brand-strong, PSIR-aligned, and simultaneously sets up UGC-NET/JRF and the academic route.** **Second choice, if funded — an MPP at a premium school (Jindal or an IIM/IIT programme, or NLSIU if you are a confident self-networker)** for a faster, more corporate/consulting-oriented policy launch. Avoid taking on large debt for programmes with thin placement support.
