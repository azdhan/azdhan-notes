**# Bengaluru court issues gag order for Yash's Toxic against ‘derogatory content’

  

A Bengaluru court passed  an ex parte (without listening to the other party) temporary injunction against the publication of so-called ‘false, malicious, defamatory, or derogatory content’ related to Kannada actor Yash's upcoming film "Toxic: A Fairy Tale for Grown-Ups." We couldn’t locate the court order on [Bengaluru e-courts webstie](https://bengaluru.dcourts.gov.in). The News Minute reported this indicident first, read the original reporting [here](https://www.thenewsminute.com/karnataka/yashs-toxic-gets-gag-order-against-false-defamatory-content-before-aug-26-release).  

  

Movie producvers, KVN Productions, approached the court just days before its worldwide theatrical release on August 26, 2026. This temporary injunction will remain in force for nearly a month, until the next hearing on September 28. 

  

X and an unidentified person listed as "John Doe / Ashok Kumar" are parties to the case. Through this gag order, they are prohibited from circulating "false, malicious, defamatory or derogatory" material about the film through any medium before or after its release. 

  

Apart from this, the producers also sought a permanent injunction against the defendants. However, the court order does not expressly restrict reviews or criticism of the film.  

  

Under the terms of the order, as reported by The News Minute, the film’s producers should share the copies of the complaint, documents, and temporary application with the defendants (like X), as required by Order 39 Rule 3A of the Civil Procedure Code. This rule says courts should try to decide temporary injunction requests within 30 days or explain why it takes longer. The producers must also help resolve the temporary application within 30 days of any defendant responding. If the producers don’t follow these rules, the court’s injunction will be automatically canceled.

  

Why it Mattters:  What exactly counts as "false, malicious, defamatory, or derogatory" content?  Off late, there has been a growing effort to stifle free expression, particularly in the Telugu film industry.

This new trend began during the Sankranti/Pongal festival season in January. For the [first time](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-bookmyshow-disables-reviews-telugu-film-court-order/), film producers secured court orders to prevent BookMyShow and IMDb from allowing user ratings. Medianama later revealed that these orders originated from a Bangalore court.

In an email to Medianama, YouTuber Sushanth, who runs the channel Barbell Pitch Meetings, identified AI Plex as the company behind serving and coordinating these gag orders given by the courts. All the films released during Sankranti and Pongal had their ratings blocked on BookMyShow; not by courts in Andhra Pradesh or Telangana, where Telugu films are primarily watched, but by a court in Bangalore.

The censorship did not stop during the Sankranti/Pongal festival season. Vishwanath and Sons, released on August 14, also had its reviews and ratings [disabled](https://in.bookmyshow.com/movies/mysuru-mysore/vishwanath-and-sons/ET00489815) by a court order just two weeks ago. More gag orders continue to appear, suggesting this is far from an isolated incident.

In June 2026, Medianama [reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-telugu-producers-misusing-court-orders-curb-critical-online-reviews/) that Telugu YouTuber Sushanth had over ten active legal cases against him at the time. He posted a video about how Telugu film producers were targeting online movie reviews after the release of Peddi using the Copyright Law.

“How many cases is he planning to file? Public talk is getting dragged into this too — people on Twitter are saying far worse, far more vulgar things. Are you going to file cases against all of them? What does this even mean? The moment you release a film, should every review just be praised? Should critical reviews not exist at all? I’ll say it plainly: filing four or five cases might be able to silence me, but it cannot silence public opinion. It cannot shut down the public domain — remember that.” – Telugu YouTuber Sushanth (Barbell Pitch Meetings)

Since then, copyright law has been also weaponized to take down social media content, including clips recorded in theaters or user-generated coontent about films.

Sushanth is not the only one affected. Another YouTuber, Ravan, faced takedowns as well. Even two popular X accounts were temporarily restricted under mysterious circumstances. This is not just about movie reviews, either. For instance, smartphone manufacturer [AI Plus sent legal notices to YouTubers for negative reviews](https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-delhi-hc-techwiser-techbar-barred-ai-plus-smartphones/) of their products. [Motorola](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-motorola-youtube-video-ban-india-against-creators-platforms/) too did the same.

The broader issue is that Indian courts are enabling this censorship rather than defending free speech. How does so-called derogatory remarks give the additional rights to violate someone’s fundamental right of free speech? They may infringe on dignitym of the person, or they may occur financial loss, but they also restrict someone else’s right to free expression. If this is all about the negative impact of online free express (like memes), then there are various other factors too that impacts the businesses in various other ways, why try to regulate only speech?

Dignity or respect is not a black-and-white issue, it always exists on a spectrum. Legal interpretations may vary, but that nuance must be resolved in favor of free speech, not against it.
