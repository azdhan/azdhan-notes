 **# MEA admits India lacks a single, consolidated policy document for tech diplomacy, reveals Parliamentary report

Access a copy of the parliamentary report here: [[pdf](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/INDIAS-ROLE-IN-GLOBAL-TECH-DIPLOMACY-DIGITAL.pdf)]

The Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) has admitted to a parliamentary committee that India does not have a single, stand-alone policy document that consolidates India’s strategy on:

- digital governance, 
    
- artificial intelligence (AI), 
    
- cyber issues, and 
    
- space diplomacy. 
    

This was revealed torough the 17th report of the Committee on External Affairs, titled “India’s Role in Global Tech Diplomacy, Digital Governance, Outer Space Diplomacy, AI and Emerging Innovations,” tabled in September 2026. Member of Parliament Shashi Tharoor had chaired the Committee. 

Instead, the Ministry stated that India’s strategic direction in these critical domains is currently spread wide and “reflected across sectoral missions, inter-ministerial coordination mechanisms and India's positions in key bilateral, plurilateral and multilateral engagements.”

###  “In reply to a direct query on whether India has a clearly articulated strategy linking digital governance, AI, cyber issues and space diplomacy within its foreign policy, the Ministry submitted that ‘there is no single, stand-alone policy document that consolidates all these strands’, and that strategic direction is instead ‘reflected across sectoral missions, inter-ministerial coordination mechanisms and India's positions in key bilateral, plurilateral and multilateral engagements’.” – Parliamentary Report

‘Fragmentary, piecemeal and ad hoc’: The parliamentary committee strongly criticised that isolated individual approaches can’t make up for the lack of national strategy. 

“The Committee are of the considered view that this approach is fragmentary, piecemeal and ad hoc, and falls short of what an arena as significant and fast-evolving as technology diplomacy demands. A collection of well-conceived individual initiatives, however successful in isolation, cannot substitute for an articulated national vision, nor can tactical adaptability substitute for strategy.” 

It indicates that rather than operating under a centralized, whole-of-government tech diplomacy guidence/rules, various ministries are essentially running parallel, uncoordinated tracks. 

The Committee’s Recommendations: The parliamentary panel has specified these recommendations for the MEA and government:

1. A technology vision and strategy document: The Committee recommended that the MEA, in consultation with MeitY and other concerned ministries, must “initiate consultations on a Technology Vision and Strategy Document that brings together India's objectives, priorities, sequencing and institutional responsibilities across digital governance, artificial intelligence, cyber issues, space and emerging technologies within a coherent framework.”  
      
    
2. Formalise Inter-Ministerial Coordination: The Committee recommended the establishment of a “formally constituted Inter-Ministerial Coordination Mechanism on Technology Diplomacy, chaired by MEA,” with defined participation from MeitY, the Department of Space, and the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, meeting at a specified periodicity.  
      
    
3. Public scrutiny and a periodic review of the policy: The Committee further recommended that both the Technology Vision Document and the complementary Technology Diplomacy Strategy “should also be placed in the public domain and reviewed periodically to keep pace with technological change.”
