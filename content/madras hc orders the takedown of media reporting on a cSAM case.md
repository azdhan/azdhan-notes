# Madras HC orders media takedown of content in Chennai child sexual abuse case

The Madras High Court on September 25 restrained media outlets, social media intermediaries and digital platforms from publishing or circulating "any video" connected to a child sexual abuse material (CSAM) case that is currently being investigated by the Central Crime Branch in Chennai. It also directed those who had already uploaded material on the case to remove it by September 26. These restrictions apply to unknown persons (“John Doe / Ashok Kumar”) too. 

The Ministry of Information and Broadcasting has since forwarded the order to the following news channels: 

- India Today (Living Media India Limited), 
    
- Republic TV (Republic Media Network) and 
    
- Sathiyam TV (Sathyam Media Vision Private Limited). 
    

The MIB’s letter is also addressed to all newspapers, private satellite TV channels, publishers of news and current affairs content on digital media, their self-regulatory bodies, and social media intermediaries. It says the order is enclosed "for immediate compliance". Without naming each Youtube channel, the court noted that atleast 28 YouTube channels were allegedly involved in spreading or discussing the CSAM-related content in this case. 

"The media outlets, entities, digital platforms, etc., who have already uploaded any material… are directed to remove them forthwith, failing which the police shall vigorously apply the law and ensure their removal. This exercise should be completed by 26.09.2026 and a compliance report shall be filed on the next date of hearing [September 30, 2026]." – Justice V. Lakshminarayanan, Madras HC

Justice V. Lakshminarayanan also said that the Special Investigation Team formed by the Director General of Police should publish a press release once every week to let public know “what the Police are doing with respect to this serious crime"

The press has right to report, but media trail aspects should be probed: When Republic TV's argued that it had a right to report and complied with the POCSO Act, the Madras HC agreed that "the Press has the right to report". But, it also said, "whether this right to report is unrestricted and absolute or would include the right to media trial would have to be probed into."

The court said it "cherishes the freedom of speech and expression" and the freedom of the press to disseminate information. It also referred to media coverage of the security operation during the Bombay terrorist attack, and said terrorists learned of the strategies of law enforcement agencies through constant press coverage. 

The court further said that the news channels and other entities are “at liberty to have a discussion on the issues that arise out of child abuse, as long as it does not touch upon the factual matrix in” the concerned CSAM case. 

"Every right has a corresponding duty and this duty is not only to be owed to the children, but also to the accused involved in the case," it said.

Background of the case: Tulir Charitable Trust, an organisation that works on the prevention and healing of child sexual abuse filed the petition with HC. The petition stated that the Central Crime Branch registered the crime under the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act. According to the order, the police first filed a closure report in February 2026 citing inadequate evidence. The POCSO court refused to accept it and ordered further investigation. 

The petitioner said that TV channels like India Today, Republic TV and Sathiyam TV "have not only discussed intricate details of the case, but have also uploaded the child sexual exploitation and abuse materials on the social and mainstream media".

It alleged that the Republic had a full debate showing the material. It argued that the channels were violating [Section 15 of the POCSO Act](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/10002733/) and Rule 11 of the rules made under it, and relied on [Section 67B of the Information Technology Act,](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/176300164/) which penalizes the publishing and transmitting material of CSAM content.

Besides those three media organisations, the respondents were the Greater Chennai Police Commissioner, the Director General of Police (Cyber Crime Wing), the Tamil Nadu Social Welfare and Women Empowerment Department, MeitY, the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, and "John Doe", described as all other unknown persons, news channels, media organisations, social media pages, accounts and intermediaries.

Why it matters: For all the rightful reasons, online CSAM content has been at the forefront of platform and content regulation. The Indian government and courts have been seeking better the content regulations and platform-level accountability frameworks for the CSAM-related content hosted and distributed on the social media intermediaries. Some of such recent incidents are: 

- Supreme Court sought MeitY’s action-taken report on CSAM: On September 25, the Supreme Court of India has [asked](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-supreme-court-meitys-action-csam/) Indian government to file its response on what action and precautions the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) and the Ministry of Law and Justice (MOLJ) have taken in response to the issue of Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) on online and social media platforms. 
    
- Google to report child sexual abuse content directly to Indian authorities: On September 22, beyond traditional reporting to US National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC), Google [said](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-google-child-abuse-reporting-india/) that it will directly share the CSAM-related reports directly with Indian law enforcements.
    
- Meta too report CSAM content to law enforcement authorities: Days before Google's announcement, Meta also [said](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-meta-child-abuse-cases-indian-police/) that it will report child sexual abuse cases to Indian law enforcements. 
    
- MeitY summons Meta over Instagram ads promoting CSAM: On July 4, MeitY [summoned](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-bbc-investigation-meity-summon-meta-instagram-ads-promoting-child-sexual-abuse-material/) officials of Meta after a [BBC investigation](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cvgm4e0316zo) found that Instagram was running paid advertisements promoting CSAM in India and later sent a "stern notice."
    
- X faced safe harbor threat after Grok generated CSAM content: On January 5, the government [said](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-safe-harbor-x-grok-csam-content/) that if X doesn't comply with the AI-generated obscene content, it would revoke the safe harbor status. 
    

Additionally, intermediaries are also changing how their platforms are designed for better ‘monitoring’ of CSAM content. For instance, citing the circulation of CSAM material via DMs on Instagram, Meta also [discontinued](https://www.medianama.com/2026/03/223-meta-ending-instagram-dm-e2ee/)  end-to-end encryption (E2EE) on Instagram, which exposes all the unencrypted conversations and exchanges to Meta’s internal systems and/or employees. The breaking of encryption on popular platforms like WhatsApp also has been a [long ask](https://www.medianama.com/2024/04/223-whatsapp-india-operations-end-to-end-encryption/) of the Indian government.
