**# Hackers accessed Central Bank of India's communications systems siphoning Rs. 13.97 crore

The Central Bank of India has filed a complaint with the Mumbai police alleging that 187 unauthorised transfers moved Rs. 13.97 crore from its settlement account with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) between October 15 and 17, according to a [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/mumbai-news/central-bank-of-india-reports-13-96-crore-loss-in-187-unauthorised-transactions-101792780875635.html) report published on October 24. 

How did the attackers accessed the public bank’s systems: The public bank's complaint at Nodal Cyber Police Station in Mumbai alleged that unknown persons had accessed the central Structured Financial Messaging System (SFMS) server, which is used as the communication system among the financial institutions like banks and RBI. Police have reportedly registered a case against unknown persons for cheating and personation.

The complaint states that in the normal RTGS process, a customer's account is debited in the bank's core system before a payment message passed through the bank's SFMS server to IFTAS's central server and the bank's RBI settlement account. The complaint alleges that unknown persons gained unauthorised access to IFTAS's central SFMS server through the bank's SFMS server and routed the money. 

While six transfers took place on October 15, 88 on October 16 and 93 on October 17, the HT report states that money was transferred to 36 different recipients.  10 accounts appeared in SFMS database logs, but the transaction IDs were not logged in the bank's records. The complaint also states that the accounts listed as payers did not belong to the Central Bank of India. 

Therefore, the bank's internal team could not find the 187 transfers in its branches, internet or mobile banking channels, core banking system, RTGS settlement account or SFMS server, according to the [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/mumbai-news/central-bank-of-india-reports-13-96-crore-loss-in-187-unauthorised-transactions-101792780875635.html) report. An unnamed police officer reportedly told the newspaper that little was known about the transactions, making it “very difficult to find how the money was siphoned off”.

‘No compromise’; banking communications are ‘safe and secure’: SFMS is operated by Indian Financial Technology and Allied Services (IFTAS), which is a RBI’s subsiadiary. IFTAS claimed in a [press release](https://www.iftas.in/#procurement) that “there is no compromise on any of the systems managed by IFTAS” and the Structured Financial Messaging System (SFMS) which is used for online money transcrtions is “safe and secure.”

In its [clarification note](https://x.com/aseemmanchanda/status/2114383934490558511?s=20), Central Bank of India said that "there was a technical issue observed in the interface environment of the localised RTGS server maintained at Bank's end" Without providing more details, the public bank claimed that no customer account is impacted and their "CBS System remains entirely secure and safe."

"RTGS Services to the customers have been fully resumed across all Channels," noted the bank in its public response. 

What is a Structured Financial Messaging System (SFMS)? It is a standardized electronic messaging platform for intra-bank and inter-bank financial communication. It was launched in 2013 and its systems were designed by the Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) for Institute for Development and Research in Banking and Technology (IDRBT) under the Reserve Bank of India, according to the [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/finance/idrbt-unveils-structured-messaging-system-101121701009_1.html) report when it was launched.

The research institute that developed the communication system SFMN says that it [uses](https://idrbtca.org.in/sfms.html) the Public Key Infrastructure (PKI ) method to secure its systems and for “Authentication, Confidentiality, Non-Repudiation and Integrity” 

“Under Public Key Infrastructure each entity will have a public key and a private key. Digital Signature will be a part of every message sent on SFMS. Digital Certificate to reside in smart card issued to authorized personnel. Smart card based access at the point of message generation will provide additional security and audit trail.” – [IDRBT](https://idrbtca.org.in/sfms.html) 

SFMS carries messages exchanged for payments carried throug the following payment systems Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS), which is often used for real-time high volume transactions and National Electronic Funds Transfer (NEFT), which is generally used for low volume transactions. 

The transfers reportedly left the bank's RBI settlement account, but the bank could not locate corresponding transactions in its ordinary banking records. 

The bank reportedly informed the following entities: 

- Reserve Bank of India (RBI), the core regulatory body for India's banks and financial institutions as well as the payment infrastructure.
    
- Indian Financial Technology & Allied Services ([IFTAS](https://www.iftas.in/)), RBI’s subsidiary that provides critical banking infrastructure to RBI, banks, cooperative societies and other financial institutions.. 
    
- DAKSH portal, which is [RBI’s platform](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=54503) for monitoring compliance, centralised reporting system for fraud and cyber incidents.
    
- National Critical Information Infrastructure Protection Centre ([NCIIPC](https://nciipc.gov.in/index.html)), [national nodal agency](https://www.india.gov.in/category/science-it-communication/subcategory/research-development/details/website-of-national-critical-information-infrastructure-protection-centre) established forprotecting the country's Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) from cyber threats, cyber terrorism, and cyber warfare
    
- Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In): India’s nodal agency for responding to computer security incidents
    

It remains unclear whether the money from the users’ accounts were debited, and whether customers suffered losses or if additional data breach or leak had occurred.

In a similar instance, in 2016, cyber hackers have stolen [100 million USD from the Bangladesh Bank](https://www.databreachtoday.com/bangladesh-bank-hackers-steal-100-million-a-8958). Similar to the Central Bank of India’s case, the attackers infiltrated the SWIFT software through malwares. Similar to SFMS, SWIFT is also a communication system that the banks use.
