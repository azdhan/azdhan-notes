# MeitY maintains no official data centre records; Parliament panel asks MEA to seek verified data

The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) has no official records on data center capacity and it is relying on the “industry estimates” to figure out the data centre growth and future demand requirements, revealed the recent parliamentary report. 

"Officially no data is maintained in MeitY regarding capacity of Data Centres," noted the recent parliamentary committee report titled "India’s Role in Global Tech Diplomacy, Digital Governance, Outer Space Diplomacy, Ai and Emerging Innovations" The parliamentary committee asked Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) for the verified data to encourage international investment. 

The same was also noted in the committee recommendations. Under one of the recommendations, the Committee noted MeitY's response: 

"...when asked whether India hosts nearly 20% of the world's data while accounting for only 3% of global data centre capacity, MeitY candidly acknowledged that neither figure is officially verified, and that no official data on India's data centre capacity is centrally maintained by the Ministry, with the figures placed before the Committee drawn instead from industry estimates. The Committee note that this uncertainty exists notwithstanding the acknowledged scale of the challenge, with industry estimates suggesting that capacity may need to expand several-fold, to somewhere between 5 and 10 GW, by 2030."

In this context, the committee has recommended that the Ministry of External Affairs to "take up with MeitY and other concerned Ministries the need for systematic collection and publication of verified data on India’s data centre capacity, utilisation and growth and use this evidence to prioritise international investment and technology partnerships addressing identified capacity gaps."

The requirement for the Minsitry to maintain the data is relevant considering the upcoming multi-crore AI data centers projects across states, including [Andhra Pradesh](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-andhra-pradesh-ai-data-centres/), [Telangana](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-tcs-hypervault-ai-data-centre-telangana/) and [Maharastra](https://www.medianama.com/2024/03/223-google-land-navi-mumbai-india-data-center/), 

Why the call for local data centers in India: "The Committee are concerned that the present gap results in a portion of India's data being stored or processed outside the country, raising concerns of latency, cost and, most significantly, data sovereignty"

The data sovereignty issue is relevant as the Reserve Bank of India's Storage of Payment System Data Directive (2018) mandates the regulated entities across the sectors like banking, insurance, fintech, healthcare, and government agencies require payment transactions data, including customer credentials, transaction logs, and personal details, must be stored exclusively in systems located within India.

Among various other issues related to data sovereignty, various countries' local laws on foreign surveillance could offer more surveillance risks. For instance, in April 2024, then US president Joe Biden signed a the [‘Reforming Intelligence and Securing America Act’(RISAA)](https://www.medianama.com/2024/04/223-us-senate-foreign-surveillance-bill/) that allows the US authorities seek data belonging to foreign nationals. The current plans for data centers by Indian government: 

- Long-term tax holidays for foreign cloud companies: "Government shall be providing a long-term tax holiday to foreign cloud service provider on procurement of Data Centre services from Indian Data Centres. This initiative is aimed at positioning India as a global digital infrastructure hub and is a major step in attracting investment in Data Centre industry provider on data centre and cloud infrastructure investment." Read more about such tech-policy announcements from the recent Union Budget [here](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-union-budget-2026-27-important-tech-policy-updates/)
    
- Industry status that could give them long term loans at cheaper rates: "For facilitating ease of finance, Data Centres were included in the Harmonized list of Infrastructure Sub-Sectors (HML) qualifying them for the infrastructure status at par with other infrastructure projects." Read more about this on [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2309950&reg=48&lang=2)
    

Relying on the industry estimates the Report noted that the data center capacity “has grown rapidly, from around 520 MW in 2020 to approximately 1.5 GW in 2025. It is projected to reach approximately 2 GW by 2026 and over 8 GW by 2030." It further noted that the major data centre hubs include Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, and Delhi NCR.
