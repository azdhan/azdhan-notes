# Elon Musk accuses ‘monopolisitc oligarchs’ for blocking the lauch of Starlink in India

Elon Musk has posted a cryptic post accusing Indian monopolistic “oligarchs” of stopping the launch of Starlink in India. He wrote on X: 

“Unfortunately, we are being blocked by certain oligarchs in order to maintain their monopolistic chokehold on the Indian people. You can guess who they are …This is a crime against the people of India!”

After this post went viral, the Press Bureau of Information (PIB) released a press release. It said: 

“The Government has put in place a fair and non-discriminatory authorisation framework for all telecommunication services, including satellite  communication services…. Under the terms and conditions of license, every such licensee is required to demonstrate compliance with security conditions. This is aimed at ensuring that services provided meet various security requirements to ensure safety of all users and their data.… At present, all the three entities are at broadly the same regulatory stage. As such, the suggestion that the framework or its application is unfair or discriminatory is baseless and misconceived.” - PIB

Elon Musk's post comes at a time when it is still awaiting approval to start its operations in India. Recently, in September 2026, Economic Times [reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-starlink-oneweb-jio-dcc-spectrum-approval/) that the Digital Communications Commission (DCC) has approved most of the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India’s recommendations on satellite spectrum allocation that could clear the regulatory hurdles/requirements for Starlink, Eutelsat OneWeb and Jio Satellite Communications. Meanwhile, Amazon’s Project Kuiper (Now rebranded as [Leo](https://www.aboutamazon.com/what-we-do/devices-services/amazon-leo)) was [awaiting](https://telecom.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/portal-in-portal/satcom/amazon-kuipers-satcom-application-under-review-as-nelco-and-yotta-get-rejected/127790073) a GMPCS licence from the DoT. 

All the above mentioned three entities already have the following licenses/permissions: 

- Global Mobile Personal Communication by Satellite ([GMPCS](https://www.medianama.com/2025/06/223-starlink-india-gmpcs-license-dot-approval/))
    
- India’s space regulator, the Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre [(IN-SPACe)](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-starlink-inspace-approval-india/),
    

However, these are not operational in India. Because they, especially Starlink, are awaiting for the final approval from the required security approvals from the Department of Telecommunications.

Despite these hurdles, Starlink has made its infrastructure ready for launch in India: Speaking at the Indian Mobile Congress, Starlink's Vice President of Starlink Business Operations at SpaceX Lauren Dreyer [said](https://x.com/ANI/status/2107737396020449584?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw%7Ctwcamp%5Etweetembed%7Ctwterm%5E2107737396020449584%7Ctwgr%5E23e7cfa82d394a464b9be939b397d1808dd313fe%7Ctwcon%5Es1_&ref_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.firstpost.com%2Ftech%2Fimc-2026-starlink-says-india-operations-ready-awaits-government-clearances-14051157.html), "We have built our Starlink operations specifically for India in recognition of Indian security requirements and other regulations... We have already made the investment and built the existing ground infrastructure here: 20 gateway sites comprising hundreds of antennas, and we have established the operations to be ready to serve India."

Even before the regulatory approvals, Maharastra CM Devendra Fadnavis [signed](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-maharashtra-starlink-satellite-internet-rural-remote-areas/) a letter of intent to enable internet or network access to the remote regions like  Gadchiroli, Nandurbar, Washim, and Dharashiv.

What are the security concerns of Starlink in India? In June 2026, MediaNama [reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-india-security-risks-starlink-oneweb-ses/), based on an Economic Times report, that the  Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) had formed a Group of Secretaries to examine the security risks, like “signal spillage” in border areas. The national security concerns of the government could be legitimate concerning the atleast two of the following incidents:

- Potential internet shutdown bypass: In January 2025, The Guardian [reported](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/jan/03/elon-musks-starlink-satellites-allegedly-used-to-defy-indias-internet-shutdowns) that illegally smuggled Starlink devices were used by the civilians and armed groups at Manipur and the surrounding regions to bypass the internet shut downs. 
    
- Operations without approvals: Starlink is being operated in Iran without required regulatory approvals. Recently, according to [Tehran News](https://www.tehrantimes.com/news/530789/Iran-protests-Norway-s-inaction-over-unauthorized-Starlink-operations), the Iranian Foreign Ministry Director General summoned the Norwegian Ambassador to Tehran's formal protest over Norway’s failure to take effective action against unauthorized Starlink operations.'
