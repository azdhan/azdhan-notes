 **# What remains unanswered from Delhi Police's RTI response to live CCTV facial recognition and surveillance

 Internet Freedom Founation (IFF) has filed an Right to Information (RTI) application with the Delhi Police seeking the documents related to the deployment of live CCTV vans deployed at the July 2026’s student-led Jantar Mantar Protests. It received the Delhi Police’s [standing order dated June 9, 2022](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1oeOJGeZjyWE1OvStTfiyJuVHjb-BypLR/view?ref=static.internetfreedom.in), which was [referred](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/268/AU1996_LM2Ok7.pdf?source=pqars) ([archived pdf](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/annex_268_AU1996_LM2Ok7.pdf)) by the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA), when asked about safeguards for facial recognition does not mention facial recognition

The copy of the RTI reply reviewed by MediaNama likewise has no reference to biometrics, databases, watchlists, match thresholds, retention, deletion, privacy or audits.

Despite being [publicly seen with a mobile CCTV surveillance monitoring system](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-delhi-police-facial-recognition-jantar-mantar-protests/), when IFF asked for the tenders, contracts and data-handling terms for the "Ikshana" surveillance van and received none, the Delhi Police’s Special Cell reportedly [said](https://internetfreedom.in/iff-makes-public-the-delhi-police-order-cited-for-facial-recognition-as-it-continues-evasion-we-will-file-appeals/#:~:text=The%20Special%20Cell-,replied,-that%20%E2%80%9Cno%20such):“no such type of vehicle is being used/purchased”

Delhi Police’s standing order, receieved through the RTI response, states that the "real time city-wide [CCTV] surveillance" and that the Safe City system "will have tools for predictive policing". It does not say what these tools are exactly or what are the databases they use. Integrated Command, Control, Coordination and Communication (C4i) system is their command centre that receives the city's camera feeds. They established it in 2010, which is nearly at the “end of life stage” as the new Safe Ciry project is yet to be fully established.

The Delhi Police’s RTI reply doesn’t reveal anything about teh Delhi Police’s CCTV surveillance and reported live facial recognition matching. It leaves out more questions than the answers.  

What remains unanswered: 

- What is/are the database(s) used to match the individuals?
    
- What are the needed safeguards and thresholds to avoid state overreach/misuse? 
    
- What are the data retention limits and other privacy preserving guidelines to protect the privacy of the citizens?
    
- Are there any accuracy audits or a privacy impact assessment? If so, who conducts them and at what frequency? 
    
- What is the overall legal basis of the SOP itself, which doesn’t detail the surveillance or sensitive data handling?
    
- Who are the tech-builders behind the surveillance infrastructure and the tenders or contracts that enabled them?
    

“This is the gap in the law that we have been pointing out since 2020. In [Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/91938676/?ref=static.internetfreedom.in) the Supreme Court held that any intrusion into privacy must first be authorised by law, and an internal order that does not bind anyone outside the force, and does not even mention the intrusion in question, cannot be treated as such a law.”  – IFF

What we know about Delhi Police’s CCTV surveillance vans: CP PLUS has [announced](https://lnkd.in/p/g7bQvFsd) in 2023 that it designed the Ikshana prototype for Delhi Police. CP Plus’ Linkedin post detailed that “the mobile surveillance vehicle - Ikshana, has been equipped with: 

- Advanced AI-powered Automatic Number-Plate Recognition (ANPR) Cameras, 
    
- Mobile PTZ (Pan-Tilt-Zoom) cameras and 
    
- Varifocal Bullet Cameras to provide 360° coverage of its surroundings,
    
- Face Detection and Recognition Functions,
    
- Vehicle and Vehicle Number Plate Detection and Recognition Functions, 
    
- Intelligent Analytical Support On The Spot, 
    
- Traffic Management,
    
- Crowd Control and Management Functions, etc.”

The 2023’s linkedin post also stated tha the pilot project was already operational in select regions. IFF has archived a similar press release [here](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1zV8XNIz-YNghOW4DO1Hi6fooFHrGZroK/view?ref=static.internetfreedom.in). 

Similar to IFF’s RTI reply, MediaNama's own [RTI response](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-rti-delhi-police-claims-no-records-of-ai-glasses/) on the Delhi Police and Meta-AI-glasses in February found no tender, approval or grievance procedure on record. They said, “no such record/information/directions/tender is available in this Unit,”

Why this matters: In July 2026, during the [first series](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-digital-developments-second-day-internet-shutdown-jantar-mantar/) of Crockroach Janta Party (CJP) and student-led protests at New Delhi’s Jantar Mantar protest site, the [Delhi Police were seen monitoring](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-delhi-police-facial-recognition-jantar-mantar-protests/) live CCTV footage via remote police vans and possibly matching with the existing unknown databases. In their analysis IFF noted that the police's [own protest guidelines](https://delhipolice.gov.in/StandingOrderFiles/83377_Law%20and%20Order-48-2022.pdf) named Jantar Mantar and Ramlila Ground as the venues for demonstrations, so both were connected to the command centre by June 2022. 

After the July 2026 Jantar Mantar protests and police violence, the questions concerning facial recognition appeared before the Delhi High Court and Supreme Court of India. In response to the SC, the Delhi Police said in an [affidavit](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-student-protests-delhi-police-affidavit-facial-recognition-software-only-captures-serious-offense-criminals-546263) that its system identified 2,873 people with criminal antecedents at the protest site between July 20 and 26. IFF says, citing the affidavit, that 2,402 were matched against "Crime Kundli" and 471 against "criminal records" [The Indian Express found](https://indianexpress.com/article/express-exclusive/delhi-police-facial-recognition-jantar-mantar-protest-accused-in-jail-10862099/) that at least 25 of them were in jail at the time, as [MediaNama reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-delhi-polices-facial-recognition-jantar-mantar/).

While the state continues to use the advanced tech for the surveillance and monitoring purposes at the sensitive regions, like Jantar Mantar, the public is still unsure of how these operations of live surveillances are governed and the if the Police or Ministry of Home Affairs (which governs Delhi Police) has issued any safeguarding measures that would protect the citizens from the state overreach. 

What does Delhi Poice monitor duing the “Digital/virtual patrolling" According to the Delhi Police’s SOP document, the police officials are supposed to monitor for these activities: 

- “Suspicious Object/Person.
    
- Any abandoned vehicle left parked.
    
- Any vehicle found moving suspiciously.
    
- Any Illegal/Criminal/Abnormal activity.
    
- Uncontrolled/Unruly crowd behaviour.
    
- Trafficj am.
    
- Stray Cattle/Animals etc. on road.
    
- Any other object such as Tree Fall, Pole etc. on the road.
    
- Bursted water pipe line etc.”
    

None of these are clearly defined in the Delhi Police SOP order. The operators who surveil have to pass alerts to police stations.
