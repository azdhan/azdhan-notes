# Modern Masters: SS Rajamouli (2024): A Simple and Humble Hagiographic Tribute by Netflix.

The contemporary audience of Telugu Cinema grew as Rajamouli grew as an excellent film maker. We all saw his films progress breaking the budget limits first before breaking the box office collections. This one-hour length documentary is a tiny dive into the life of Rajamouli.

The central element of the documentary is how the whole family comes in together to make one movie. If you see the whole family of SSR is working for the movie, from sons to cousins. At this discovering moment, the thugs of internet might shrug him off as another nepotistic family in Telugu cinema. This documentary produced by Film Companion Studios, the neo-avatar of Film Companion, where this documentary excels. It shows you how passionately everyone in the family is coming together for one man's dream to come true.

It is delightful to see SSR being SSR while he is shooting, a literal workaholic. The hard work he does is romantically celebrated across the documentary. SSR doesn't hesitate to admit that his way of working to achieve perfection is tiring for the whole cast and crew but his loyalty is also to the producer whose loss is unaccountably lost if the discomforts are given the importance it rightfully demands. He says as the documentary ends, the only thing I am slave to is my story.

Anupama Chopra, confronts SSR with the criticism that his movies were accused of being sexist and casteist and propagating the right wing sensibilities. He elaborates on the pulling more no. of people and how "they don't understand storytelling". It makes me wonder, are we ought to see the act as a emotive storytelling tool and not as an element that has been incorporated deliberately to invoke anything that is political? At large, the question is this: should we see art as just entertainment?
