Nikhil predicts that in the future AI traffic will overpower human traffic. This seems obvious, but the more important implication is how business websites will be reconfigured. Today, websites are designed from a human UI/UX perspective. In the future, they may shift toward agentic optimization.

One possible outcome is the emergence of two interfaces: one for human interaction and another for agentic interaction. The agentic interface might not be visible to humans. However, this separation may not be fully possible. Agentic browsing could require human monitoring, especially when interventions are needed, such as authenticating payments or entering passwords or login credentials. Whether two distinct interfaces will exist remains uncertain.

This leads to the question of machine readability. Current website UI and UX are not inherently machine-readable. In contrast, an MS Excel sheet is human-readable in rows and columns, but when converted into a CSV file, it becomes less readable for humans while remaining easily interpretable by machines. Similarly, the web could develop a “CSV-type” interface that supports UX but cannot be understood at a glance without an additional interpretive layer.

Nikhil’s second prediction concerns the integration of health records. This may not work at a systemic level. In India, much health-related data still originates in rural areas, and the overall system remains fragmented. At the hospital level, change may be limited. However, at the user level, change is more plausible.

A possible model is a service where users intentionally provide their health data. That service could store the data and offer historical context over the user’s lifetime. This user-driven model may function more effectively than a centralized system. Large-scale implementation, such as centralized health IDs proposed by the government, may struggle due to the lack of consistent information flow and fragmented records. Centralized records risk inaccuracy because the data itself is incomplete. In contrast, when users directly provide their data, the service can make statements with greater accuracy, since conclusions are based on explicitly submitted information.

The third point concerns agents and social media. Agent-driven social media management appears plausible. At the same time, the survival of social media platforms depends heavily on network effects. For example, in India, it is nearly impossible to remain socially connected without WhatsApp because of its network dominance.

Without network effects, it is unclear whether an application can survive independently. Meta AI currently lags behind top AI platforms such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, and Google AI in capability. However, Meta benefits from strong network effects across its platforms, which supports its long-term survival.

In contrast, apps or services that depend solely on the services they offer—especially when those services are easily replaceable by competitors through better quality or lower prices—remain vulnerable. Replaceable services can be displaced, but network effects are harder to replicate. Migration from one ecosystem to another requires significant shifts, such as the movement from Facebook to Instagram. Even that transition was facilitated by their shared ownership under Meta. Ecosystem-level change requires radical adoption shifts.

This suggests that the social layer in AI applications may become a major challenge unless platforms deliberately build it. There may also be new developments such as agentic wallets with limited access to funds. Since agents cannot be fully trusted with unrestricted financial access, limiting their spending authority could reshape agentic commerce.

Regarding social media and video platforms, there may eventually be features that deliberately filter out AI-generated content and preserve only human-generated content. As AI-generated content becomes cheaper and more abundant, human-generated content may become more valuable. Scarcity could increase the cost and perceived value of human-created material.
