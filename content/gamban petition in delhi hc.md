SYNOPSIS
The instant Writ Petition has been filed by the Petitioner under Article 226 of the Constitution of India, 1950 ["the Constitution"], inter alia challenging the validity of the Order(s) issued by the Respondent Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), under Section 69A of the Information Technology Act, 2000 ["IT Act"] read with the provisions of the Information Technology (Procedure and Safeguards for Blocking for Access of Information by Public) Rules, 2009 [the "Blocking Rules"], for blocking the Petitioner's website and software/ mobile application Gamban in India, and seeking issuance of a writ in the nature of certiorari or any other appropriate writ, quashing the Order.
The Petitioner, Gamban Ltd., is a global company established in the United Kingdom in 2017, which has developed and offers the software/ mobile application "Gamban" ("the App") — a tool that assists users suffering from gambling-related harm to control their addiction by blocking access to gambling, betting, casino and online money gaming apps, websites and platforms. The App is offered in 197 countries, has over 600,000 registrations across more than 800,000 protected devices, and is ISO 27001:2022 and ISO 9001:2015 certified.
The Petitioner's website and the App have been blocked since February 2023, when the Respondent had, in a bid to crack down on online gaming/ gambling, blocked several online gaming apps in India, apparently on the mistaken premise that the App is an online gaming/ gambling application. In fact, the App does the opposite — it helps individuals overcome gambling addiction.
Despite the Petitioner addressing repeated representations to the Respondent since May 2023 — clarifying the true nature and functioning of the App, refuting the allegations subsequently levelled by the Respondent, and furnishing an independent ISO 27001:2022 security certification, the Respondent has not restored access to the App, nor furnished the Petitioner a copy of the blocking order. Further, the Respondent vide its latest email reply dated 05.05.2026 had stated that the Petitioners request for unblocking its app has been forwarded to the concerned nodal agency and that they would revert shortly with an update, indicating that the Petitioners request merits consideration. However, for the past over two months, there has been no further response received from the Respondent despite following up.
The grounds for invoking the jurisdiction of this Hon'ble Court are as under:
The blocking order suffers from a complete violation of principles of natural justice and the procedural safeguards mandated under Section 69A of the IT Act and the Blocking Rules, as interpreted by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Shreya Singhal vs Union of India, (2015) 5 SCC 1, inasmuch as no notice, no reasoned order, and no opportunity of hearing was ever afforded to the Petitioner.

GROUNDS
I. VIOLATION OF THE PROCEDURAL SAFEGUARDS PROVIDED IN SECTION 69A OF THE IT ACT
A. BECAUSE the Order directing blocking of the App is completely arbitrary, unfair and unreasonable. The covert and clandestine manner in which the App has been blocked, without communicating the Order to the Petitioner and without providing the Petitioner with an opportunity of hearing, is violative of principles of natural justice.
B. BECAUSE the Order directing blocking of the App is violative of the procedural safeguards provided in the IT Act and the Blocking Rules. As per Section 69A(1) of the IT Act, the directions for blocking any information must be by way of a reasoned order. Further, as per Rule 8 of the Blocking Rules, the designated officer responsible for issuing blocking orders is required to: (i) make all reasonable efforts to identify the originator or
intermediary hosting the information to be blocked; (ii) issue a notice to such person; (iii) provide an opportunity of hearing to such person before the concerned authority.
C. BECAUSE the Hon'ble Supreme Court, in Shreya Singhal vs Union of India, (2015) 5 SCC 1 @ Paras 114 and 115, had upheld the validity of Section 69A of the IT Act solely for the reason that S.69A (unlike S.66A of the IT Act, which the Hon'ble Supreme Court had declared unconstitutional) provided several safeguards viz.:
(i) That blocking can only be resorted to where the Central Government is satisfied that it is necessary to do so; (ii) Such necessity is relatable only to some of the subjects set out in Article 19(2) of the Constitution of India; and (iii) Reasons have to be recorded in writing in such blocking order so that they may be assailed in a writ petition under Article 226 of the Constitution. (iv) The Rules provide for a hearing before the Committee set up, which Committee then looks into whether or not it is necessary that a blocking order is made.
D. BECAUSE the Respondent cannot be permitted to bypass the procedural safeguards provided by the statute by invoking emergency powers under Rule 9 of the Blocking Rules. Even if an interim order of blocking is passed under Rule 9, due process requires that notice be issued to the originator or the intermediary hosting the information after the request for blocking is placed before the Review Committee in terms of Rule 9(3) and before a final order of blocking is passed under Rule 9(4). Any other interpretation of Rule 9 would render the procedural safeguards as redundant and Rule 9 as violative of Article 19(2) in terms of the judgment in Shreya Singhal (supra).
E. BECAUSE even if the blocking order was passed under Rule 9 and not Rule 8, it is the Respondent's own understanding that under Rule 9, notice is required to be issued to the originator or
the intermediary within 48 hours of the passing of the interim blocking order, and an opportunity of hearing must be given to the originator or the intermediary. In this regard, reliance is placed on the judgment dated 19.06.2026 passed by this Hon'ble Court in Telegram FZ LLC & Anr. vs Union of India & Ors. [W.P.(C) No. 8259 of 2026], wherein the following submissions had been made on behalf of the Respondent:
"14. Mr. Tushar Mehta, learned Solicitor General of India along with Mr. Chetan Sharma, learned Additional Solicitor General of India appearing for MeitY and MHA, made the following submissions:
… 14.2. In compliance with Rule 9 of the 2009 Rules, due intimation was issued to the Petitioners within the prescribed period of 48 hours, and a personal hearing was afforded before the Committee on 17.06.2026. Thereafter, the Committee passed the Final Order, which is a detailed and reasoned order, upon due consideration of the Petitioners' submissions…"
F. BECAUSE it is a settled principle of law that if a statute provides for a thing to be done in a particular manner, then it has to be done in that manner alone and no other manner. Therefore, when an officer is vested with power and that power is circumscribed by a procedure laid down in a statute, then that power must be exercised in the manner prescribed, failing
which, it would fall foul of the requirement of complying with due process under law. In this regard, reliance is placed on Chandra Kishore Jha vs Mahavir Prasad, (1999) 8 SCC 266 and OPTO Circuit India Ltd. vs Axis Bank, (2021) 6 SCC 707.
G. BECAUSE despite repeated requests from the Petitioner over the course of three years, the blocking order has not yet been supplied to the Petitioner, which is in contravention of Section 69A as interpreted by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Shreya Singhal (supra). Section 69A of the IT Act read with the judgment in Shreya Singhal (supra) requires that the reasoned order of blocking be supplied to the originator or the intermediary hosting the information, since in Shreya Singhal (supra) @ Para 114, the Hon'ble Supreme Court held that reasons have to be recorded in writing in the blocking order so that they may be assailed in a writ petition under Article 226.
H. BECAUSE the Hon'ble Supreme Court has time and again reiterated that the free flow of information from petitioners and the state, in writ proceedings, is an important step towards governmental transparency and openness, and the State has an obligation to disclose information which is in its possession and which may be necessary for a petitioner to articulate his case. Reliance is placed on Anuradha Bhasin vs Union of India, (2020) 3 SCC 637 and Manohar Lal Sharma vs Union of India, (2023) 11 SCC 401.
I. BECAUSE this Hon'ble Court has also previously directed the Respondent to supply copies of the blocking orders to the aggrieved parties. Reliance is placed upon the Order dated 11.05.2022 passed by this Hon'ble Court in Tanul Thakur vs Union of India [W.P.(C) No. 13037/2019].
J. BECAUSE apart from the fact that no reasoned order has been supplied to the Petitioner, even the Email dated 06.05.2025 issued by the Respondent merely states that "It is to informed you that Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) is empowered to issue blocking directions to block the Information for public access under section 69A of the Information Technology Act
    2000. Section 69A of the IT Act, 2000 empowers Government to block

information from public access under specific conditions i.e., in the interest of : (i) sovereignty and integrity of India, (ii)defense of India, (iii) security of the State, (iv) friendly relations with foreign States or (v) public order or (vi) for preventing incitement to the commission of any cognizable offense relating to above" and that "MeitY after following due process as envisaged in the Information Technology (Procedure Safeguards for Blocking for Access of Information by Public) Rules, 2009, ordered to block Gamban mobile application" Merely reproducing the statutory language and stating that due process was followed cannot make an order reasoned or speaking.
K. BECAUSE even otherwise, the contents of the Email dated 06.05.2025 are incorrect, as the proper procedure prescribed under the Blocking Rules read with the judgment in Shreya Singhal (supra) was not followed inasmuch as (1) no notice was issued to the Petitioner prior to blocking the App; (2) no opportunity of hearing was provided to the Petitioner; (3) no order, reasoned or otherwise, has been communicated to the Petitioner.
L. BECAUSE if the proper procedure had been followed, the Respondent would have been aware that the App is not an online gaming/ gambling app but an app that blocks access to online games/ gambling websites and assists in rehabilitation of gambling addicts. Therefore, the Respondent would not have directed the blocking of the App, since blocking the App would be counterproductive to the Respondent' objective of reducing online gambling. The App in fact helps in furthering the Respondents' cause of ensuring that online gambling/ betting/ online money games are not disseminated and accessed by users in India and the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025, which prohibits all forms of online money games, is implemented in letter and spirit.
II. THE BLOCKING OF THE APP IS NOT JUSTIFIED
M. BECAUSE the limited circumstances under which a blocking order under Section 69A of the IT Act can be passed is in the interest of (1) sovereignty and integrity of India, (2) defense of India, (3) security of the State, (4) friendly relations with foreign States, (5) public order or (6) for preventing incitement to the commission of any cognizable offense relating to above. The Email dt. 06.05.2026 does not specify which of the abovementioned grounds have been invoked by the Respondent for blocking of the App.
N. BECAUSE the only allegations against the Petitioner/ the App in the Email dt. 06.05.2025 are: (a) It was observed that there were significant concerns
regarding the user data privacy, particularly for android users; (b) The App was found to be operating as a 'super app', which can monitor browser history and scan other apps, including banking apps; (c) It was observed that if users did not continue with the subscription, they might be exposed to higher volume of gambling-related advertisements.
