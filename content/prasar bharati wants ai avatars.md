# Prasar Bharati seeks feedback on AI avatars & content creations; suggests mandatory labeling

In an attempt to expand its [AI avatars](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2021431&reg=48&lang=2), Prasar Bharati, India's public service broadcaster, is seeking industry feedback on a draft Expression of Interest (EOI) to empanel agencies, studios, start-ups for AI-generated avatars and other AI content creation, localisation, and archive repurposing across its broadcast and digital platforms, like WAVES OTT, Doordarshan, Akashvani and DD India.

Notably, the draft EOI specifies India's AI/synthetically generated-media governance norm. All AI-generated content delivered to Prasar Bharati must comply with statutory labeling requirements for "synthetically generated information" under applicable IT rules.

What Is Prasar Bharati seeking? Companies can apply to one or more of nine categories, each evaluated independently:

- AI Avatars, Digital Humans & Virtual Presenters: AI anchors, synthetic newscasters, multilingual digital humans
    
- AI Localisation, Translation & Dubbing: multilingual dubbing, lip-sync, subtitling
    
- Short-Form Content (30 sec–3 min): explainers, promos, social-first videos
    
- Micro Dramas (1–5 min): short fictional/semi-fictional narratives for OTT and social
    
- Music Videos (2–6 min)
    
- Intermediate Series (8–10 min): fiction, infotainment, documentary shorts
    
- Regular Series (25–30 min): multi-episode serials for linear and OTT
    
- Feature Films (90 min+): subject to enhanced eligibility norms
    
- Archive Repurposing & Restoration: AI-assisted restoration, upscaling, metadata tagging of Doordarshan/Akashvani archives
    

AI Avatars, Digital Humans & Virtual Presenters: This covers AI anchors, digital presenters, virtual hosts, multilingual digital human solutions, interactive public information presenters and synthetic newscasters. Citing the "unique ethical, legal and reputational implications" of this category for a national public broadcaster, the draft EOI singles it out for the most additional conditions of any category.

Prasar Bharati’s AI Ethics and Deepfake Policy Declaration:

- No deepfakes of living public figures, elected officials or religious figures without prior approval.
    
- No synthetic voice cloning without the consent.
    
- Machine-readable provenance record (C2PA or equivalent) on all AI-generated/assisted content
    
- On-screen AI disclosure for all AI anchor/presenter deployments.
    
- DPDPA, 2023, compliance for all personal data processed.
    
- All primary AI models, platforms and APIs should be disclosed in the Technical Note/Capability Matrix.
    
- All AI models used should be commercially licensed and cleared for public-broadcasting use.
    
- Training data complies with applicable data rights, with no unlicensed third-party content
    
- Documented human editorial review before any AI content is broadcast-ready
    

As per the EoI, the companies should have the documented consent framework for any digital human or avatar based on, or derived from, the likeness, voice or persona of a real individual, alongside a clear policy on creating synthetic newscasters or AI presenters, including public disclosures to audiences that the presenter is AI-generated. 

Technically, they must show multilingual digital human capability with real-time or near-real-time lip-sync across at least three Indian languages, and must have already deployed at least one AI anchor or digital human presenter in a broadcast, OTT, or digital publishing context, with some experience..

Prasar Bharati stated that it is "turnover-agnostic" while selecting the companies. It reasoned that frontier AI capability in video production, digital humans and archive restoration often resides in "younger companies and specialist teams" rather than only large incumbent production houses. Accordingly:

Data-handling conditions: Companies should have mandatory NDAs, compliance with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and a general prohibition on storing or processing Prasar Bharati's archive material outside India without explicit written consent, except where it is "technically necessitated by the AI workflow and disclosed upfront." Agencies must also disclose any third-party AI model providers or cloud platforms used in production. 

Why It Matters: The draft EOI is a notable case of a public broadcaster writing India's still-forming synthetic-media rules directly into its own procurement conditions, especially by including the MeitY’s SGI labeling requirement for social media content under the IT Rules. By making C2PA-like provenance tagging, on-screen AI disclosure, and statutory synthetic-content labeling non-negotiable for empanelment, Prasar Bharati is effectively preempting compliance obligations that might be pushed on to private players too. 

This is just an expression of interest for empanelment only, and may not constitute a tender, a guarantee of work, or any financial commitment. Stakeholders can submit comments, suggestions and queries by September 21, 2026, 5 PM, to Amit Tandon, Deputy Director General (Sales – OTT), at amittandon@prasarbharati.gov.in, or by post to the address specified in the EoI.
