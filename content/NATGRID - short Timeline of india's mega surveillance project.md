#techpolicy/surveillance/india


Here are the key developments around NATGRID since 2008: 

2008: Mumbai city saw coordinated Islamist terrorist strikes in Mumbai, India, from Nov. 26 to 29, 2008. These were later termed as 26/11 Mumbai Terror Attacks.  
Ten gunmen from the Pakistan-based group Lashkar-e-Taiba carried out the assault, killing 166 victims and nine attackers and injuring more than 300 people.

2009: Even before NATGRID was envisioned, the central government [proposed](https://www.medianama.com/2023/02/223-indian-govt-dragnet-surveillance-challenged-delhi-high-court-2/#:~:text=%3A%20In%20late-,2009,-%2C%20the%20government%20announced) a “Centralised Monitoring System” (CMS) that would monitor communications across mobiles, landlines, and the Internet in India

2009: The Ministry of Home Affairs said the government had [approved](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ywkNx-ZX_AxjdacsZiaJuJyCvcHbZrHj/view?usp=sharing) the creation of the National Intelligence Grid and would soon appoint a CEO.

2010: After Mumbai's 26/11 attack, then Union Minister P Chidambaram gave the [authorisation](https://www.medianama.com/2021/09/223-natgrid-database-live-soon-4/#:~:text=was%20first%20given-,authorisation,-by%20then%20Union) for the setup of NATGRID through an executive order.


2011: An RTI [response](https://www.medianama.com/2023/02/223-indian-govt-dragnet-surveillance-challenged-delhi-high-court-2/#:~:text=others%2C%20a%202011-,RTI,-revealed.) reportedly revealed that the NATGRID collates and analyses information from 21 departments, like a few mentioned above, to “detect terrorist activity, and pre-empt attacks or find the perpetrators”  
  
Later, NATGRID was pulled out from the purview of RTI, making it more difficult to know anything about NATGRID and also to hold it accountable.

2011: Congress MP Manish Tewari introduced [“The Intelligence Services (Powers and Regulation) Bill”](https://www.medianama.com/2021/07/223-bill-surveillance-regulation-reform-2/) that sought to regulate surveillance by the government bodies. The bill, which mandated authorised warrants to intercept any form of communications, was never passed.

However, in 2024, he presented [another bill in 2019](https://sansad.in/getFile/BillsTexts/LSBillTexts/Asintroduced/249%20of%202019%20as.pdf?source=legislation) [[archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20251208084500/https://sansad.in/getFile/BillsTexts/LSBillTexts/Asintroduced/249%20of%202019%20as.pdf?source=legislation)] and [in 2024](https://sansad.in/getFile/BillsTexts/LSBillTexts/Asintroduced/78%20of%202024%20AS89202454426PM.pdf?source=legislation) [[archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20251208084547/https://sansad.in/getFile/BillsTexts/LSBillTexts/Asintroduced/78%20of%202024%20AS89202454426PM.pdf?source=legislation)] as well. 

2013: Before the NDA government took over the power in National politics, the UPA-2 regime was [planning](https://www.medianama.com/2013/12/223-natgrid-will-be-up-and-running-in-four-months/) to launch the NATGRID before the 2014 general elections-- which began the NDA's reign to power that continues till date.

2013: [https://www.medianama.com/2013/07/223-india-to-ask-isps-telcos-to-link-to-cms-wholl-sue-the-indian-government/](https://www.medianama.com/2013/07/223-india-to-ask-isps-telcos-to-link-to-cms-wholl-sue-the-indian-government/) 

2013: The Indian government [sought](https://www.medianama.com/2013/12/223-india-us-help-decrypt-whatsapp-skype/) US aid, asking to share the technology that could enable the state to decrypt the conversations happening through encrypted platforms like WhatsApp. The then UPA government also cited the suspicion of anti-social elements as the reason to snoop into online (encrypted) conversations.

2013: Starting in 2013, the Indian government [started](https://www.firstpost.com/india/telecom-central-monitoring-system-in-10-circles-by-december-914389.html) installing the Centralised Monitoring System (CMS) in 10 out of the country’s 22 service areas to intercept the telephonic communications without requiring the need of telecom operators, like Airtel or Vodafone. [[archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20251208111811/https://www.firstpost.com/india/telecom-central-monitoring-system-in-10-circles-by-december-914389.html)]

2014: There were [talks](http://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/politics-and-nation/natgrid-to-take-off-soon-on-home-ministry-data/articleshow/28921529.cms) within the Home Ministry to [partially pilot](https://www.medianama.com/2014/01/223-natgrid-partial-roll-out/) the NATGRID project by linking databases like the National Population Register (NPR) and Immigration, Visa, Foreigners Registration and Tracking System (IVFRT) to NATGRID's central database.

2014: NETRA, Network Traffic Analysis, was [launched](https://www.medianama.com/2014/01/223-indian-govt-internet-monitoring-system-netra/) to monitor the social media content in real-time.

2014: When a parliamentarian asked a question on if the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has objected to sharing information with NATGRID, [RBI and the Indian Banks' Association (IBA) "unanimously" said](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1VSWLKqxpFyPQaHUiXkKl9YDMH7r3_a1t/view?usp=sharing) that "it would not be possible for banks under the current legislative framework to disclose customer information."

2015: MoS Home Haribhai Chaudhary [told](https://www.medianama.com/2019/09/223-indias-home-minister-amit-shah-revive-natgrid-all-you-need-to-know-natgrid/#:~:text=On%204th%20March-,2015,-%2C%20the%20Minister%20of) Parliament that NATGRID has 10 user agencies: IB, R&AW, CBI, DRI, ED, FIU, CBDT, CBEC, DGCEI and NCB.

2016: Similarly, after two years of NETRA, the central government also [launched](https://www.medianama.com/2016/05/223-india-central-monitoring-system-live-in-delhi-mumbai/) CMS, Central Monitoring System, that would automatically surveil through intercepting and monitoring phone calls and the internet as well.

2016: However, it was only in 2016 that NATGRID's CEO was [appointed](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/natgrid-wants-to-link-social-media-accounts-to-central-database/article29402252.ece). IB officer Ashok Patnaik became its first CEO.

2018: The 2018-19 finance reports show that Rs. 181.75 crores were sanctioned to build the NATGRID buildings in Delhi and Bengaluru. These figures were revealed in the [Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs' Committee on Public Undertakings (2020-21)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1JZ2I-SEFiddHuHHe7xeeI96nU_5h43hi/view?usp=sharing)

2020: The database was [scheduled](https://www.medianama.com/2019/11/223-natgrid-operational-december-2020-parliament-2/) to be operationalised in December 2020, as per the response given in the parliament.

Though there is no official information about NATGRID starting its operations, the [media reports](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/natgrid-to-scale-up-surveillance-offer-real-time-intel-on-individuals-123042800263_1.html) indicate that it started its operations in 2020.

2020: In 2020, NATGRID [signed](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/natgrid-to-have-access-to-database-that-links-around-14000-police-stations/article32058643.ece) an MoU with the National Crime Records Bureau to access the Crime and Criminal Tracking Network and Systems (CCTNS) database, which connects about 14,000 police stations nationwide. 

All state police are required to register all First Information Reports on the CCTNS platform.

2021: Home Minister Amit Shah [cited](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/india/explained-what-is-natgrid-indias-counter-terrorism-platform-7459611.html) Covid as the reason for the delayed operationalisation, which was originally envisioned for 2020.

2021: MeitY [sent](https://www.medianama.com/2021/10/223-chinese-smartphones-snooping-govt-inspection/) notices to China-originated smartphone companies, like OnePlus, Xiaomi, Vivo, and Oppo, asking them the detailed information about the smartphones and their components, as the government was planning to inspect and test the smartphones and the applications installed in it for any potential snooping activities.  
  
A year before that, MeitY also [banned](https://www.medianama.com/2020/09/223-india-bans-118-chinese-apps/) several Chinese apps after a turf at the national border.

2021: More than 12 indian agencies like IB, RAW, NIA, ED, CBI, NCB, etc, were reportedly [snooping](https://www.medianama.com/2021/06/223-government-prying-clubhouse-chatroom/) into the online chatroom conversations, like Clubhouse-- which was also [banned](https://www.medianama.com/2021/02/223-clubhouse-blocked-china/) in mainland China as it was adopted for "uncensored" discussions.

2021: In the same year, the government allegedly used Israeli spyware, Pegasus, to snoop on opposition leaders, prominent people, including a few journalists. Despite finding the malware in atleast 5 devices, out of 29 devices submitted to the court, the Supreme Court of India [didn't find conclusive evidence](https://theleaflet.in/privacy/justice-r-v-raveendran-committee-found-no-conclusive-proof-of-use-of-pegasus-software-against-dissidents-supreme-court#:~:text=Justice%20R.V.-,Raveendran%20committee%20found%20no%20conclusive%20proof%20of%20use%20of%20Pegasus,submitted%20to%20the%20Court%20today.) and thus couldn't pronounce the Indian government as guilty.

(Saudi-based satirist was also hacked with pegasus and [won](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/26/saudi-satirist-hacked-with-pegasus-spyware-wins-damages-in-court-battle/) the damages from the court in 2026)

2022: A [petition](https://sflc.in/legal-challenge-cpil-and-sflcin-surveillance-projects-cms-natgrid-and-netra/) was filed in the Supreme Court challenging the state surveillance programs like [Centralised Monitoring System](https://cis-india.org/internet-governance/blog/india-central-monitoring-system-something-to-worry-about) (CMS), the [Network Traffic Analysis](https://cis-india.org/internet-governance/blog/the-design-technology-behind-india2019s-surveillance-programmes) (NETRA) and the [National Intelligence Grid](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/national-intelligence-grid-to-finally-see-light-of-day/article36414741.ece) (NATGRID) by Centre for Public Interest Litigation (CPIL) and Software Freedom Law Centre (SFLC)

2025: In its [Demands for Grants (2025-26) report,](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1o-05j0UMmwOhh9y1GJDgs9xd4zwp8rZX/view?usp=sharing) the Ministry of External Affairs said PRIDE, which serves as the repository of all passport-related data, has integrated the Passport Seva Programme with the NATGRID and Bureau of Immigration, but integration with the MHA’s CCTNS database remained in-progress.

2025: Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI)'s draft National Telecom Policy 2025 [proposed](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-draft-telecom-policy-2025-satcom-entry-security/) monitoring satellite communications to ensure cybersecurity, data integrity, and, ironically, protection against unauthorised access and interference.

2025 Dec:  the GoI is the plans to [publish](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/indias-first-anti-terror-policy-to-be-released-soon/article70430070.ece) anti terror policy of action to curb the digital radicalisation.
