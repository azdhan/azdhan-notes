# Australia to propose a law to turn off algorithmic recommendations on social media platforms

 The Australian government is preparing draft laws that would force social media platforms to give users the power to switch off the algorithmic recommendation systems that control what appears in their feeds, as part of a new digital duty of care framework. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese is expected to promote the digital duty of care plan at the United Nations General Assembly in New York later this month, as per [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2026/sep/06/labor-plans-crackdown-on-social-media-algorithms-as-taylor-says-hes-deeply-sceptical) report. 

Non-algorithmic chronological feeds: Under the proposed model, users who exercise the opt-out would see their feeds revert to chronological content only from friends, family, and groups/pages that they have chosen to follow.

Why? The move is aimed at curbing the spread of content that reinforces harmful behaviors around body image, women's safety, and child protection, areas where algorithmic amplification has been identified as a contributing factor.

Additional monitoring obligations on platforms: In addition to the algorithm opt-out function, the laws would require platforms to better identify and act on problematic or illegal content to protect vulnerable users, including children.

“I’m deeply skeptical. I fear that this is just going to be an attempt by the government to censor social media…. I fear, given Labor and the minister’s past biases, that this will just be an attempt to censor the internet, and that’s not what we should be doing,” said Angus Taylor, opposition member, in an interview with The Guardian. 

What comes next: The draft legislation is expected to be released as Parliament resumes this week. After placing the bill, the government may conduct public consultation and seek comments before any bill is formally introduced or enacted. 

In a separate amendment introduced to Parliament, the Online Safety Amendment (Strengthening Enforcement for the Social Media Minimum Age) Bill 2026 [[pdf](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/download/legislation/bills/r7512_first-reps/toc_pdf/26090b01.PDF;fileType=application%2Fpdf#search=%22legislation/bills/r7512_first-reps/0000%22)], would double civil penalties for noncompliant platform providers from 30,000 to 60,000 penalty units and expand the commissioner's information-gathering powers. This lets the commissioner go beyond the platform itself and compel third parties such as age verification vendors, contractors, and others involved in a platform's age-assurance processes.  

Why it matters: After [Meta temporarily blocked PM Modi’s reel](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-censorship-tracker-live-list-jantar-mantar-protests-new-delhi/), when Meta was [summoned](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-india-summons-meta-global-affairs-chief/), it was also questioned for [algorithmic bias](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/government-to-question-meta-global-team-on-issues-around-algorithmic-bias-role-in-public-order/article71290530.ece/amp/) too. Similarly, in February 2025, French prosecutors opened an [investigation](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/02/07/elon-musks-x-faces-criminal-probe-in-france-over-algorithm-concerns.html) into X over allegations its algorithms distorted public discourse. Algorithmic transparency is one of the major concerns in the platform regulation as it has real-world consequences, including misinformation or child harms. 

For instance, before [Elon Musk acquired X](https://www.medianama.com/2022/10/223-tesla-ceo-elon-musk-will-buy-twitter-44-billion/), a 2021 Twitter study ([pdf](https://cdn.cms-twdigitalassets.com/content/dam/blog-twitter/official/en_us/company/2021/rml/Algorithmic-Amplification-of-Politics-on-Twitter.pdf)) found right-leaning content received greater amplification across most countries examined. 

In Feb 2026, [Meta has launched "Dear Algo,"](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-meta-threads-dear-algo-feature-to-personalise-algorithms/) an AI-powered feature on Threads that lets users directly influence their feed by publishing a post starting with "Dear Algo" followed by what they want to see more or less of. The platform's AI systems interpret the request and adjust the user's feed for three days. When it was launched, the feature had rolled out in the US, UK, Australia, and New Zealand, with plans to expand to more countries. In testing, MediaNama [found](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-meta-threads-dear-algo-feature-to-personalise-algorithms/) the feature initially worked as intended but subsequently began surfacing unrelated posts in Tagalog for a UK-based user.

In a similar attempt to give aglo-flexibility to the users, Elon Musk has [announced](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-x-elon-musk-ai-driven-feed-user-prompts/) that [X's algorithm will become "purely AI"](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-x-elon-musk-ai-driven-feed-user-prompts/) by November or December 2025. Users will be able to adjust their feeds dynamically by prompting Grok, the platform's AI chatbot. When MediaNama queried Grok bot to regarding this feature, it cautioned:

“Results aren’t always instantaneous or perfect (the system still balances many signals, and availability of full natural-language control can vary), but asking Grok is now one of the most direct ways to steer your personal algorithm. Be specific for the best effect,” [said](https://grok.com/share/c2hhcmQtMw_8fb1c813-017d-4438-937d-90ed005e0652) the Grok chatbot.

Why chronological feeds are bad business for social media platforms: A multimillion-dollar Meta-backed study, [published in the journal Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abp9364), ran from September to December 2020 and served reverse chronological feeds to approximately 7,200 US adults on Facebook and 8,800 on Instagram, instead of the standard algorithmically ranked feeds. The key findings of the study are: 

Users on chronological feeds got bored faster and left for rival platforms: Instagram app users spent nearly eight hours less on Instagram over three months, increasing their time on TikTok by 36 percent and YouTube by 20 percent.

The findings line up with at least two earlier internal Facebook experiments from 2014 and 2018, which also found that chronological feeds drove users away. You can read [TechDirt’s report](https://www.techdirt.com/2021/10/27/when-facebook-turned-off-news-feed-algorithm-it-made-everyones-experience-worse-made-facebook-more-money/) based on Facebook’s internal documents [leaked](https://bigtechnology.substack.com/p/facebook-removed-the-news-feed-algorithm?s=09) by whistleblower Frances Haugen. 

The Meta-backed study also stated that switching to a chronological feed did not meaningfully change users' political knowledge, attitudes, or behavior, including whether they attended a protest or voted.
