# CoinDCX admits $16M USDT loss days after its CEO denied the breach

After [denying](https://x.com/smtgpt/status/2113181173480607981) a breach on October 22, three days later, CoinDCX, an India-based cryptocurrency exchange, [confirmed](https://coindcx.com/blog/coindcx-news/on-chain-wallet-activity-update-21-oct-2026) a loss of 15.99 million USDT from a depreciated wallet from the previous similar cyber attack. 

The Mumbai-based crypto exchnage platform later admitted that their automated sweeping service failed for a “particular period” that resulted because of “unknown vulnerabilities” in their  “old, deprecated wallet infrastructure.” Automated sweeping is a process used by the company to transfer the funds from the deprecated address into the primary and secure CoinDCX wallet.

The current breach is ~2.4x than the previous breach: At the time of writing this report, the standard rate of one UDST is Rs. 95.07, according to [Binance's figures](https://www.binance.com/en-IN/price/tether/INR). This would amount to Rs. 152.02 crore. This is not the first time the crypto exchange has lost money due to cyber attacks. In July 2025, the Mumbai-based crypto exchange faced  the $44.2 million breach, which [MediaNama reported](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-how-india-first-crypto-unicorn-coindcx-lost-368-crore/) cost the company about Rs. 368 crore, which is 2.42 times more than the previous breach.

The initial denial of the attack: Before the company publicly admitted the breach, Darcy Niu disclosed the breach. He is the Vice Precident of Global Law Enforcement Cooperation at Salus, a cybersecurity research team on web 3.0, and Co-Founder of FlashRescue, a digital-asset investigations company that can investigate crypto frauds or wallet thefts. Responding to this, CoinDCX’s CEO had called it a ["false alarm"](https://x.com/smtgpt/status/2113181173480607981) and asked people not to spread rumours. As [MediaNama reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-cbfc-data-breach-censor-cuts-distribution/) in its October roundup of Indian breaches, the consistent pattern of organisations is either to first deny or minimise/narrow the scope of attack/breach.  

CoinDCX's co-founder and chief executive, Sumit Gupta, replied that "CoinDCX wallets are not breached" and asked people not to spread rumours. He argued the addresses had been compromised in 2025 and that attackers were reusing them. The exchange's [X account](https://x.com/CoinDCX/status/2113182200648864243) said the flagged addresses "have not been used by CoinDCX since" and called the breach claims as misleading. 

[https://x.com/smtgpt/status/2113181173480607981](https://x.com/smtgpt/status/2113181173480607981) 

The company said that it had noticed an unusual activity in their systems and followed up with a  deeper investigation following the community reports. 

“However, the next day we noticed irregular fund movement via the blockchain data and through reports from our user community. A deeper investigation revealed that the unusual activity had impacted our old, deprecated wallet infrastructure which had unknown vulnerabilities. The investigation also revealed that our automated sweeping service had failed for a particular period, causing deposits to accumulate in the deprecated address. This led to a loss of 15.99 million USDT which was absorbed by the company.” – [CoinDCX](https://coindcx.com/blog/coindcx-news/on-chain-wallet-activity-update-21-oct-2026).
