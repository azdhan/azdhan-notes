# Supreme Court seeks MeitY’s action taken report on CSAM

The Supreme Court of India (SC) has given a final deadline to Indian government to file their response on what action and precaution the Ministry of Electronics, Information and Technology (MeitY) and Ministry of Law and Justice (MLJ) has taken in response to the issue of Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) on online and social media platforms, according to the court proceedings reported by [Bar and Bench](https://x.com/barandbench/status/2103013424099983854?s=20). SC also asked the Minsitries to submit how they intent to prevent the lapses regarding the spread of CSAM content on social media.

"We grant one last opportunity to both the ministries to file that counter/ report. The counter/report should explain clearly what steps the two ministries have taken to givemeaningful effect, to the directions issued by this court in the main judgement, and what steps have been taken so far to curb this menace [CSAM]...The counter should also indicate, in so many words, what precautions the government would be taking in future to prevent lapses on the part of social media intermediaries" -- [SC](https://x.com/barandbench/status/2103013424099983854?s=20)

The SC will hear the case again on October 15. 

**SC's notice came after** [](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-google-child-abuse-reporting-india/)**[Google](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-google-child-abuse-reporting-india/)** **and** [](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-meta-child-abuse-cases-indian-police/)**[Meta](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-meta-child-abuse-cases-indian-police/)** **has said that they will now report the Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM).** Under the current CSAM reporting process, social media intermediaries can report the CSAM incidents on their platforms to US-based non-profit National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC). Then, NCMEC can forwards such reports to relevant law enforcement agencies, including those in other countries.

**When Madras HC said just downloading of CSAM is not a crime:** In January, 2024, the Madras High Court [ordered](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2024/01/17/merely-watching-child-porn-not-an-offence-under-pocso-act-or-s-67b-it-act-madras-high-court/) that just watching or downloading child pornography does not make out an offence under [Section 67(B) of IT Act](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/176300164/), criminalizes publishing, transmitting, browsing, downloading, or collecting CSAM. This case involves a man storing CSAM content in this mobile phone. 

  

“To make out an offence under Section 14(1) of Protection of Child from Sexual Offences Act, 2012, a child or children must have been used for pornography purposes. This would mean that the accused person should have used the child for pornographic purposes. Even assuming that the accused person had watched child pornography video, that strictly will not fall within the scope of Section 14(1) of Protection of Child from Sexual Offences Act, 2012. Since he has not used a child or children for pornographic purposes, at the best, it can only be construed as a moral decay on the part of the accused person.” – [Madras HC (2024)](https://www.verdictum.in/pdf_upload/2024-mhc-5769-1578897.pdf)

The accused later told court that he’d seek counselling for his porn addiction.

**Supreme Court sets aside Madras HC order:** Later, this case was heard in SC. In September 2024, the SC said that the Madras HC has “committed an egregious error” in passing the judgement and it had set aside the Madras HC order. 

Then, the SC ordered to send a copy of the order to Principal Secretarties of Union Ministry of Law & Justice and Ministry of Women and Child Development. 

**What Supreme Court has said about intermediary responsibility in CSAM content?** In 2024, the SC said that intermediaries are required to report SCAM material to local authorities and concerned police officials under the  Protection of Children from Sexual Offences [(POCSO, 2012) Act,](https://ncpcr.gov.in/public/viewPdf/17829716116a45fcdbe84e2_1764750002692ff2b28d2b7_pocso-act.pdf) and [Rules (2020):](https://ncpcr.gov.in/public/viewPdf/165648753162bbfe6bea3ba_pocso-rules-merged_2.pdf) 

"It has been brought to the notice of this Court that social media intermediaries do not report such cases of child abuse and exploitation to the local authorities specified under POCSO and rather only comply with the requirements stipulated in the MOU. In view of the salutary object and the mandatory character of the provisions of Sections 19 and 20 of the POCSO read with Rule 11 of the POCSO Rules, we are of the considered view, that an intermediary cannot claim exemption from the liability under Section 79 of the IT Act for any third-party information, data, or communication link made available or hosted by it, unless due diligence is conducted by it and compliance is made of these provisions of the POCSO. We are also of the view that such due diligence includes not only removal of child pornographic content but also making an immediate report of such content to the concerned police units in the manner specified under the POCSO Act and the Rules thereunder." -- [Supreme Court (2024)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/37078038/)

**Why this matters:** It is not yet clear if the MeitY or any other Ministry or any law enforcement agency has mandated Meta and Google to report CSAM incidents to Indian authorities directly. However, in July 2026, after the [BBC investigation](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cvgm4e0316zo) that [reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-bbc-investigation-meity-summon-meta-instagram-ads-promoting-child-sexual-abuse-material/) the CSAM content on Meta ads, MeitY summoned Meta's officials. Later day, the Indian government has reportedly sent a “stern notice” ordering Meta to disable all CSAM-related ads and asked the company to submit a detailed explanation within seven days.  

Similarly, in July, according to the report by Money Control, MediaNama [reported](https://www.medianama.com/2025/05/223-social-media-sites-sahyog-portal-report-csam/) that Home Affairs Ministry’s Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) has instructed social media platforms to integrate with the Sahyog Portal for reporting of child sexual abuse material (CSAM).

So, when the SC hears this case again on October 15, MeitY may submit its action taken and precautionary plan of action report. Similar to how [public became aware of the reasoning behind the temporary ban on Telegram](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-home-ministry-cybercrime-report-on-telegram/), the public can know what action MeitY or other Ministries have taken to address the issue of CSAM.
