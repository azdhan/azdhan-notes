**# Meity says AI safe arbour depends on the platform's 'nature and function.' Here's what we think

# Parliament question asked if AI Platforms get safe harbour. Here's what MeitY said and what we think

The safe harbor protections under the IT Act for AI systems "depends upon the nature of the service provided, the functions performed by such system or service" and the provisions of IT Act and Rules, read Union Minister of State for Electronics & Information Technology, Jitin Prasada's reply to a question by MP Manish Tiwari on July 29, 2026.

The IT Act is a “technology-neutral legislation,” he added. “Its provisions apply to computer resources and intermediaries… irrespective of the underlying technology used, including AI,” read his response. 

MediawNama's Take: 

According to IT Act's definition of the Intermediary, it is supposed to handle someone else's data as an ‘intermediary’. Here is the full definition of the intermediary under the IT Act: "'intermediary', with respect to any particular electronic records, means any person who on behalf of another person receives, stores or transmits that record or provides any service with respect to that record and includes telecom service providers, network service providers, internet service providers, web-hosting service providers, search engines, online payment sites, online-auction sites, online-market places and cyber cafes;" reads the intermediary's definition verbatim under the  [Section 2(1) of](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1752240/) the Information Technology Act, 2000. 

In the case of most of the AI platforms, the AI platforms don't just record/hold/transmit users' data unlike the traditional intermediaries. These AI systems also generate their own data/responses. So, should an AI system still be considered an intermediary and therefore enabling the safe harbor protections under the IT act?  

Moreover, Safe harbor protection is not a legally guarenteed right just because a platform is an intermediary by its function. There are a few conditions under the [IT Act's Section 79 2(b).](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/show-data?actid=AC_CEN_45_76_00001_200021_1517807324077&orderno=105) Platforms are counted as intermediaries if they doesn't:

- “initiate the transmission,
    

- select the receiver of the transmission, and
    
- select or modify the information contained in the transmission;”
    
- observes due diligence under IT Act and other guidelines
    

In the case of AI platforms, Generative AI, by design, selects and generates the information itself. This might not disqualify generative AI from 79(2) safe harbour protections regardless of how widely the IT Act's intermediary definition is interpreted.

Therefore, Prasada's response saying that the AI platform's safe harbor protections depends on the nature and the functions of the AI platforms doesn't give either a referrential or decisional criteria to determine the safe harbor status of AI platforms broadly. 

What were the questions asked by Manish Tiwari? 

1. Has the government studied how the 2026 IT Rules amendment applies to AI systems, including generative AI? 
    
2. Do generative AI systems and chatbots count as "intermediaries" under Section 2(1)(w) of the IT Act, making them eligible for safe harbour under Section 79?
    
3. Will the government issue guidelines distinguishing AI that merely surfaces third-party content from AI that generates its own content, for the purpose of deciding liability?
    
4. If so, what would those guidelines say?
    
5. Is the government planning a dedicated law to govern AI liability?
    

What were the answers given by Jitin Prasada? 

The Minister didn't answer the above-mentioned itemised questions in an itemised manner. His long response didn't address the questions directly. However, here is what he said regarding each question: 

1. The minister avoided directly answering whether the Indian government has studied how the IT Act will apply for generative AI platforms. in a Yes-or-no format. He added that the IT Act is a "technology-neutral" and applies to computer resources and intermediaries "irrespective of the underlying technology used, including AI."
    

2. As mentioned above, addressing the status of intermediary liability for AI platforms, he said it depends on "nature of the service provided, the functions performed."
    
3. On issuing guidelines for distinguishing AI systems that rely on third-party content and generating their own information, Prasada avoided answering it directy and explained the general due-diligence framework under the IT Rules, 2021, and the SGI amendments notified on February 10 2026. 
    
4. He didn’t address it directly. 
    
5. On a dedicated AI law, while MeitY is in the works of preparing a dedicated AI law, Prasada hasn't referred to the draft bill. He instead referred to November 2025's [India AI Governance Guidelines](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-meity-ai-governance-liability-regulation-innovation/) as a road map for the possible future laws. He added that any future "legislative or regulatory measures, if considered necessary, are processed in accordance with the extant policy and established legislative procedures"
