Today is the second day of [internet shutdown](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-internet-shutdown-delhi-jantar-mantar-protest-march/) at New Delhi's Jantar Mantar region and the third day of peaceful protest and Sonam Wangchunk's indefinite hunger strike that unfortunately turned into violent protests after the Delhi Police and RPF unleashed police brutality on the civilians, students and protestors. 

Here is the list of all digital developments from the Jantar Mantar protest site: 

**Telecom companies cut off the internet access at the Jantar Mantar protest site** at least since yesterday (July 21) night from 8:30 pm. MediaNama's reporter Prabhanu Kumar Das confirmed this through individuals present at the protest site. 

However, internet seems to be accessible out side Jantar Mantar region. MediaNama's subeditor, Rohit Singh, got it confirmed from an individual working at the Patel Chowk, which is about 700 meters to 1 kilometer, which away from the main protest site. 

No one in the government or at the telecom companies made the blocking order public. We don't know how long this internet shutdown will last. On the first day of internet shutdown (July 20), the access to internet was blocked at around 10:45 AM and later restored at the around 10 pm. We are not sure exactly when the internet access was restored. 

Food delivery and quick commerce stopped delivering the food at the protest sites. MediaNama has checked the Zomato and Zomato-owned Blinkit; Swiggy and its Instamart and Zepto. All these platforms are not delivering the services at the protest centre. 

MediaNama sent a list of questions to these companies asking why the delivery services has been stopped despite of there not being a ban on the mobility via roads in that area. However, Delhi's metro has [shutdown](https://x.com/ANI/status/2080105993086898536?s=20) 16 of its stations.

As there is an internet shutdown, individuals have begun using mesh messaging applications that doesn't rely on the internet for communication. For instance, Briar, Bitchat and MediaNama's Editor Nikhil Pahwa's [vibe-coded Messy ](https://x.com/nixxin/status/2079430975260688469?s=20)are few examples of applications that can enable communication among peers in a certain geographic limits connected via either bluetooth or hotspot. Interception by police or the state into these networks and communications is not possible, especially if the texts are protected by end-to-end-encryption. 

On July 22, Times of India's Raj Shekhar Jha [reported](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/delhi/bluetooth-messaging-apps-outsiders-people-from-other-states-under-radar/articleshow/132545043.cms) that Delhi police is 'investigating Bluetooth messaging apps used' at the protest site.  Responding to this, Pahwa [wrote](https://x.com/nixxin/status/2079842679689720274?s=20), "lol. what a bunch of idiots in Delhi Police. If the apps dont collect data, have no server linkage with the provider, may be open sourced and available to fork, what exactly will you probe? the source code on github?"

**A brief context:** Climate activist and educator Sonam Wangchuk was [forcefully removed](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/18/world/asia/sonam-wangchuk-hunger-strike-delhi-police.html) from Delhi’s Jantar Mantar by the Delhi Police on Day 21 of his indefinite hunger strike and taken to Safdarjung Hospital. The action followed a [Delhi High Court order](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showlogo/1784189116_0a7a8c304d45dcf9_683_94982026.pdf/2026) to monitor his health and it later [refused](https://lawbeat.in/amp/news-updates/sonam-wangchuk-protest-delhi-hc-refuses-urgent-hearing-on-pil-challenging-police-action-1613877) to listen to a Public Interest Litigation filed against illegal detention of the activist.

The Jantar Mantar protests initiated by the Cockroach Janata Party (CJP), a youth-led collective, originally demanded accountability and resignation of Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan after NEET examination paper leaks. Earlier last month, the government [blocked access](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-is-telegram-restriction-india-necessary-disproportionate/) to the Telegram app until the June 22 NEET re-exam happened.
