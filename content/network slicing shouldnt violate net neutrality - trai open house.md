**# Network Slicing should not dilute Net Neutrality: stakeholders at TRAI open house discussion 

“We believe that this [Netwok Slicing], if allowed, will be a violation of the net neutrality guidelines, which prevent paid prioritisation on the basis of selected content,” said Broadband India Forum (BIF) at Telecom Authority of India’s (TRAI)  open house discussion on its [recent consultation](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-trai-5g-network-slicing-quality-of-service/) on network slicing and quality of service in 5G networks.

The Broadband India Forum (BIF), the Consumer Care Society and MediaNama founder and editor Nikhil Pahwa told the TRAI that 5G network slicing should not be permitted at the cost of net neutrality and inferior quality to the general consumer. Meanwhile, Airtel and Reliance Jio pushed back on TRAI’s proposed 21-day advance notice for new slices and its 80% Physical Resource Block (PRB) utilisation rule. Here are a few important statements from the discussion, particularly related to network slicing and net neutraity:

1. Slicing can coexist with net neutrality, but not application-specific quality on demand: BIF
    

Debashish Bhattacharya, Additional DG at BIF, said network slicing is an important technological advancement, and that it can coexist with net neutrality only under certain conditions.

“Network slicing, in our view, can coexist with the net neutrality framework, provided its deployment is transparent and non-discriminatory, and does not adversely affect the quality or availability of general internet services for ordinary users.” – BIF 

Responding to stakeholder views that TRAI should expressly recognise network slicing and quality on demand for applications such as live video streaming, cloud gaming, live broadcasting and enterprise applications, Bhattacharya said these views “are not in consonance with the core principles of net neutrality.” He explained:

“To suggest that different apps, sites or URLs can be given differential speeds and terms of access by a telecom service provider implies basically three things.

One, a telecom service provider can offer an OTT platform guaranteed bandwidth, lower latency or fewer interruptions for live broadcasting — such as a cricket match or a music show — or other premium content, to some consumers who are in the fast lane. This amounts to discrimination by content.

Two, to be able to do this, the telecom operator could possibly charge the OTT platform for the special treatment granted to that particular service or application. In such a case, this begins to resemble paid prioritisation of selected content.

And the corollary is that a platform that does not pay such a charge could remain in the ordinary internet lane and therefore perform less well during congestion.”

He further added:

“This content discrimination is not allowed under the current net neutrality rules. In view of the clear reasons and rationale we have provided, we believe that this [Network Slicing], if allowed, will be a violation of the net neutrality guidelines, which prevent paid prioritisation on the basis of selected content.”

BIF also pointed that in other countries the network slicing is followed with strict net neutrality protections:

“We would also like to point out that even where quality on demand within 5G network slicing has been implemented in other regimes, like Europe, it is permitted within strict net neutrality safeguards. Broadcasters, regulators and streaming services have opposed any arrangement that allows telecom operators to charge content providers, prioritise selected services, or degrade ordinary internet access. Unfortunately, the submission made by the stakeholder concerned, despite mentioning such EU-style use cases, does not acknowledge the accompanying safeguards that have characterised the EU regulations on quality on demand.”

BIF also asked for clarification of the definition of TRAI’s proposed disclosrues of ‘typical speed’: “In the absence of clarity on what typical speed means or implies, it would be difficult for the consumer to visualise the speed he would get from that particular tariff plan. Therefore, we feel there is a need to lay down an unambiguous definition of what typical speed means, to avoid multiple interpretations,” said BIF’s Bhattacharya. 

He further said that it should have factors like time of day, peak hours and location with respect to the cell site, so that service providers can also comply with the regulations if these parameters are clearly and explicitly defined.”

2. Slicing should not dilute net neutrality: Consumer Care Society
    

Gopal Ratnam, Secretary of the Consumer Care Society, said the group takes the issue “at face value” and believes there is “no hidden agenda here on TRAI's part of introducing something contrary to net neutrality.” 

However he further clarified his stance: “We very clearly want that network slicing should not dilute net neutrality. Differentiation should be based on objectively defined parameters. Having worked with businesses, I do understand that some differentiation of certain services may be essential for TSPs — we acknowledge that. But it should not be preferential treatment of a particular application, content provider or commercial partner.”

“There should be no discrimination, and the TSP should not block, throttle, degrade or favour any competing application.” – Consumer Care Society

3. Emergency services could get better services through network slicing, but general consumers shouldn’t be affected: Dillipkumar Jena

Dillipkumar Jena, who identified himself as being from the Rajkot Jilla Grahak Suraksha Mandal, a consumer guidance and welfare organization located in Gujarat, spoke in Hindi. In the transcript’s English translation, he said:

“The network slicing proposal is very good, but care should be taken that general customers — ordinary consumers and rural consumers — are not adversely affected. Certain categories, such as senior citizens and emergency services, could get better service, but it should be ensured that general customers do not face a lot of trouble with their service.”

What Telecom operators have said about network slicing and network capacity: 

Reliance Jio’s U.K. Srivastava: “From RJIL, I would like to say that we are fully cognisant of the requirements of the regulation, and we follow the regulation at all times while offering our services.”

Airtel’s Rahul Vatts: He said that “slicing is a specific 5G architectural capability, which involves logical network instances with defined resources and performance characteristics. So slicing-specific regulatory obligations should be considered only when a genuine 5G network slice has been created and managed”. Then a TRAI official interrupted him, asking him to keep his remarks to the amendments under discussion at that point.

Later in the session, Airtel’s vice president of regulatory affairs, Tarun Chitkara said:

“We recognise that TRAI, being the telecom regulator, should be aware of who is launching network slicing and what impact it has on the consumer. This is a fair ask. Our only request is that it should relate only to the consumer, to anything that has a direct impact on consumers.”

He was referring to the technical measure of PRB utilisation. TRAI’s proposal mandates that it should not cross the 80% capacity. “You might have 80% utilisation and still continue to offer better, or normal, quality of service to the customer. Whether you are at 80% or 85%, you can ensure there is no direct impact on the consumer side. But if you bring this into the regulation, you are importing an internal, inherent percentage into a regime where operators will be bound to undertake capacity enhancement, even where, based on the traffic trend, they believe it is not required right now. So we believe it is unnecessary. Since it has no consumer impact, this requirement should be reconsidered,” reasoned Airtel’s Chitkara.

Jio’s UK Srivastava also argued that “prescribing a fixed PRB utilisation threshold such as 80% as a mandatory regulatory trigger for suspension or deactivation of a network slice would be disproportionate and technically inappropriate, and would also impact customers being served through such a slice. PRB utilisation is an internal radio resource management parameter, and by itself cannot be treated as a determinative indicator of service degradation or customer experience.”

MediaNama’s founder and editor Nikhil Pahwa also made his submission at the TRAI open house discussion. Here is a brief excerpt from his submission: 

“So, at this point in time, the fact that you [TRAI] are looking at network slicing — and, from what I have heard, even considering a consultation paper on net neutrality — is a serious cause for concern: first, that you are considering it, and second, that the DoT has asked you to look at it again. This would effectively upend India’s digital economy as it functions right now. Telecom operators should not shape consumer experience or consumer choice by treating different services differently. That is why service-specific, sector-specific and content-specific network slices should not be allowed. As a country, we rejected the idea of allowing telecom operators to play gatekeepers instead of gateways. If you are planning to reverse that, it needs transparency and accountability from TRAI, including about its engagement with telecom operators.” – Nikhil Pahwa
