# AI could be used for live market surveillance, but no tools in market: National Stock Exchange

"The live market surveillance is the area where AI has a lot of room to play, and still there are no tools in the market," said Sanjay Shorey, Executive Director of National Stock Exchange of India (NSE), at Global Fintech Fest (GFF 2026) on September 10. He also said that AI is already being implemented in market investigations. He made these statements at a panel discussion [titled](https://www.globalfintechfest.com/agenda) "AI in Capital Markets: From Trading to Market Surveillance."  

Can live AI market surveillance be used to detect and cancel illegal trade before the market closes? "Today, surveillance is a post-activity. It happens after the market is closed. But if you do it post-trade, can AI do it almost in real-time after the trade? And can you actually, as a plan B, cancel that trade before the market closes? That is a possibility," wondered Ishan Bansal, Co-founder and CFO, Groww. He also stated that techniques like reinforcement learning can be used to reduce the repetition of errors.

Current rule-based investigations lack market view, says BSE: "Whenever you look at any investigation, you pick up a particular transaction and go after the transaction and find out what has gone wrong. There is no market view. There is no relationship built on what is the information is which actually triggered the action," said  Gopalan S Raghavan, Executive Director, BSE. "SEBI is going to have the view of the entire market and also see exactly where it all touched and what all happened with respect to that industry," he later added. 

On the limits of rule-based surveillance and what AI-based surveillance can do: "We are going to have to not just look at the transaction but also map it to what triggered that transaction. Who triggered that transaction? Where is the information? Who was involved in that information? When it was discussed, how that information passed on… which are the trading clients, which are the members, and what is the pattern that is emanating in the market," said Gopalan S. Raghavan, Executive Director, BSE. 

SEBI is also starting to use AI for investigation and surveillance processes, says its ED: "The rule-based alerts are there, but now we have patterns. How abnormal patterns are coming up because of AI. Though AI is in a nascent stage, we have been seeing a lot of things happening on the trading side. We have been working on that, how to come around with it and see people do not misuse the system," stated Securities and Exchange Board of India's (SEBI) Executive Director, Aliasgar Mithwani. He moderated the discussion. However, he also reminded that "human intellect is very important" and AI has to be an "assistant, not a leader."

A co-owned sandboxed environment: "It is time for us to create a sandbox which can be co-owned by all players, owned by SEBI, by frontline regulators, and by marketplace, internet, and technology companies. All of us can come together and create this infrastructure that can help us test the products that we are going to be doing. And since it is going to be co-owned by regulators, we can also supervise what's going on there. So what happens is, this becomes a very responsible way of building the infrastructure, and it can also help us build the regulation," said BSE's Raghavan.

"When we saw an incident where the AI was in a weird mood and it entered somewhere and created havoc, we cannot afford that thing in the market where you have crores and crores at stake," stated SEBI’s ED elsewhere in the discussion.

The delays in deepfake detection and verification could impact markets: Mithwani stated that because of AI deepfakes, especially if the users/investors realize that they are false and deepfake, the market could go down.  "What I understand is AI tools all would be moving in the same direction, and it would squeeze the liquidity by the time the information is verified and designated to be persons," said Shorey. 

"[Transparency] can be extended to the media platforms too, which I think some of the social media platforms have already started doing. Wherever there is a AI video, there is a tag that says 'AI,' which gives transparency, I think, that gives some confidence to the customer," stated Bansal.
