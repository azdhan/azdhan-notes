**# GAC ordered to block Youtuber Dhruv Rathe’s video, Google tells Delhi HC

In its submissions to Delhi High Court, Google said that it blocked YouTuber Dhruv Rathe's video in India complying with the Grievance Appellate Committee (GAC) order, according to the live tweets posted by Bar and Bench. View the full thread [here](https://x.com/barandbench/status/2087074599007891639). 

What is the blocked video: "Can Hindus Eat BEEF? | Kerala Story 2 EXPOSED | Dhruv Rathee"  --  [ [URL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rygbPO6hM9Y) ] (Accessible via VPN) 

IMAGE

When the geo-blocked video's URL is accessed without a VPN, it displays this notice: "Video unavailable. This content is not available on this country domain due to a legal complaint from the government."

Background to the case: On July 31, [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/delhi-hc-gives-centres-grievance-committee-15-days-to-decide-on-removing-dhruv-rathees-video-on-hindu-gods-eating-meat) reported that Delhi High Court ordered GAC to take a decision within 15 days on removing the concerned Dhruv Rathe's video on YouTube. Then, Justice Swarana Kanta Sharma noted that the GAC's order should be considered as the "imitation to this court" and that "any disregard to this [GAC] order will be taken note of seriously."

Delhi HC said this after advocate Amita Sachdeva filed a petition against Rathe's video. Sachdeva claimed that in the vidoe Rathe falsely stated that Ram, Seetha and Krishna consumed meat and alcohol. She said that the video is "highly derogatory, inflammatory and communally sensitive."

What is a  Grievance Appellate Committee (GAC)? It is a centeral government-appointed online-only appellate body established by MeitY under the [Rule 3A of IT Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/550681ab908f8afb135b0ad42816a1c9.pdf) to counter the decisions taken by the concerned intermediary's Grievance Officer and if intermediaries fail to follow due diligence. 

If platforms fail to comply with the GAC orders, intermediaries could lose their safe harbor protections and be liable for penalties. 

A few more points on GAC: 

- Anyone can raise a complaint with GAC if: 
    

- General complaints: If the grievance is not resolved within the time specified in Rule 3(2)(a)(i), which deals with the general complaints and obligates the intermediaries to respond within 7 days. If the concern is regarding a specific content removal request, then the intermediary should respond within 36 hours.
    
- Intimate images or impersonation: If the grievance is not resolved under Rule 3(2)(b) within 2 hours when the concerned content is dealing with intimate imagery and impersonation. 
    

- All the complaints raised at GAC must be resolved within 30 days. 
    
- GAC can seek assistance from qualified experts if needed.
    
- GAC doesn't deal with the news and publishers, which are covered under the Part-3 of the IT rules, which comes under the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting (MIB).
