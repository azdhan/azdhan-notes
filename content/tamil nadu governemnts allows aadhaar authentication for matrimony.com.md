# Tamil Nadu allows Matrimony.com to use Aadhaar authentication for user verification

The Tamil Nadu government has notified that Matrimony.com can now perform Aadhaar authentication to establish the identity of users on its platform. It states that the authentication will be voluntary, according to a notification issued by the state's Social Welfare and Women Empowerment Department on August 28, 2026. TN government notified ([pdf](https://www.stationeryprinting.tn.gov.in/gazette/2026/37_II_1_2026.pdf)) this in Tamil Nadu Government Gazette website on September 16, 2026. 

To prevent impersonation and fraud: The notification states that the authentication is meant to "enhance trust, prevent impersonation, and curb matrimony-related fraud" through a verified user indicator on user profiles. Matrimony.com will use Yes/No and/or eKYC authentication, and only for users accessing its platform.

According to the notification, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), after consulting the Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI), allowed Matrimony.com to perform authentication and to use the Aadhaar number during authentication.

Bharat Matrimony "become the first private sector company in Tamil Nadu" to receive MeitY's  approval for Aadhaar Authentication through Aadhaar, [said](https://www.instagram.com/p/DYwv8dcmNhi/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&stkn=NTc4MTIwNjQ2YQ==) the company on May 25, nearly after a month of TN Social Welfare and Women Empowerment Department’s notice.

Why this matters: Citing the similar reasons of cheating, in 2014, the Women and Child Development ministry has [reportedly](https://www.medianama.com/2014/12/223-shaadi-bharatmatrimony-aadhaar/) suggested that Aadhar should be ised to authenticate the profiles on matrimonial sites. Their reasoning was: “There are hundreds of people who register online on matrimony sites every month and there are increasing instances of women being cheated while looking for grooms. There are men who have multiple accounts in different websites.”

The TN notification also mirrors similar language. It reasons that the authentication is meant to "enhance trust and prevent fraud." 

In 2018, the Supreme Court of India has  struck down section 57 of the Aadhaar Act stating that the government cannot allow private companies to use Aadhaar-based KYC. Later, In 2025, the government has [allowed](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-private-companies-aadhaar-authentication/) private companies to carry out Aadhaar authentication for providing their services under the Aadhaar Authentication for Good Governance (Social Welfare, Innovation, Knowledge) Amendment Rules, 2025. 

Speaking to MediaNama in 2021, Prasanna S, a Delhi-based lawyer who assisted petitioners in challenging the Aadhaar Act 2016, [said](https://www.medianama.com/2021/09/223-aadhaar-for-telecoms-reintroduced/): "The logic of Supreme Court having struck down (the then) Section 57 of the Aadhaar Act 2016 is a prohibition against private commercial profiteering over Aadhaar. Trying to reintroduce it now as if the Supreme Court judgment didn’t exist (even assuming it is done under the amended Act, which is also under challenge), shows scant respect for the court." 

In the case of Tamil Nadu government's notice, the notification names  only one company, Matrimony.com Ltd, as the entity permitted to perform Aadhaar authentication under it. Interestingly the [privacy policy of Bharath Matrimony](https://www.bharatmatrimony.com/privacy.php), [JeevanSaathi](https://www.jeevansathi.com/privacy-policy) and [Shaadi.com](https://www.shaadi.com/info/privacy) doesn't state anything about Aadhaar authentication.

However, usage of Aadhaar authentication for matrimony purposes has been happening since over a decade, including in matrimonial sites. For instance, LoveVivah, which [appears](https://www.instagram.com/lovevivah/) to be not active, explicitly [states](https://www.lovevivah.com/privacy-policy) that it uses Aadhaar authentication since 2016 itself. "From 18th November, 2016, [LoveVivah.com has implemented Aadhaar authentication](https://www.medianama.com/2016/11/223-lovevivah-aadhaar-signup/) of profiles through UIDAI in real-time and hence all the new Users/Members are required to furnish their Aadhaar details. For those who do not have Aadhaar card details, they have to upload scan copy of PAN card/Passport/driving license to verify their profile and for accessing complete services of LoveVivah.com"

LoveVivah made Aadhar linking voluntary-but-mandatory: One of the concerns of implementing Aadhaar authentication is to a situation where the Aadhaar is voluntary on paper but the systemic design makes it difficult/impossible to access certain functions or use cases without Aadhaar. 

The company [says](https://www.lovevivah.com/privacy-policy): "For all paid members, linking Aadhaar number or other verification document is mandatory in order to access the contact details of other users. Without linking Aadhaar or other valid documents, even a paid user will not be eligible to gain access to contact details of other users on LoveVivah."

What are the alternatives to Aadhaar authentication: The Tamil Nadu notification also lists three alternatives: 

- driving licence
    
- passport; and
    
- Permanent Account Number (PAN) card
    

What are the [Matrimony.com](http://matrimony.com)’s obligations: Under the notification, Matrimony.com must:

- obtain the consent of the Aadhaar number holder for authentication;
    
- inform the holder of alternate and viable means of identification other than Aadhaar; and
    
- not deny any service to a holder who refuses to, or is unable to, undergo Aadhaar authentication.
    

These obligations are under the Authentication for Good Governance (Social Welfare, Innovation, Knowledge) Rules, 2020. 

‘An entity’ can perform Aadhaar authentication in the “interest of the state:” The Section 4(4) of the Aadhaar Act lets “an entity” perform Aadhaar authentication if UIDAI is satisfied that the entity meets the privacy and security standards set by regulations, and that it can either permit the authentication if the purpose of authentication is in the “interest of the state” after Central government’s permission, which will consult with the UIDAI. The Act also permits to offer authentication services under another law made by Parliament. 

The Act defines authentication as the process in which an Aadhaar number, along with demographic or biometric information, is sent to the Central Identities Data Repository (CIDR). The CIDR checks whether the information is correct against what it holds. UIDAI replies with just a positive/yes, negative/no or other appropriate response. The reply does not include core biometric information.

What the Rules say: Under rule 3(1) of The Aadhaar Authentication for Good Governance (Social Welfare, Innovation, Knowledge) Rules, 2020, the Central Government may allow authentication by requesting entities for these purposes: 

- “using digital platforms to ensure good governance; 
    
- promoting ease of living of residents and enabling better access to services for them; 
    
- preventing the dissipation of social welfare benefits; and 
    
- enabling innovation and the spread of knowledge.”
    

 Rule 3(2) says authentication under the Rules is voluntary.

What Yes/No and eKYC authentication mean for offline verification: While Matrimony.com’s authentication may happen online, the Aadhaar (Authentication and Offline Verification) Regulations, 2021 describe the two methods of authentication. They are:

- Yes/No authentication: The requesting entity, like Matrimony.com, submits the user’s Aadhaar number and identity information after the consent. UIDAI matches them against Central Identities Data Repository (CIDR) data and returns a digitally signed "Yes" or "No" with technical details of the transaction, but no identity-related information is leaked.
    
- eKYC authentication: The requesting entity submits the Aadhaar number and biometric information and/or an OTP with the holder's consent. UIDAI returns a digitally signed response with eKYC data, which has full/limited demographic information and/or a photograph. This data may contain a full or masked Aadhaar number.
    

“Offline Verification” refers to  the process of “verifying the identity of the Aadhaar number holder without authentication, through such offline modes as may be specified by regulations.” It means that 

The Regulations list four authentication modes: 

- demographic, 
    
- OTP, 
    
- biometric (fingerprint, iris or other biometric modalities) and 
    
- multi-factor, which combines two or more of these. 
    

Yes/No type of authentication can use any one of the mode, while eKYC can use only OTP and/or biometric authentication (regulations 3 and 4).
