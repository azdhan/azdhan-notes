# RBI to make account aggregators interoperable: What changes for the users?

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has made three major announcements through a recent order. They are: 

- Interoperable Account Aggregators (AAs): NBFC-Account Aggregators (NBFC-AAs) are going to be interoperable, so that customers can access and share their financial information from different institutions through any AA of their choice. 
    
- Demat investmaents are now visible on AAs: SEBI-regulated depositories can add bank deposit information to the Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) through AAs.
    
- A tech-policy committee for financial markets: Technical Consultative Committee for Financial Markets, a forum for structured engagement with market participants on policy and operational matters relating to the money, government securities and foreign exchange markets, their derivative markets and infrastructure.
    

While the first two changes are expected to be implemented by December 31, 2026, the tech policy committee’s terms of references will be notified later. 

How account aggregators work today, and where the gap is: 

AAs are NBFCs licensed by RBI that act as a consent-based data sharing bridge between users (Financial Information Users) and financial institutions like banks and loan providers (Financial Information Providers). Under the RBI directions, the financial information providers have to cross-share the data.

“The AA framework is based on federated interoperability, in which all AAs operate on standard open protocols and APIs which makes it technically easy for all FIUs and FIPs to integrate with all AAs. FIPs are not mandated to work with all AAs and cite operational and compliance challenges as a hurdle to work with all AAs. MeitY has further clarified that it is not mandatory for Data Fiduciaries to integrate with CMs. Hence, to this extent, interoperability can be guaranteed under both regimes, as long as all AAs/ CMs operate on common underlying technical specifications such as those laid down by ReBIT, and those to be prescribed by the DPB.” - [Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/reconciling-the-account-aggregator-and-consent-manager-frameworks/), an AA industry alliance.

In March 2025, Sahamati, the industry alliance for the AA ecosystem [said](https://sahamati.org.in/reconciling-the-account-aggregator-and-consent-manager-frameworks/#:~:text=FIPs%20are%20not%20mandated%20to%20work%20with%20all%20AAs%20and%20cite%20operational%20and%20compliance%20challenges%20as%20a%20hurdle%20to%20work%20with%20all%20AAs), “FIPs are not mandated to work with all AAs and cite operational and compliance challenges as a hurdle to work with all AAs.” The RBI’s  new regulation could change this as the definition of interoperability requires the ability to cross-share the data among the involved entities.

What changes for a user:

When the user applies for a loan or a credit card, or is exploring to apply for a loan, the banks or service providers might ask your consent to access your financial information from the Account Aggregator (AAs) that they have partnered or have an association with. 

And, if the users’ bank is not connected to the AA they have signed up with, you may have to create a new account on the new AA to link that account. 

However, under this interoperability framework; that second sign-up wouldn't be needed, if the user has consented to share/process the financial information with one AA, you might not need to consent to another AA as the particular bank or the service provider can access and therefore process the data from the already consented AA. 

Demat holders get deposits in the Consolidated Account Statement (CAS). The [CAS](https://www.cdslindia.com/CAS/FAQ.html) refers to the “transactions and holdings in investor's demat account(s) held with CDSL and NSDL as well as in units of Mutual Funds held in Statement of Account (SOA) form.” Under the new measure, the demat deposits will also get reflected in the CAS and thereby potentially impact financial scores like CIBIL.

A few questions to ask:

- How will interoperability work technically and legally? What are the rules or guidelines that the banks and AAs have to follow? 
    
- Whether FIPs will be required to connect to all AAs, or AAs will interconnect?
    
- Who will/should bear the cost of operational and compliance challenges?
