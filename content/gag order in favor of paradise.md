# Bengaluru Court restrains X from hosting 'false & defamatory' content about 'The Paradise' film

Four days prior to the release, a Bengaluru court has passed an immediarte and temporary injunction restraining X from hosting, publishing, or circulating what the court noted as "false and defamatory content" concerning the upcoming film The Paradise. 

"If an ex-parte protection is not granted at this juncture, the unhindered viral dissemination of the impugned materials right before the theatrical release could cause unquantifiable and irreversible economic and reputational damage to the Plaintiff.  The balance of convenience tilts heavily in favor of the Plaintiff."  – Additional City Civil and Sessions Judge, Bengaluru. 

The order was passed on September 19, 2026, by the Additional City Civil and Sessions Judge on Bengaluru, in a law suit filed by the film's producer, Lakshmi Venkateswara Cinemas, against X. Interestingly, unlike the previous orders, like Toxic, this order doesn’t mention John Doe / Ashok kumar.’ We are not sure if John Doe is mentioned in the petition. 

Order applies to the content flagged by the producers, not a blanket ban: The injunction restrains X, along with "their officers, agents, or any persons acting through them." from hosting, publishing, transmitting, reproducing, or making available content concerning the film "as identified specifically in the Schedule to the Plaint." On its face, then, the order is confined to the URLs the plaintiff has already flagged, rather than a blanket restriction on all future commentary about the film.

"The restriction is confined to specifically identified malicious URLs and does not hamper bona fide public reviews or genuine critique," noted the court, 

However, as [MediaNama’s previous reporting documented](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-telugu-producers-misusing-court-orders-curb-critical-online-reviews/), such vague advance gag orders would indirectly impact a few content creators who might do review movies. 

The producer’s rationale behind seeking a gag order: According to the court order, the producers stated that huge financial investments had gone into producing, marketing, and distributing The Paradise, and the producers of the movie argued that after the release of the trailer, there were "several systematically coordinated, false, and derogatory posts, comments, and URLs" appeared on social media, like X.

Relying on screenshots and URLs collated in a their petition, they argued:

"…the content crosses the boundary of fair criticism and constitutes deep, targeted commercial defamation calculated to destroy the commercial prospects of the film." – The petition filed by The Paradise Producers 

The producers also asked the court to not to send the prior notice to the defendants. They argued that by notifying the other side in advance would give "unidentified entities an opportunity to delete, alter, or multiply the defamatory URLs, thereby frustrating the very object of seeking an injunction." 

The court noted that the temporary injunction remains in force "only till next date of hearing," which is on on October 28, 2026. It also the X to Immediately comply with Order 39 Rule 3A of the CPC, which includes serving copies of the plaint, application, affidavit, and the order on the defendants within 24 hours by either registered post or via email, followed by an affidavit of compliance. 

Why it matters:  

Courts have repeatedly accepted urgency and financial harm as grounds to pass orders in  favour of movie producers ahead of their theatrical releases. Similar to previous cases, Paradise producers also approached similar courts, Bengaluru's City Civil Courts to secure temporary injunctions against the so-called "false," "defamatory," or "derogatory" online content, naming social media platform X. 

Meidanama has been covering the censorship attempts by movie producers. Some of the instances are:

- In June 2026, similar to various other instances, Bengaluru-based "techno-legal" and reputation-management firms, AiPlex and BlockBigg among thehave used similar orders against online content creators. In one such instance, they have sent a legal and [copyright notices to individual YouTube reviewers](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-telugu-producers-misusing-court-orders-curb-critical-online-reviews/), including Sushanth of Barbel Pitch Meetings and the channel Ra One For You, asking for take down or privatise critical reviews, even where the content arguably fell under Section 52 of the Copyright Act's exemptions for criticism, news reporting, and research. They have sent the copyright notice for using the Peddi movie’s poster in the background. 
    
- In August 28, 2026, the legal team of the same AI Plex, hired by Toxic movie producers, sent legal notices to atleast two YouTubers. In one of the notice [posted](https://x.com/Surajkumarrevi1/status/2092888661423772009) by content creator @Surajkumarrevi1, it can be seen that AI Plex sent the notices. Here is the [URL](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1mFpFUVYLHeTaWCVrfdG-c7xRH7BcjthF/view) of the legal notice ([archived](https://ibb.co/mFVGcFbR)) sent by AI Plex. 
    
- In January 2026, during the festive season of Sankaranti/Pongal, which a good season for cinema in Telugu states and Tamil Nadu, all the Telugu movies released during this season (except The Raja Saab) got the court orders [preventing BookMyShow from allowing users to rate the movie](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-bookmyshow-disables-reviews-telugu-film-court-order/). This trend has been continuing, the reccen being Producer Naga Vamsi’s [Vishwanath and Sons.](https://in.bookmyshow.com/movies/bengaluru/vishwanath-and-sons/ET00489815)
    

While Karnataka’s Bengaluru civil court continues to issu gag orders in the favour of producers. Madras High Court [held](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/banning-online-film-reviews-equivalent-to-curtailing-fundamental-right-to-freedom-of-speech-and-expression-madras-high-court/article69740119.ece) that a blanket ban on film reviews would amount to curtailing the fundamental right to free speech, where as the Kerala High Court has separately examined concerns around ["review bombing."](https://www.medianama.com/2023/10/223-review-bombing-kerala-high-court/)

It is important ton note that the order accessed by MediaNama does not name AiPlex, BlockBigg, or any other so-called reputation-management companies, nor does it list "John Doe" defendants or additional platforms/reviewers beyond X, a narrower scope, at least on paper. We can only know if the producers use this court order to target the potentially legitimate movie reviewers only after the release of the films.
