# MP HC upholds IT Rules’ on copyright takedowns amid misuse on social media platforms

Access the copy of the order here: [[pdf](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/anmol-jain-v-union-of-india-and-others-2300513.pdf)]

The Madhya Pradesh (MP) High Court (HC) has upheld the IT Rules provision that platforms use to remove posts for alleged copyright infringement. A bench of Chief Justice Alpesh Y. Kogje and Justice Vivek Rusia dismissed a creator's writ petition on September 17. It held that Rule 3(1)(b)(iv) of the [IT Rules, 2021](https://claude.ai/chat/ff72c19e-da9e-4264-85ad-ee0e7ebe7967#LINK-IT-RULES) is not unconstitutional.

The order matters because creators across India allege that unknown persons of filing false copyright complaints against them. The court did not decide whether [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/) wrongly removed the petitioner's posts. It sent him to remedies under the IT Act instead, which is a grievance redressal mechanism.

The petitioner's case

Anmol Jain runs the Instagram account memexnasha, which has about 39,000 followers. His petition says the account is a substantial source of his livelihood.

Instagram removed his posts dated April 3, 17 and 24, 2025, on July 6, 2025, after copyright complaints. Jain says he got no notice or hearing. He complained on July 25, 2025 that someone was misusing Instagram's copyright policy, and Instagram told him to use its own grievance mechanism. It removed another post, dated August 16, on August 25. Jain then asked for his suspended account to be reinstated on August 31.

Jain argued that Rule 3(1)(b)(iv) hands a private party a job that belongs to courts: deciding whether a patent, trademark, copyright or other proprietary right has been infringed. He challenged the rule under constitutional rights to equality, free speech and personal liberty. He also argued that Instagram breached natural justice by suspending his account without a hearing.

What the MP HC said: The bench said Instagram is an "intermediary" under Section 2(w) of the IT Act, 2000, so it must follow the due diligence rules in Rule 3. Rule 3(1)(b) asks platforms to make reasonable efforts to keep infringing content off their services. Rule 3(1)(c) lets them suspend or terminate user access, or remove non-compliant content.

“Once, by way of statutory enactment and Rules made thereunder, the respondent no.4 has been permitted to establish an online platform as an intermediary, then the authority has also been rightly given the right to manage and control the platform. Hence, we do not find any ground to interfere with the validity of Rule 3(1)(b)(iv) of the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code), 2021.” – MP HC

How Meta's copyright systems are being misused

While the allegations of abuse are now before the Delhi High Court’s Justice Anup Jairam Bhambhani who had [asked Meta](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-metas-copyright-delhi-hc-examine/) why scammers can access its Rights Manager tool and why original creators' applications are rejected, here are a few instances where the content cerators have alleged the misuse of Meta’s copyright systems.

Mohit Kumar, who posts as [@risewithmohit](https://www.instagram.com/risewithmohit/), alleges that scammers downloaded his videos, registered them as their own on Rights Manager and issued fraudulent strikes. Meta told the court his content was reinstated and his accounts were never suspended. The court told Meta to restore any removed content if Kumar proves ownership, and [MediaNama reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-metas-copyright-delhi-hc-examine/) that it listed the matter for September 24.

In a separate law suit, [MediaNama reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-delhi-hc-meta-copyright-takedown-case/) that online educator and content creator Pushkar Raj Thakur alleges some unknown anonymous people have uploaded unrelated content to Facebook pages. They then replaced it with copies of his new videos using the desktop "Edit Post" feature. He says Facebook kept the original upload date, so the posts looked older than his videos and triggered automated removals for the allewged copyright violations.

Meta told the court it would not take down Thakur's videos or close his account over copyright strikes until the next hearing. It also said it would restore deleted videos once he submits the URLs, according to court proceedings recorded in [July 1 order](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/high_court_order.pdf). The [May 29 order](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/high_court_order-29-May.pdf) had given Meta a week to explain what measures it can take against such misuse.

    Meta also admitted in the Delhi High Court that 13 copyright strikes against creator Saurabh Maurya were fake, [Bar and Bench reported](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/why-are-scammers-getting-metas-copyright-protection-tool-but-not-creators-delhi-high-court-asks) on August 11. That court is also examining whether platforms can suspend accounts over copyright strikes. This question sits close to the one Jain raised in Madhya Pradesh.
    

Meta's own [Rights Manager FAQ](https://www.facebook.com/rights_manager) says the tool matches content technically and that "Rights Manager cannot detect copyright infringement." It also stated that no copyright checking system matches the copyright infringements accurately. 

YouTube faced a similar problem in 2019. After creators complained of aggressive manual Content ID claims, it [required rights holders](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/better-tools-to-resolve-manual-content-id-claims/) to specify timestamps for allegedly infringing content. It later warned that [repeated inaccurate claims](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/updates-to-manual-claiming-policies/) might be suspended. 

The Madhya Pradesh order settles that platforms can act on copyright complaints under the IT Rules. It does not say on what proof they must act, how quickly a creator can get a post restored, or what remedy follows a false complaint. Those questions are now being tested and would possibly be answered in the Delhi High Court.
