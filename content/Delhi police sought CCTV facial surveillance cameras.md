# Delhi Police tender sought facial recognition CCTV cameras for Delhi’s Ramlila Maidan

  

In July 2026, Delhi Police has invited bids for 338 CCTV cameras across its Central and New Delhi districts, and the specifications require face recognition on its fixed and pan-tilt-zoom (PTZ) cameras, alongside analytics to detect crowds, abandoned objects and number plates, according to the tender documents reviewed by MediaNama. Internet Freedom Foundation (IFF) has [reported](https://internetfreedom.in/a-face-recognition-camera-ring-was-ordered-around-ramlila-maidan-ten-weeks-later-it-became-the-protest-site/) about the tender documents first. 

Delhi Police had published this tender with an estimate at Rs. 15.66 crore, whose bids closed on August 20. The [request for proposal (RFP)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1c3FDCkbjYkpsX2d1qX5QGLlzn1TXAZaH/view?usp=share_link), issued by the office of the Additional Commissioner of Police of Provisioning & Logistics, says the cameras are meant "to cover VIP/VVIP route & other locations" for the Republic Day and Independence Day celebrations.

These documents reveal that Delhi Police intends to have the facial recognition capabilities at the every camera type: 

- Fixed cameras must include analytics for "Intrusion, Tripwire, People counting/people gathering, face recognition, Tamper detection," 
    
- PTZ cameras must include "Tripwire, Intrusion, Object Abandon/Missing, Motion Detection, Face Recognition, Tamper detection." 
    
- ANPR cameras, which are specific cameras for vehicle number recognition, also state that the cameras must have edge analytics for "Face Detection" alongside people counting/gathering, perimeter protection and tamper detection.
    
- Video management software also "should support Face Recognition System," which "should become part of VMS with perpetual license." 
    

“The Manufacturer shall offer a mobile application with the capability to access live video from up to 500 cameras in both iOS and Android/OS platforms.”

These are the areas specified in the documents: 

- Ramlila Maidan gate, 
    
- Turkman Gate, 
    
- Delhi Gate, 
    
- Rajghat,
    
-  the India Gate hexagon, 
    
- Mandi House and 
    
- Patiala House.
    

“CCTV cameras are to be strategically placed at suitable height with structure arrangement, so as to identy and capture the face of a particular person roaming around at Critical/vulnerable points.”

Why it matters: As the Indian state continues to use and misuse the surveillance tech,there is no statutory law that specifically authorises and regulates police face recognition. For instance, Delhi Police has not disclosed the legal framework or safeguards for its use of face recognition. As [MediaNama reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-delhi-police-rti-reply-live-cctv-surveillance/), the [standing order](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1oeOJGeZjyWE1OvStTfiyJuVHjb-BypLR/view?ref=static.internetfreedom.in) that IFF obtained through an RTI application, and which the Ministry of Home Affairs [cited in Parliament](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/268/AU1996_LM2Ok7.pdf?source=pqars) when asked about face recognition safeguards, does not mention face recognition at all.

The RFP document refers to the word “privacy” only once, that too in the context of “privacy masking.” This [refers](https://www.hikvision.com/en/newsroom/blog/Privacy-masking-an-efficient-operation-for-privacy-protection-in-video-security/) to the “function that blurs or blacks out selected regions, faces, or bodies in live or recorded video footage.” In the context of this RFP, "privacy masking 4 area" might mean that each camera must be capable of defining up to four independent privacy zones within its field of view, where each zone could be selectively blurred, blacked out, or pixelated to protect private spaces such as neighbouring windows, balconies, or sensitive areas, while the rest of the scene remains fully visible for surveillance.

We did not find references in the RFP to privacy, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act or the Puttaswamy judgment, which IFF also noted. 

The RFP sets no accuracy standard for face recognition and names no database against which faces would be matched. It sets no similarity threshold, no rules on who can run a search, and no retention period for facial data. This could be part of another product/framework/workflow, making it into another tender. It is true that the RFP is more like a product requirement document and not a legal document that can set the usage/misuse limitations. However, these should have been specified as what is described will be eventually built or delivered. 

If the privacy-first measures are not a product-side priority at the tender-leve, how can we expect the real-life products and the services that these products deliver to have the privacy preserving principles?

The current surveillance tech has a real life impact too. In August 2026, after July’s Jantar Mantar protests, Delhi Police had told the Supreme Court in an [affidavit](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-student-protests-delhi-police-affidavit-facial-recognition-software-only-captures-serious-offense-criminals-546263) that facial recognition system identified 2,873 people with criminal antecedents at the Jantar Mantar protest site between July 20 and 26. Later,  [The Indian Express found](https://indianexpress.com/article/express-exclusive/delhi-police-facial-recognition-jantar-mantar-protest-accused-in-jail-10862099/) that at least 25 of them were in jail at the time. The Commissioner of Police [de-notified Jantar Mantar](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/india-news/delhi-police-de-notifies-jantar-mantar-as-protest-site-names-3-new-spots-126101201284_1.html) as a protest site and designated Ramlila Maidan.

Integration with Safe City that follows predictive policing: The RFP requires the cameras to be "compatible to integrate with any other CCTV cameras installed in other projects of Delhi Police like Safe City Project". The Safe City system is the one the Delhi Police standing order says ["will have tools for predictive policing".](https://internetfreedom.in/built-for-womens-safety-cleared-for-riot-control-inside-the-delhi-police-drone-sop/)
