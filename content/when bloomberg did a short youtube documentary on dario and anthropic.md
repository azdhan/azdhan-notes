# The Private Sector Built the Bomb This Time

_AI-refined from voice notes. Not AI-generated._

---

Was watching a short documentary from Bloomberg Originals — interview-style, started inside Anthropic. Mostly Dario. I also learnt today, for the first time, that his sister runs the company's operations.

**On why Anthropic went B2B:** Dario's argument is that consumer-facing products are optimised for engagement and advertising — and that degrades values. If you're trying to do something bigger, like find a cure for cancer or lift people out of poverty, the enterprise model works because enterprises are run with purposes. I think that's accurate. Targeting the masses means giving into the masses. It's also a good moment to revisit Google's old tagline — _don't be evil_ — and what happened to it.

**On unemployment:** This is the first time in human history where we might have the highest GDP growth running parallel to maximum job loss. Underemployment and unemployment at the same time as unprecedented economic output. That's a significant observation and I agree with it. There was also something in there about the difference between a _task_ and a _job_ — Dario apparently has a blog where he goes into this in detail. Need to find that.

**On Claude being used in warfare:** Some US military official reportedly said that where they earlier used to target 1,000 people, they're now targeting 5,000 with LLMs. There were also references to bomb blasts in Iran — a school girl targeted, 150 people killed. One of the interviewers asked Dario directly: when a US official is targeting a specific location, shouldn't Anthropic's safety systems flag that it's a school — given that the school is visible on Google search? How did Anthropic miss it, and should they be held responsible?

Dario's answer: at the end of the day, it is the human making the decision, not Anthropic.

There was also mention of Claude being used by the US government while targeting the Venezuelan president — and the whole controversy around supply chain risk lawsuits. And apparently when **Mythos** was released, the **NSA** reportedly used it despite the US government momentarily blacklisting it. I think the block has since been lifted, but I need to check.

**On the constitution:** They said even the **Declaration of Human Rights** is one of the sources used to orient the ethical behaviour of the model. A flawed constitution, essentially. There was also a reference to **Anthropic being called by Christian Pope** to discuss AI ethics — I don't know what was discussed there, need to look into it.

**The core observation:** All the landmark technologies until now — **GPS, nuclear power, the internet, cell phones** — were invented by governments. AI is the first where something this significant was built by a private sector company. Governments don't have direct access to these technologies because they didn't manufacture them. What we're watching now is an arms race to get that power back. I think that's a fundamental framing for how any government is going to approach AI regulation.
