 

A dark-comedy, one-night story. The day is chaotic; the night is dramatic. The story is set at night, mainly inside and around a cab.

An actress, Sreeleela, returns from shopping with four to six bags—Zudio/Zepto-style plastic bags. She takes a cab. The cab goes down a slope into a high-rise/wealthy area. After she gets home, she realizes she has left a small purse or wallet in the cab. She reports it through the app. The customer executive says they cannot share the driver’s details and tells her to file a complaint. For the story, we assume the driver has accessto her phone number, or he calls her from a personal number, so they reconnect.

She calls the driver back. She will not tell him what is inside the bag—she keeps saying it must not go outside. She offers him money to keep it secret. The driver had already done a few more rides after dropping her—maybe two, three, five, or six. So they begin retracing each ride one by one: where did we drop them, which building did they go into, etc. It becomes chaotic because they cannot easily trace people in buildings and high-rise areas. This retracing journey is the main engine of the night.

Optional chaotic sequence: a celebrity-house scene, in the mood of *AK vs AK*—someone like Anurag Kashyap entering Anil Kapoor’s house and creating chaos. The overall tone is funny, goofy, dark, and chaotic—dark comedy.

The critical object in the bag is a small notebook. It contains secrets Sreeleela wrote down—things she could not tell even her mother. She became an actress at a very young age; in interviews she has said her mother used to accompany her to shoots, and the industry sees her as very close to her mother. But there are things in the industry that even her mother cannot know, so she wrote them in this notebook. If the notebook comes out, it will expose the industry—like a major scandal comparable to the Suchi Leaks episode in Tamil Nadu, involving names and photos. The notebook is therefore very sensitive.

The idea behind why she documented it: a parent is supposed to know what is happening around their child. Since she does not have a father, and some things could not be told to her mother, she wrote them down. Now, if the notebook is released, the whole industry could be exposed.

By the climax, after the entire night’s chaos, they reach a point where they cannot continue. They give up. At that moment, the cab driver asks her what is really inside the bag, and she tells him. During the journey, Sreeleela also has an internal realization: let it be whatever happens. She decides. If the industry crew also comes out, it would be even better—but that is optional.

Ending: you can take a sunrise shot in the morning, though it is a bit cliché. It is okay to end that way. The serious note should not dominate the main core—maybe only the last two minutes. The audience should feel continuity, like another story begins where this one ends. The movie ends at the beginning. Keep the stakes in that range. Ideal length: around 1 hour 20 minutes to 1 hour 40 minutes.
