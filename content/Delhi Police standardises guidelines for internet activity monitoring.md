**# Delhi Police standardises guidelines for internet activity monitoring and will use Abhigyan for Aug 15

Delhi Police has reportedly issued a guidelines document on August 8th to standardize guidelines for 'Internet Protocol Detail Records (IPDR) and dump-data analysis' of the overall internet activity, [reported](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/delhi-news/delhi-police-get-5-step-workflow-guide-on-how-to-analyse-internet-activity-amid-spike-in-idpr-analysis-101786297869930.html) Hindustan Times' Jignasa Sinha. 

What does the IPDR data contain: "IPDR contains metadata of the user’s internet activity, and can help investigators establish how much data was exchanged and which service or application was accessed through timestamps, source and destination IP addresses, source and destination ports, bytes uploaded and downloaded, session duration, and subscriber identifiers," reported Hindustan Times, citing the document issued by Sub Inspector. The report noted that such combined analysis of internet activity with voice or SMS communication or physical location data can lead to trace the users and their device. 

What is the five-step process detailed by Delhi Police? The training material shared by Delhi Police reportedly had the following recommended workflow: 

1. Data ingestion and normalisation,
    
2. IP and port mapping,
    
3. Correlation,
    
4. behavioural profiling: investigators should create user activity timelines to identify anomalies and data spikes, while also scrutinizing VPN, proxy, and Tor activity for links to malicious infrastructure.
    
5. Reporting and evidence: Relevant authorities should document all findings through timelines and screenshots while maintaining legal authorization, chain of custody, data integrity, and subscriber privacy.
    

What is IPDR analysis: According to an explainer blog by Innefu, IPDR:

- logs metadata of internet sessions.
    
- does not contain message content, 
    
- provides metadata that must be correlated with other datasets.
    
- helps investigators identify browsing history, application usage, device behavior, and communication trails.
    

#### What are the details satcom companies must include in CDR/IPDR:  According to May 2025's [instructions from Department of Telecommunications](https://www.medianama.com/2025/05/223-india-satellite-security-rules-lawful-interception-satcom-operators/) to all the satcom service providers with a Global Mobile Personal Communication by Satellite (GMPCS) license, as part of the call detail records (CDR)/internet protocol detail records (IPDR), satcom players have to provide the following information:

- User terminal location, along with exact latitude and longitude
    
- Device ID,
    
- IMEI (International Mobile Equipment Identity),
    
- MSISDN ( Mobile Station International Subscriber Directory Number)
    
- Public IP address and
    
- private IP address for a particular internet access session. Read the difference between them [here](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/computer-networks/difference-between-private-and-public-ip-addresses/).
    

If the device is directly connected to the satcom player’s user terminal, the satcom player must provide with all the requisite IP details, MAC address, or other unique identifiers in the IPDR along with the IP address for the user terminal. If the device is directly connected to the connected device, the satcom player must provide details on a “best-effort basis.” Read MediaNama’s reporting on DoT’s instructions [here](https://www.medianama.com/2025/05/223-india-satellite-security-rules-lawful-interception-satcom-operators/). 

Delhi police stresses on monitoring VPN and Tor Activity: According to another document that issued guidelines on how to identify VPNs and proxy networks, the Delhi police intends to monitor the following aspects: 

- VPN, proxy and Tor-related activity
    
- connections to unusual or high-risk IP addresses,
    
- traffic through uncommon ports,
    
- long-duration sessions involving relatively little data,
    
- encrypted traffic on non-standard ports,
    
- repeated connections to the same remote IP
    
- unusually high data volumes during odd hours.
    

Delhi Poice to use MHA’s Abhigyan for the first time for suspect identification using fingerprints: Ahead of the Independence day, Delhi Police also intends to use Abhigyan application for the first time, according to a [PTI report](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/delhi-news/facial-recognition-fingerprint-screening-delhi-police-tightens-security-ahead-of-iday-101786274709440.html) syndicated by Hindustan Times. 

Abhigyan is a fingerprint database of more than one crore criminals. The report noted that the police personnel will be using a handheld device to collect the fingerprints of random people. This handheld device reportedly shows red and green light to flag if the individual is in their criminal database or not.

During the launch of Abhigyan, Union Home Minister Amit Shah said that he wants to prevent crime before it happens, especially repeat and international criminal networks. Here's what he said at the All India Fingerprint Conference in New Delhi on June 19: 

“So, there are two ways to approach crime. Earlier, our focus was on catching the criminal. But now, merely catching him is not enough. Once caught, he must go through the judicial process and be punished—so that fear is instilled in the minds of others. And secondly, we must work to stop crimes before they are committed. Crime patterns have to be recognised. Repeat offenders and interstate, international criminal networks will have to be stopped through this.” -- [Amit Shah (June 2026).](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-preventive-policing-home-ministry-launches-abhigyan-app-fingerprint-scans-street-level/)
