**can you have both passion and excitement in a longterm relationship.** how can you make it sustain. this is the fundamental question that the book trying to answer


## **passion in love fades overtime:** 
the general consensus among the both romanticists (intensity > stability) and realists (security > passion) 
- passion is can be measured by the tolerance of the one person for the uncertainty. 
- desire is fueled by the unknown and the eroticism resides in the anxiety and fascination.

 ## **predictability is a mirage**. 
 there is no such thing as knowing the partner fully. but when we think that we know the partner fully we  are creating a manufactured equilibrium. the sense of security will be gone/reduced when the person's real sense of self is brought in, as a surprise/casually.  this is not bad per se in my opinion, because, at the end, dealing with reality is what is required. i'd always prefer dealign with reality than living in a false sense of assurance. but, the thing is: is the other person/anyone ready to face the reality as it is?  or, are we too comfortable in letting things as they are? essentially its a question of: challenging vs status quo. 

> "love is a selective perception and delicious deception. its also a desire for connection and separation too "


## **correlation of sex and love:** 
on the contrary, the author argues that if there is more emotional intimacy there would/could be less sex and the couple could be still be as happy as before. sometimes, the love can also come in the way of sex. sometimes, the distance is needed for the desire to flow (erotic synapse). desire to a relationship is what air is to a fire.
