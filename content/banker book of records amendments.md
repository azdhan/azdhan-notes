
 
 Explainer: Finance Ministry proposes bill to replace 1891 Bankers’ Books Evidence Act 
 
Reference files: 
- The  Bankers’ Books  Evidence Act, 1891 -- (PDF)
- The  Bankers’ Books  Evidence Bill, 2026 -- (PDF)

The Ministry of Finance  has introduced the Bankers’ Books Evidence Bill, 2026 to repeal and replace the Bankers’ Books Evidence Act, 1891. The proposed law would expands the definition of bank records to include cloud-based data, explicitly recognize electronic records as legally admissible evidence in courts. 

**How the proposed bill changes the definition of bank records:** ” The current Act’s Section 2(3) covers records “kept in written form or stored in a micro film, magnetic tape or in any other form of mechanical or electronic data retrieval mechanism, either onsite or at any offsite location.”

The bill’s Section 2(1)(b) would replace this with language covering records “whether kept in written or physical form or stored in any form of data storage mechanisms such as electronic or digital form, or otherwise, either onsite or at any offsite or virtual or cloud location, including a back-up or disaster recovery site, or both.”

**The bill also retains the powers allowing senior police officers at the rank of Superintendent of Police to compel banks to hand over records during criminal investigations without necessarily first obtaining a court order.** 
 
The Section 8(1) of the proposed bill states: “No officer of a bank shall, in any legal proceeding to which the bank is not a party, be compelled to produce any banker’s book, the contents of which may be proved under this Act, or to appear as a witness to prove the matters, transactions and accounts recorded therein, unless by order of the Court made for special cause, to be recorded in writing.”

 Both the existing law and the proposed bill contain a provision treating an order from a senior police officer as equivalent to a court order during certain investigations, meaning a judge does not need to sign the order first.

This means, if the bill is passed, an officer of rank not lower than Superintendent of Police can issue an order that has the same legal force as a court order for:

- Compelling a bank officer to produce books under Section 5; and
- Ordering inspection and copies under Section 6.

**"The bill only says loudly what is already a common practice** - Officers routinely ask bankers to get account transaction details of any bank account for cyber-crime investigation with no oversight whatsoever and they comply always," [wrote](https://x.com/iam_anandv/status/2085239962862236024?s=20) Anand Venkatanarayanan, Co-Founder, Deepstrat and a cybersecurity expert, while quote the news report related to the Bill. 

**Conditions for admissibility of records maintained in electronic or digital form**: The Section 6 of the bill legitimises the admissibility of digital records as evidence in the courts. The Section 7(1) of the bill set a few conditions. Some of them are: 

- **The network have to be secure and strong to beat cyber risks and threats:** “the network, devices and the data contained therein were secure and equipped to meet the challenge of cyber risks or threats;"
- **The record has to be the true record:** "the said copy is a true copy of such entry or information and was prepared from all the relevant records, and it correctly represents, or is appropriately derived from, such records;" 
- **The data entry to be done only by the authorised persons:** "the data entry or any other operation had been performed only by authorised persons after taking adequate safeguards and no unauthorised alteration or change of the data is observed or detected during the relevant time"

The conditions also asks to have a "adequate safeguards" during the data transfer process. Additionally, the bill also states that any such conditions notified by the central government  are also to be complied. 
 
Further, Section 7(2) adds that if multiple computer systems or communication devices were used to create, store or process the information, they are treated as a single system.
