# BJP MP lists private bill seeking social media ban for under-13s without parental consent

Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) MP Baijayant Panda's private member bill sought ban on creation of social media accounts for under-13s without prior parental control and also sought parental control dashboard. This was first reported by Hindustan Times' Sejal Sharma. Read the original reporting [here](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/social-media-ban-under-13-parliament-private-bill-bjp-mp-baijayant-panda-information-technology-it-act-101786166528451.html). 

Full title of the Bill: Safeguarding Healthy Internet Environments for Little Digital-Natives (SHIELD) Bill, 2025,

What is being proposed by the SHIELD Bill? 

- No social media account for under-13s without parent approval.
    
- Mandatory age-verification systems for platforms accessible to minors, which can include all the major social media platforms, like Instagram, Facebook, SnapChat etc. 
    
- Parental-control dashboards for parents/guardians to allow: 
    

- monitoring social media activity,
    
- manage privacy settings and
    
- restrict screen time.
    

- Impose safety obligations on social media platforms, online gaming platforms and 'other digital intermediaries. 
    

- This includes, preventive steps that a platform needs to ensure to prevent children exposed to 'pornography, gambling and simulated betting, violent or extremist material and drug-related content.'
    

- Prohibit platforms from 'tracking, profiling and personalized advertising.
    
- Section 69A might be invoked for temporary suspension or blocking of the platforms' services, if there are 'repeated or wilful violations,'
    

- IF a parent/guardian flags the content, the intermediaries should remove/disable the content after verification within 36 hours..
    
- If the content involves child sexual material, then it has to be removed/disabled within 24 hours.
    
- All the platforms should reportedly have a reporting button titled 'Report Child  Harm' accessible to all the users. 
    

- Upto Rs. 10 crore penalty if a platform is found guilty for any violations. 
    

According to Hindustan Times and [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/bjp-mp-lists-private-bill-on-social-media-ban-for-children/article71324617.ece) reporting, this bill wasn't placed in the Parliament on August 7 because of adjournments. 

Drafted the Bill with 'wide consultations with all the stakeholders,' says the BJP MP, who introduced the private member's bill, as quoted in The Hindu's report. He also said that it is “now well established by multiple studies that many social media algorithms are not only designed to foster addiction but also to embellish and reinforce narrow and extreme viewpoints, rather than shed light on all angles of any issue,” as per The Hindu's report.

BJP MP Baijayant Panda’s private member bill comes at the moment when various Indian states  such as [Andhra Pradesh](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-andhra-pradesh-explores-digilocker-age-tokens-social-media-curbs-children-aged-13-16/), [Karnataka](https://www.medianama.com/2026/03/223-karnataka-cm-social-media-ban-under-16/), and [Goa](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-goa-under-16-social-media-ban-australia-law/), as well as the age-graded approach to restrictions are reportedly being considered by the [central government](https://www.medianama.com/2026/03/223-india-social-media-age-restrictions-law/). 

Panda also said that the centre also seized on the issue of social media ban/restrictions for children following the global developments across various countries. While the social media ban for children is already in place in [Australia](https://www.medianama.com/2024/10/223-all-about-australia-ban-social-media-teen-users/), [Indonesia](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/346040/permenkomdigi-no-9-tahun-2026) and [Malaysia](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-malaysia-enforces-under-16-social-media-registration-ban/), here is the brief list of countries seeking social media ban/restrictions: [United Kingdom,](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-uk-govt-powers-parliament-restrict-social-media-children/) [UAE](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-uae-social-media-access-children-below-15/), [New Zealand,](https://www.stuff.co.nz/politics/360947120/parliamentary-committee-calls-under-16s-social-media-ban) [Pakistan,](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-pakistan-social-media-ban-under-16-users/) [Spain](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-spain-under-16-social-media-ban-greece-may-follow/)  [France,](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jan/27/france-social-media-ban-under-15s) [Denmark,](https://apnews.com/article/denmark-social-media-ban-australia-1e96a3df3276cc2033a6f04effb89f51) [Germany](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-germany-ruling-coalition-social-media-age-limits-children-eu-wide-standards/) and [France](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-french-parliamentary-committee-approves-u-15-social-media-ban/).
