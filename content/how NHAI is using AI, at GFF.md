# NHAI, GST Network & Mumbai Metro listed their AI use cases at GFF 2026

"In NHAI, we have an inherent advantage because AI is inbuilt in the road-making cycle. It starts from planning, … At all stages, we have been using AI extensively," said Vishal Chauhan, Member of Administration at National Highways Authority of India (NHAI), and Chairman and Managing Director of Indian Highways Management Company Limited (IHMCL), at Global Fintech Fest (GFF) 2026 on September 11. He was speaking during a panel [titled](https://www.globalfintechfest.com/agenda) "AI-Led Commerce: Building Intelligent, Trusted and Inclusive Trade Systems," moderated by NPCI’s Executive Director, Nitin Mishra.

Some of the AI use cases at NHAI: 

- AI drone analytics: "What we are doing is drone analytics. In every construction stretch, every three months we fly a drone, take static photographs, and based on that we monitor 52 parameters using AI. Like, at what subgrade level the route is under construction, which layer of work is complete, which is not. We monitor the plantation using drone analytics."
    
- AI-based scanning of vehicle numbers: "With the help of AI, we are able to read the number plate, every number plate is unique to a FASTag. If I'm able to read the number plate, I'm able to charge through FASTag only. Now we are able to read about 99% of vehicles [with AI]. There is hardly any leakage." He stated this in the context of failure rates in the FASTag system.
    
- Pricing the annual toll pass: "When we were planning the annual pass, we wanted to fix a price and put a cap on the government's toll loss. We did a lot of data crunching using AI on this [FASTag] data and the pass-check transaction [data]. We arrived [at the conclusion] that if we fix the annual pass rate at like Rs.3,000 per annum, the loss to the government will not be much, and it will be affordable to the public." 
    
- Auditing road safety signs: “What we do is put a dash camera on a vehicle and take the picture. With the help of AI, the dashcam will tell us, as per the Indian Road Congress [norms], [X] road signs are required on this road…. Otherwise, it would take a team one week or 15 days to count and analyse the road signs required. AI gives us, in three hours, what the shortages in road signs are." 
    

On barrier-free tolling and number-plate recognition: "The best of the FASTag readers is able to read only 90% of FASTags. That means, if it's a barrier-free [toll], we are going to lose 10% of the revenue.” He further stated that this loss-leakage could be mitigated by using AI-enabled Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) cameras. 

“Now, we are able to read about 99% of the vehicles. There is hardly any leakage in there. That's very best use of AI, where we are able to capture the vehicle, toll it without even stopping even for a second at a time," said Vishal Chauhan  

How GST Network is using AI:  Satish Kumar Srivastava, Senior Vice President at Goods and Services Tax Network (GSTN), stated the following use cases: 

- On using AI to read unstructured taxpayer documents: "Data variety is a category of use case we used to hear about often in the big-data world — velocity, volume and variety. Velocity and volume were solved, but variety was not. AI can potentially solve it. If, in an unstructured document, the data embedded has to be used in a use case from the front-office side, that is now made possible by leveraging AI."
    
- On AI in GSTN's own software development: "AI has been extremely, extremely good on the coding side since January; for development, testing, design, documentation. AI-led software engineering is [also] in a sweet spot from our perspective."
    
- On security: "GSTN has always faced [attacks](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/gstn-safe-from-global-malware-attack-no-need-to-worry-ceo/articleshow/59350941.cms). From within this industry as well as from across. From a security perspective, we are also leveraging leading AI vendors."
    
- On the economic impact of multilingual AI: "If a small business is doing compliance, and if they have a Hindi-speaking tax team, [multilingual AI] will lower the cost for them and that's an economic impact."
    

"Whatever use cases we roll out, from the front-office, taxpayer-facing perspective, or the back-office, tax-officer-facing perspective, need to be grounded in law, in acts, and in rules. AI, by its very nature, is very probabilistic. We cannot have a situation where a tax officer is issuing a notice, and that's not explainable to a particular act," said Satish Kumar Srivastava, GST Network.

How Mumbai Metro is using AI: "We have AI-monitored pantographs. The pantographs are the most important things; they ensure that electricity reaches the coach. Earlier, this used to be physically monitored, and it used to take a long time. Now this is happening on its own," said Dr. Sanjay Mukherjee, Metropolitan Commissioner, Mumbai Metropolitan Region Development Authority (MMRDA).

Tata deployed 300 agents using Google’s Gemini: During the same session, Rajesh Ranjan, India Lead for Government Affairs and Public Policy at Google, said that the Tata company deployed 300 agents using Google’s Gemini platform in the span of nine months. “The efficiency that it brought to the entire commercial operation, and the economics of scale are staggering, in terms of the benefits they are driving out of it," he said. 

He then stated Google's recent open protocols for agentic commerce. They are:

- [A2A](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/): Agent-to-agent protocol,
    
- [AP2](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-agents-to-payments-ap2-protocol): Agent payment protocol, and
    
- [UCP](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-google-agentic-commerce-protocol-e-commerce-user-behaviour/): Universal Commerce Protocol. 
    

“What it does for smaller enterprises is, from day zero, when you're innovating and creating something based on agentic commerce, you have a standard already adopted by large enterprises, and you don't have to face the resistance of integration when doing business with larger enterprises," he added. 

He later said that Google’s AI products like DeepMind is working “very closely on the ground with the Ministry of Agriculture” for “a decision-support system,like weather warnings, flood warnings.” 

Addressing the AI usage in health care sector, he said that "one of our models was able to do a retinal scan and predict diabetes [risk] for around 600,000 people in India in the pilot itself, which will scale to about 6 million [over] two to three years. The impact in terms of early detection, and [reducing] the cost of treatment, will be huge."
