Why Pranit More's case at the SC could reopen what Ranveer Allahabadia's closed

The Supreme Court of India has sought the Haryana and Maharastra government responses to controversy around Stand-up comedian Pranit More's crowd work in Gurugram in June 2026, according to the [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/370-biryani-comment-row-pranit-more-tells-supreme-court-he-is-ready-to-face-trial-seeks-clubbing-of-firs)'s reporting. 

SC clubbed the FIRs from Maharastra and Haryana police. “Even before the FIR was registered, as soon as I got to know about the gravity of the situation, I deleted the video. I was travelling at that time, and the PR team that I have, they did not take my approval before publishing the video,” he reportedly submitted before the SC. 

**What is the controversy:** At Pranit More's live comedy show in Gurugram, during the crowd work, one of the audience member, Himanshu Jangra, was explaining his interaction with a woman. Jangra 'jokingly' said that he was expecting some sexual favors in return for the "investment" of Rs. 370's worth of Biriyani. Replying to this, More said, "peak Gurugram content" and reportedly handed him Rs. 5,000 cash prize. 

https://www.youtube.com/shorts/2Cv_ciVcUhI  

The short reel eventually went viral and prompted a hashtag [#biryaniisnotconsent](https://x.com/search?q=%23biryaniisnotconsent&src=typed_query) campaign. Later, Jangra's employer [fired](https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/bollywood/rs-370-biryani-row-himanshu-jangra-fired-2-clients-stepped-back-pranit-more-10734294/) him and Moore [apologized](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DZj2js5CPZZ/?hl=en) and deleted the video. 

Later, Maharashtra Cyber Police had registered an FIR against More. Then, a separate FIR was later registered by the Gurugram Police on a complaint by the National Commission for Women (NCW).

**A PIL in SC seeking platform regulations over individual liability:** Apart from the two separate FIRs leading to the hearings in Supreme Court, advocate Vishal Tiwari [filed a Public Interest Litigation (PIL)](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-pil-against-digital-misinformation-rs370-biryani-pranit-more-all-india-judges-badminton-event-statutory-framework-for-stand-up-comedy-539472) arguing that individual creators shouldn't be penalized for their remarks. Instead, he argued:

- Regulations should be around  that social media platform's algorithms which can potentially amplify the so-called controversial remarks, which can in result can influence the public attitudes towards women, consent and dignity.
    
- A independent judicial commission for studying the impact of 'unrestricted [access to social media on children below 16 years and age verification mechanisms](https://www.medianama.com/tag/nama-age-verification-and-restricting-social-media-for-children/) along with some safe guarding mechanisms. 
    

**Why this case matters?** After the Ranveer Allahabadia and Samay Raina controversy from India's Got Latent show, When the Allahabadia sought some reliefs from SC, the court [asked](https://www.medianama.com/2025/03/223-sc-asks-govt-to-draft-proposal-to-regulate-online-obscene-content-in-ranveer-allahbadia-hearing/) the Solicitor General of India to draft a regulatory proposal to prevent the airing offensive content to “well-known moral standards of our society.” 

Later in November 2025, the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting [stated](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-mib-draft-rules-for-perverse-ugc-sg-mehta-informs-supreme-court/) that it is considering new regulations for content moderation of “perverse” online content and will put the draft rules up for public consultation.

“A book, painting, or other creation works differently because people have a choice. Physical restrictions can still apply, and the material is not always open to the public. But when you turn on your phone, and something appears that you never wanted, or never wanted your children to see, and it is forced on you, what should happen then?” -- [Justice Surya Kant (November, 2025)](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-mib-draft-rules-for-perverse-ugc-sg-mehta-informs-supreme-court/) 

However, MIB hasn't comeup with any of such consultations to regulate the free speech. However, in August 2026, the SC [closed](https://x.com/internetfreedom/status/2098378465741606968?s=20) the case. 

Nonetheless, the Pranit More controversy is such similar incident that has reached the Supreme Court, which can potentially trigger or invite some online regulations to "moderate" free speech or platforms themselves. While these regulations may or may not take force, the self-censorship has already started in the name of being "careful" as the online abuse, heckling and even death threats from the mob has become an unfortunate reality.

For instance, after Samay Raina's India's Got Latent controversy, he began the Season 2 with even more sponsors. But, the content that eventually goes online has become too filtered and doesn't that edginess that the Season One had. Samay Raina now has a legal team [11 member](https://youtu.be/rkKZIMPecRA?si=-ESk4e_-dtaEPy6K&t=3177) to vett his content before it goes online. Meanwhile, we are not sure if Samay had a legal team to review his Season One content because the end credits of Season One doesn't have any end credits. 

"Just like we can’t expect theaters to protect films, or comedy clubs to protect comedians, we can’t expect platforms to protect content either. YouTube has removed the video, it appears because of a legal request, and it has probably in case of countless other government requests that are afforded secrecy. That is their right as a platform, but it is also a failure of principle. If a regulation demands censorship, platforms will most likely comply and take the path of least resistance, just as Twitter, Google, and Facebook did with the IT Rules." -- [Nikhil Pahwa (February 2025)](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-in-defence-of-offence-on-the-ranveer-allahbadia-controversy/)

Why should the internet audience get to see only the so-called "morally" or legally correct versions of any content, while the live audience might get an uncensored versions? It will end up creating two parallel versions of same/similar content degrading the whole internet experience. As MediaNama's editor Nikhil Pahwa [argued](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-in-defence-of-offence-on-the-ranveer-allahbadia-controversy/), "The mob shouldn’t win."
