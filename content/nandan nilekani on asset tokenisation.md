# Nandan Nilekani’s vision for asset tokenization in India

“Now it's very clear that tokenization, the time has arrived,” declared Nandan Nilekani, the chief architect behind the UIDAI's digital ID, Aadhaar. 

  

Nandan Nilekani, the chief architect behind the Aadhaar, said this in the context of how RBI and SEBI are approaching tokenizations. For instance, at the same Global Fintech Fest (GFF 2026): 

- Land: The Maharashtra CM Devendra Fadnavis [announced](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-maharashtra-land-tokenization-devendra-fadnavis/) that they will pass an act to tokenize lands. 
    
- Gold: RBI said that they are [considering](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-rbi-gold-tokenization-gff-2026/) gold tokenization. 
    
- Corporate bonds: SEBI and RBI are going to [launch](https://www.globalfintechfest.com/agenda) the Demat 2.0 and “India’s First Tokenized Corporate Bond.” 
    

  

“Companies are already launching these bonds,” he said, referring to the RBI and SEBI’s tokenized corporate bonds. “So, clearly there's a lot of action in the era of tokenization,” he stated. 

  

Nilekani explains what is asset tokenization is: “What it really means is that you take an asset and describe all its attributes and put it in one package along with the asset so that that token which is both the asset and its descriptors is all bundled together and is portable to give to somebody else.”

  

“A government file is a token because a government file on a particular topic has all the documents over the years of what has transpired on that topic, the comments of various bureaucrats. It's all in one place.”

  

“And, when that file goes from A to B, the entire information travels in the file. So, in fact, the file is like a token. Or your currency note; if you have a 500 rupee note, it has everything about the note in the note, whether it's the value, the fact that it's certified by the RBI, its number, and all that. And so when you give that 500 rupee note to somebody else, the other person is confident that they received the full value of that. So, that's a token. So, tokens are known to us.”

“There are about 600 billion dollars of tokens today. Most of them are like stablecoins. But the expectation is that there'll be a lot more. I mean, one estimate is 19 trillion dollars of assets on tokens in the next seven, eight years.”

  

Nilekani’s three examples: "It's like a voucher, which is purpose-specified. That's a horizontal capability that's used across these applications.” He illustrated the asset tokenization through these three examples: 

  

1. When a living cow becomes an asset for loans 
    

  

“In a village in Tamil Nadu, Udhangarai, meet Devanai. She keeps cattle and leads a dairy cooperative where 40 families bring milk each morning. She wants more cattle, more milk, and a proper shelter but cannot get the finances she needs. The value is in front of her. Yet a distant lender cannot easily verify the animal, confirm it is hers, or assess its condition and earning potential,” he stated as a problem that asset tokenization can solve. 

  

- Biometric IDs for Animals: “A Dwara-E dairy field officer arrives with a phone. The muzzle pattern of each animal can help distinguish one from another, much like a biometric marker.”
    
- A detectable and traceable token will have all details: “One of Devanai's cattle is tokenized, creating a unique digital representation linked to trusted information about the animal, its identity, ownership, age, health, and milk productivity. A digital proof helps make later changes detectable and traceable. A verifiable cattle credential is issued for the record and made available to her.”
    
- Consent and data sharing without human contact: “She decides what information to share, when, and with whom. With her consent, selected information moves across an open network to the lenders she chooses. Participating lenders can discover her request and respond without her visiting them one by one.”
    
- “Two lenders express interest. She compares their offers and chooses one. After Devanai gives further consent, the selected lender verifies the information, carries out its usual credit assessment, and approves the loan.”
    
- “She reviews and accepts the terms. Once she accepts, the lender makes 50,000 rupees available to Devanai as a purpose-based digital loan balance, visible in her app and usable for cattle feed and shelter materials. At an eligible supplier, she scans a QR code with her phone.”
    
- “The transaction is checked against the agreed conditions, and the approved amount is paid directly to the supplier. Soon, the cattle have a roof. The value was already there.”
    

  

“The trusted record made it easier to recognize and use. And cattle are only one example. A machine powering a small business, rooftop solar, a registered fishing boat, an accepted invoice,” he added. He also said that asset tokenization is already rolling out in Tamil Nadu, led by the Dvara group. 

  

2. When idle goods in a warehouse can become transferable tokens
    

  

“If you can unlock warehouse receipts and tokenize them, and then offer that token to multiple lenders, then suddenly you have created liquidity for your inventory,” he said. 

Giving a hypothetical example of Rohan, who doesn’t have much agency in the mandi (local farm markets), he stated what the reimagined tokenized world could offer instead of “watch the harvest rot in the rain”: 

  

- Tokens as the standards and dignity: “The farmer, grains, warehouse, and insurance in one token. Four lenders, one receipt. And for the first time, Rohan wasn't the one asking. He was the one choosing. The produce stayed in the warehouse. The dignity came home with him.”
    
- Tokens as portable receipts: “For years, the harvest waited on one door. Now, multiple doors wait on the harvest. So this again is an example of how you can increase the bargaining power of the farmer by creating a portable receipt for his warehouse goods, which is acceptable to every lender.”
    
- Tokens and the details it contains: And here again, the token has all the details about that. How many kilos or tons of wheat or rice? What is the quality? Which warehouse is it stored in? Is it insured? So all the information that a lender needs to give a loan against a warehouse receipt is in the token. And that can be now shared with multiple lenders.
    
- Tokens bring agency and bargaining power: “And then the bargaining power goes to the farmer because he can choose which lender. Because in the old model, you could only take a loan from a person, and then obviously that person has power over you. And this is a general principle we have done in all the design of public systems.”
    

“So you can see that all these ideas are working, are either live or going live. They're using all the internet principles, and they're actually going to make a difference to people on the ground. This is again a second example of what is possible.” 

  

3. Tokenizations for the interoperable loan documents. 
    

  

“UB and Homewell, are now looking at securitizing the loans that are there on the marketplace. And using the internet infrastructure, they are coming out with a single standard so that all the loan documents are done properly and tokenizing them so that they're interoperable.”

  

“They're using smart contracts to standardize things. So here again, you take an unstructured area, bring in standards, bring in tokenizations, create portability, and create securitization.”

  

“A token is an asset with the rules that are traveling with it. And this is designed for interoperability so any bank can use it or any plugin can use it.” – Nandan Nilekani. 

  

Nilekani on why asset tokenizations have to be on a public chain: “But if you want to take regulated assets, bonds, and certificates of deposit and so on, and you have to look at the full ecosystem design, not just issuance, but how it's used and how it can be, how do you get liquidity? That is why you need a public chain. If you do this on a private chain, nobody else has access to the private chain. You have to use a public chain.”

  

“But if you use a public chain, then you have worries about data security. And therefore, what you write on the public chain has to be unique but encrypted. And there are many, many, many technical issues to using a public chain.”

  

“I think we have to think through the ecosystem design of tokenization of regulated assets.”

  

Nilekani on how AI agents and asset tokenization: 

  

“The [AI] agent can package the token and offer it to different kinds of lenders and get loans. So agents are complementary to tokenization. Tokenization by itself will only handle issuance.”

  

“This is not just going to be transactions initiated by people. These are going to be transactions initiated by agents. Agents that are working 24/7. They're finding markets. So somebody has an invoice.”

  

“But tokenizations on public chains supported by agentic transactions will also create demand. And therefore, that is also important for this. And I believe that we have to look at how to bring agents and tokens together to create the volumes that we need to justify these kinds of platforms.” 

  

“Today, a small business can have a 24/7 agent, the best analysis on the internet, a token that proves itself, and a marketplace where we can either sell it or give a loan against it or whatever.”

  

He also stated that he thinks that Indian regulators are invited to the five-day Tech Week event at Cambridge’s Center for Regulation and Innovation, where the discussions around regulating asset tokenization could be discussed. Along with him, he said that people like Augustine Carstens, Prabhakar Raghavan, and Pramod Verma can help for the idea of asset tokenization.
