# SC dismisses YSRCP plea against takedown of 'Jagananna Connects' handles

On August 25, the Supreme Court of India refused to take up the takedown of Yuvajana Sramika Rythu Congress Party's (YSRCP) social media handles. Justice Dipankar Datta and Justice Sheel Nagu asked the petitioners to approach the High Courts. [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-refuses-to-entertain-ysrcps-plea-against-take-down-of-its-social-media-posts-asks-to-move-hc-547271) reported this first. 

YSRCP filed this petition under the Article 32 of the Constitution, alleging that the takedowns were carried out contrary to the procedure laid down in the Shreya Singhal judgement. 

Brief Context: In June 2026, MediaNama reported that on the blocking of their Facebook page, “YSR Congress Party – YSRCP." When the account was blocked, the ex-AP CM and current opposition to TDP-JSP-BJP alliance, said that he would "challenge these unfair actions through every legal and constitutional avenue. No intimidation, censorship, or misuse of institutions can silence the voice of the people.” 

He also tagged Union Minister for Electronics and Information Technology, Ashwini Vaishnaw, MeitY, Meta and Amit Shah and PM Modi in the tweet.

A very brief timeline of events: 

- October 26, 2025: YSRCP reportedly posted on X about the Kurnool tragedy allegedly [caused](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/andhra-pradesh/purchase-of-fake-liquor-from-illegal-shop-caused-kurnool-bus-fire-deaths-ysrcp-1912660) by illegal belt shops.
    
- Later, an FIR was registered alleging that the YSRCP's posts insulted AP CM and were intended to provoke the rival TDP. 
    
- January 27, 2026: Inspector of Police in Kurnool issued a notice to Twitter (now X) to take down the URLs posted by the party.
    
- August 25, 2026: The case goes to the Supreme Court of India, which directs the YSRCP to approach the high courts. 
    

Why it Matters: To understand the scle of this censorship, let's look the censorship figures [reported](https://x.com/imsoumyarendra/status/2089596107974406478/photo/1) by Indian Express’ Soumyarendra Barik: 

-  One blocking order every 68 seconds.
    
- ~1,275 orders per day.
    
- ~1.95 lakh orders in just five months (March - July 2026).
    

While the core issues of free speech restrictions remain, another core problem that is being faced by the victims of online censorship is the inability to receive the original blocking or takedown orders signed by the assigned designated officials. 

Increasingly, the censorship victims have been [approaching](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-drnimoyadav-kumar-nayan-meity-x-account-block-delhi-hc/) various [courts seeking reliefs.](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-inside-petition-journalists-challenge-blocking-instagram-reel-related-aps-ai-data-center/) In some instances, the victims had to appraoch HCs only to get the concerned takedown orders. Only during the court’s intervention, the intermediary platforms are [providing](https://www.medianama.com/2026/03/223-meity-blocking-order-x-account-drnimoyadav-ai-content/) the original blocking providers. This case of YSRCP approaching the Supreme Court of India against online political censorship is another instance of how censorship victims had to approach courts.
