The contingency argument stood out in the debate between Javed Akhtar and the Mufti on the existence of God, partly because it is more difficult to follow. The argument begins by defining God as a non-contingent being. A non-contingent being is not dependent on anything else, whereas contingent beings depend on other beings. This dependence is illustrated through examples like the food chain. Even after a person dies, bacteria and other life forms depend on that body, so the chain of dependency continues. In this view, dependency seems to extend almost infinitely.

Believers who support the contingency argument claim that such an infinite chain cannot ultimately explain existence. They argue that if everything were contingent, God would also end up being dependent on something else, possibly the universe. This would be contradictory, because God is claimed to be the creator of the universe and therefore cannot depend on it. Hence, they conclude that there must be a non-contingent being that does not rely on anything else.

An opposing argument is presented by a person on Instagram who discusses Indian philosophy and draws on Nāgārjuna’s reasoning. This argument uses the concept of cause and effect, where an effect is typically understood to depend on a cause. For example, if a storm destroys a house, the destruction is the effect and the storm is the cause. The cause can occur regardless of whether the house exists, so the cause is not dependent on the effect.

Nāgārjuna challenges this one-way dependence and argues that cause and effect are mutually dependent because of the nature of causal elements. This mutual dependence is explained through the example of a mother and a child. For a woman to become a mother, she must have the essential qualities or potency required to produce a child. Without the ability to produce the effect, she cannot be called a mother. In this sense, her identity as a mother depends on the existence or possibility of the child. The cause and the effect define each other.

Applying this reasoning to God and the universe, the argument suggests that if God is the creator of the universe, then God must possess certain qualities in order to create it. These qualities may themselves depend on the universe. If those qualities cannot exist without the universe, then without the universe God would not possess them, which raises a challenge to the idea of God as an independently existing creator.
