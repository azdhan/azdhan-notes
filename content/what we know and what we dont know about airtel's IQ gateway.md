# Airtel IQ Gateway takes enterprise messaging into its own network; what we know, and what we don't

Airtel Business recently [launched](https://www.airtel.in/press-release/10-2026/airtel-business-launches-iq-gateway-indias-first-business-communications-platform-with-telco-grade-network-intelligence-for-safe-enterprise-messaging/) Airtel IQ Gateway. It says that it is like an all-in-one platform that lets businesses manage their enterprises’ customer communications through various channels like SMS, WhatsApp, RCS and voice, along with a silent SIM authentication that may not depend on traditional OTPs. 

This is the URL to the platform: [www.airtel.in/b2b/iq-gateway](http://www.airtel.in/b2b/iq-gateway) 

Airtel said that it was launched after some trial deployments with a few banks, financial institutions. However, Airtel hasn’t shared any metrics or benchmarks or results from these tests. So, we can’t independently validate the accuracy of their network-level intelligence systems. It will be showcased at Airtel's booth at the India Mobile Congress, October 7-10.

What is Airtel trying to solve with IQ Gateway? It is trying to be a single control point for the enterprises’ communication channels to reach telco users via channels like SMS, voice as well as WhatsApp and Rich Communication Services (RCS)

  

This is to remove the external/ecosystem dependencies as the enterprises or the businesses no longer have to rely on the multiple vendors for each/different services. It also decreases/removes Airtel’s own dependencies, like telemarketers for bulk business communications.

"By packaging voice, SMS, WhatsApp and video connectivity into a developer-facing, API-driven, pay-as-you-go product, Airtel repositioned itself from an upstream infrastructure supplier to a direct participant in the CPaaS value chain competing with vendors such as Twilio, Tanla, Route Mobile and Gupshup, several of whom depend on operators like Airtel for underlying connectivity access." -- [MarkHub24](https://www.markhub24.com/post/airtel-iq-bharti-airtel-s-communications-platform-as-a-service-cpaas-model), a marketing research company.

No OTPs; no SIM Binding, claims Airtel: It claimed that this could replace the "OTP SMS for login and SIM-binding flows by validating that the entered number matches the active SIM in-device, reducing SIM-swap and social-engineering risks."

This comes at the moment when the messaging/communication platforms operating in India that use SIM as an identifier/authentication could be forced to implement [SIM Binding](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-sim-binding-unworkable-for-apps/). The current deadline for the compliance is [December 31, 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-dot-sim-binding-deadline-extended-dec-2026/). [Follow this tag](https://www.medianama.com/tag/sim-binding/) to catch up MediaNama’s coverage of SIM Binding. 

Prevention of template misuse to send spams: Among the other usescases, it said that their platform can prevent the potential misuse of pre-approved message templates by tele marketers. Businesses/Telemarketers have to register their message template on the TRAI-mandated DLT (Distributed Ledger Technology). 

  

For instance, one of such abuse is, a telemarketer can misuse a genuine service template to send out spammy promotional messages. Airtel wants to fix this by using the Airtel/telecom-side control layer on top. Although, it is not exactly clear how Aitel would prevent such misue.

  

According to the press release, a few problems that their system is trying to solve; 

- To reduce/remove the OTP dependency and prefer “silent authenticaiton”
    
- To fix the fragmented communication channels/tech-stack used by businesses. 
    
- To enable the businesses to know when a particular message/communication has successfully reached the intended user. 
    
- To prevent bot-driven campigns that can inflate business bills through international OTP traffic SMS pumping.
    

Who are the competitors to Airtel in this space? Through this, Airtel is now entering into Communication Platform as a Service (CPaaS and directly competing with like Indian and non-indian-based non-telecom companies including Twilio, Tanla, Route Mobile, AMD Telecom (Routee) and Netcore Cloud.

Airtel's direct telecom competitor Jio already has [JioCX](https://www.jio.com/business/services/jiocx/) as its own CPaaS platform. Similarly, Vodafone Idea (Vi) has [Vi Business](https://www.myvi.in/business/business-communications/cpaas-solutions). 

This can be integrated with business’ existing communication workflows or vendor ecosystems, and can be installed on an enterprise's own premises or hosted on Airtel's cloud as software-as-a-service. This is relevant if the regulated enterprises like BFSI are on board as customers. 

The press release leaves a lot of questions open. Some of them are: 

- Which network, device and usage signals does IQ Verify use, and how does it turn them into a score? The press release names the three categories (network, device and usage) signals but doesn’t detail how these signals are used to measure the decisions. 
    
- Is the scoring available only for Airtel subscribers, or for numbers on other networks too?
    
- How does Silent Authentication verify the SIM in the device, and does it work for users on other operators' networks?
    
- What happens when the fraud/spam score is wrong/false positive? Is there any grievance redressal mechanism for the flagged user or enterprise to contest it?
    
- The release says the platform is DPDP-compliant but what types/categories of data is stored specific to IQ Gateway? What is the data retention policy? Are there any use case limitations, like AI data training?
    

- Who were the banks and financial institutions in the trial deployments, how long did they run, and what results did they show on fraud reduction or billing savings?
    

We have sent these questions to Airtel seeking clarity, we will update the article once/if they respond to us.
