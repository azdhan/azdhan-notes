# Kerala HC seeks government response in petition challenging Bitchat blocking

- Kerala HC gives government four weeks to respond to challenge against Bitchat blocking
    

The Kerala High Court has reportedly directed the government to file its response within four weeks in a petition challenging the blocking of Bitchat in India, [SFLC.in said in a thread on X](https://x.com/SFLCin/status/2110290236496544120). SFLC.in said it "drafted the petition and represented the petitioner, Kerala-based Appropriate Technology Promotion Society is the petitioner of the case, according to [the thread](https://x.com/SFLCin/status/2110290236496544120).

Bitchat, an open-source messaging app that works over Bluetooth mesh without internet or cellular connectivity, was removed from Apple's India App Store earlier this month following a blocking order under Section 69A of the IT Act.

SFLC.in said the petition argues that the blocking "violates the rights to equality and freedom of speech and expression, as well as the principles of natural justice, procedural fairness and proportionality". The petition seeks "disclosure of the blocking order and its quashing."

The post also read that the petition challenges the blocking "for failure to follow the procedure prescribed under the Information Technology (Procedure and Safeguards for Blocking of Access of Information by Public) Rules, 2009".

SFLC.in said the app "subsequently became unavailable on @GooglePlay, and its website was inaccessible through several internet service providers in India", [per the thread](https://x.com/SFLCin/status/2110290242809024787). MediaNama had [reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-indias-bitchat-ban-highlights-two-key-issues/) that the government blocked Bitchat from the Apple App Store and the Google Play Store, and "apparently ordered ISPs to block its website".

  

What SFLC.in said earlier on the BitChat blocking order: In its [October 5 statement](https://sflc.in/sflc-in-condemns-the-blocking-of-bitchat-in-india/), SFLC.in said, "Dorsey's post suggests that the developers were neither heard nor served with a blocking order, and learned of the block only through Apple's notice." It was referring to the following post: 

[https://x.com/jack/status/2106359514391871540?s=20](https://x.com/jack/status/2106359514391871540?s=20) 

SFLC.in said "no blocking order for Bitchat is publicly available, and without it the specific grounds relied upon cannot be assailed". It added that "using Section 69A to block an entire application that enables communication is highly disproportionate."

On timing, SFLC.in said the block "immediately followed the internet shutdown imposed by the government on 2.10.2026 during a protest at Jantar Mantar", which it said "gives rise to a strong inference that the application may have been targeted because it enables communication during internet shutdowns and protests". It further said, "Enabling communication among protesters cannot, in any circumstance, be a valid ground for blocking an application under Article 19(2) or Section 69A."

SFLC.in said in its statement that Bitchat's popularity in India "surged during the repeated, unconstitutional internet shutdowns imposed by the government around Jantar Mantar, New Delhi, during the NEET UG protests in July 2026".

Background in the BitChat case: This is the second government action against Bitchat. On July 23, the Ministry of Home Affairs' Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) [issued a takedown order to GitHub](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-github-bitchat-home-ministry-order/) under Section 79(3)(b) of the IT Act, read with Rule 3(1)(d) of the IT Rules, 2021, directing it to remove or disable access to Bitchat repositories within three hours, MediaNama reported.

Furthermore, according to [documents reviewed by independent journalist Aditi Agarwal, who runs newsletter The Tech Trace](https://thetechtrace.substack.com/p/edition-28-the-indian-govts-crackdown-on-bluetooth-enabled-messaging-apps-that-wasnt), as [reported by MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-government-targets-bitchat-briar-bridgefy/), I4C also directed Google to remove Bitchat, Briar and Bridgefy from the Play Store, and Apple to remove Bitchat and Bridgefy from the App Store. Contrary to the takedown notice, Government Officials apparently instructed the companies the next day orally , on July 24, ‘not to enforce the takedown directions’, the report said.

Why the government ordered BitChat take down in the past: In July 2026, during the first major Jantar Mantar protests seeking the resignation of then Union Education Minsiter Dharmendra Pradhan, the blocking order noted that Bitchat allows anonymous communication "without mandatory user registration, phone number verification, or centralized logging", and that its architecture impedes lawful interception, [MediaNama reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-github-bitchat-home-ministry-order/). 

The order also said the platform could be misused to "continue communication during internet shutdowns or restrictions imposed by authorities", according to the same report.
