MIB asks ministries to setup quick response teams to counter 'fake content' 

Indian government has reportedly asked the ministries to set up a Quick Response Teams (QRTs) to counter the fake news on social media, according to PTI news repot, syndicated by T[he Hindu Business Line](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/govt-departments-setting-up-quick-response-teams-to-counter-misleading-fake-social-media-content/article71352322.ece) and [Business Standard.](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/govt-departments-form-qrts-to-counter-fake-misleading-social-media-content-126081600250_1.html)

The advisory directed the social media teams of various ministries to monitor the content related to the respective ministries and departments. As per this advisory, such 'fake, misleading, factually incorrect, manipulated, distorted, taken out of context, or likely to cause public confusion' should be reported to the QRTs. These QRTs should co-ordinate with Press Information Bureau's Fact Check Unit to issue a fact-checked information. 

**"Ideally, the response from the ministries should go in two hours of noting such content,"** said an anonymous government official, as quoted in PTI report. 

According to the July 27 MIB order, as seen by [The New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Jul/29/secretaries-told-to-check-fake-news-in-two-hours), Secretary Chanchal Kumar reportedly said that rumors on social media “travel fast” and therefore the government must act fast while countering such alleged false claims.  The directive also said that the fact-checked response must be limited to 200 words and the content must be cross-posted across platforms like Instagram and YouTube, using memes, posters, short videos for "algorithmic amplification." 

This development comes after the CJP Protests. During the CJP Protests, PM Modi asked his ministers to 'create' reels to reach the younger generation, including GenZ. 

**Why it matters:** Though this notice is from the Ministry of Information and thus falls under the coordination efforts with PIB's fact checking unit, the PIB's fact check unit is different from the Fact Check Unit (FCU) under the IT Rules, which was [struck down by Bombay HC in February 2024.](https://www.medianama.com/2024/02/223-deep-dive-bombay-high-court-verdict-indian-govt-fact-check-unit/)

Under the IT Rules, fact check unit is referenced in Rule 3(1)(b)(v), under the Part-2's intermediaries due diligence**.** It is a now-struck-down fact checking unit to fact-check information related to the "any business of the Central Government, " of the Central Government. 

_“The lack of definition of these words: business of the government; fake; false; and misleading makes the amendment both vague and overbroad. Anything might be the business of government. Anything could be said to be ‘fake’. ‘Misleading’ is entirely subjective. And as to ‘truth’ and ‘falsity’, throughout recorded human history there are few, if any, absolute truths. Perceptions, perspectives, possibilities, probabilities — all will to a greater or lesser extent colour what one chooses to believe or hold or chooses not to believe or hold. The assumption that there are absolute truths to even the business of government, even if we knew what that included and what it did not, is unsubstantiated.” --  [Justice G.S. Patel (January 2024)](https://www.medianama.com/2024/02/223-deep-dive-bombay-high-court-verdict-indian-govt-fact-check-unit/#:~:text=his%20concluding%20remarks%2C-,Justice%20Patel,-observed%3A)_

Later in March 2026, the Supreme Court of India refused to strike down the 2024's Bombay HC's order. The SC also noted that the IT Rules hasn't clearly defined what "misleading" content.
