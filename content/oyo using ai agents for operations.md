# The backend operations of OYO’s Prism are run by AI agents: Ritesh Agarwal at GFF 2026

“Oyo’s Prism today is run by agents. Our accounts receivable, accounts payable, treasury, taxation, all of those are being decided, delivered by agents around the world. Because that's the only way you can run a 35, 40 country business, because you have the general intelligence of AI that you do not need now a consultant for everything, at least the basic things,” said Ritesh Agarwal, Founder and Group CEO of Prism, the parent company of OYO, during the recent Global Fintech Fest ([GFF](https://www.globalfintechfest.com/agenda)) 2026

"Within our engineering, one year ago, I used to speak to our engineers about seeing what percentage of our code would be written by AI... But now with loop engineering, you are launching new versions almost entirely by the machines, which is almost like a cottage industry going from factories to autonomous factories." - Ritesh Agarwal, OYO.

Over two weeks before the GFF 2026, Prism’s financial report revealed that the company intends to use AI agents for end-to-end operations “without a human triggering each step”. [Read about their GM Agent here.](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-oyo-prism-ai-agent-hotel-operations/) 

Data structuring of human emotions for better AI agentic decisions: Ritesh Agarwal suggested that structured data feeding of decisions taken from human perspectives could help agents to make better decisions. 

“We're working on structured data. Because humans see images, videos, and content, they're more influenced by emotion as much as content and structured data. But what we're starting to see with agents is they are relatively unemotional when they have to make a decision. And hence, we have to feed it with data that it can use to make a decision. And hence, we have to feed it with data that it can use to make a decision.”

Agentic productivity is ahead of agentic commerce: He also stated that “agentic commerce, for us at least, has been very nascent.” Addressing agentic commerce, he said he is hopeful that it is just a “matter of time.”

“Agentic productivity has been very advanced. I think the benefits of agentic productivity has been unbelievable, which is we are starting to see hotels' entire white-collar labor delivering their services by means of agents in the back end. But that's on the productivity side.” - Ritesh Agarwal

AI agents could fill the demand-supply gap of human labor: "It is certainly more meaningful in higher per capita jurisdictions than not. But that doesn't mean it is irrelevant…. But if you have countries where the demand for labor exceeds the supply, you don't have a choice, you've got to do what it has to do. So agents suddenly become access for more productivity," he added.  

Maintaining humans in the loop while reducing the human involvement: Agarwal said humans still need to be in the loop given how often edge cases arise in AI agent-driven decisions.

“Human in the loop is going to be a very relevant part of agentic productivity, as well as agentic commerce. The edge cases could be 5% or 40%, so it’s a journey in terms of when you can reduce the human involvement.” - Ritesh Agarwal

Fragmented tech stack in budget and mid-market hotels: Agarwal said the company operates in the two-to-three-star segment (OYO, Townhouse) and four-star segment (Sunday Hotels), where hotel owners typically have to run separate, disconnected systems.

“The current technologies are not full-stack in nature. The hotel owner has to have a different property management software, a different channel manager, a different dynamic pricing system, a different card acceptance system. And if they don’t talk to each other, you have to have people to manage those guests,” - Ritesh Agarwal
