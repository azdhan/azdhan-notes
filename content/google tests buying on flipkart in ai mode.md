# Google Tests "Buy" Button for Flipkart Products in Gemini and AI Mode in India: What We Know and What We Don't

What we don’t know about AI shopping on Flipkart through Google’s Gemini and AI Mode.

  

# Google tests AI shopping on Flipkart through Gemini and AI Mode. Here’s what we don’t know

Google is testing a way for some users in India to buy products from Walmart-owned Flipkart without leaving Gemini or AI Mode, [TechCrunch reporte](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/26/google-tests-buying-from-walmart-owned-flipkart-through-gemini-and-ai-mode-in-india/), citing people familiar with the matter and an experience it saw. Users in the test see a "Buy" button on select Flipkart listings, which opens a Flipkart checkout flow inside the Google’s AI interface. Amazon listings showed up alongside Flipkart's but did not offer direct purchase, it reported.

It also reported that this feature could go live at/before Big Billion Days, which begins on October 9th. A Google spokesperson reportedly told TechCrunch that the company is "always testing new features and experiences", that it regularly runs experiments, and that it had no further details to share. Flipkart did not immediately respond to TechCrunch's request for comment.

In January 2026, Google announced the [Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP)](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/agentic-commerce-ai-tools-protocol-retailers-platforms/) as an open standard that lets AI agents work with retailers across the shopping journey, including checkout. [MediaNama reported at the time](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-google-agentic-commerce-protocol-e-commerce-user-behaviour/) that Google said that Flipkart backed the protocol.

Google's test comes after OpenAI tried-and-scaled-back, checkout inside ChatGPT: OpenAI launched its [Agentic Commerce Protocol](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-google-agentic-commerce-protocol-e-commerce-user-behaviour/) with Stripe in September 2025, starting with single-item purchases from US Etsy sellers. In March, [The Information reported](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/openai-scales-back-shopping-plans-chatgpt) that OpenAI was dropping the plan to let users buy from product listings in ChatGPT search results and would focus on checkouts inside specific apps that plug into ChatGPT. The reasons cited were that few merchants were selling through the checkout, and that users researched products in ChatGPT but did not buy them there. 

Product and price data is not ready for agents. The Information reported that ChatGPT had to interpret pricing and stock information that was often ambiguous and spread across several systems, with the risk of charging the wrong price or ordering an item that was out of stock. In an earlier essay on [why commerce isn't ready for AI](https://isreasoned.substack.com/p/why-commerce-isnt-ready-for-ai-yet), Pahwa wrote: "Agents don't simplify commerce: they force it to be explicit." In the March piece, he called the dozen live merchants "a data readiness problem, not a product problem."

The second digitisation demands that products should be unambiguous: legible not just to people, but to machines that must reason, compare, substitute, and act, or even invoke in chat. When interpretation and execution are a part of the same system, ambiguity becomes a systems problem. Autonomous agents with agency only increase the scale of errors that already exist.” – [Nikhil Pahwa](https://www.reasoned.live/p/why-commerce-isnt-ready-for-ai-yet)

Read Nikhil Pahwa’s brief list of things the AI agents might need to finish to complete a purchase [here](https://www.reasoned.live/p/why-commerce-isnt-ready-for-ai-yet).

Merchants could have no reason to pay extra for AI-shopping, if charged: Since the financial terms of the deal are not official yet, we are not sure if the users/flipkart or businesses would be charged for the purchases made available via Gemini or AI mode. For instance, The Information [reported](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/openai-scales-back-shopping-plans-chatgpt) that OpenAI planned to charge merchants up to 4% on completed purchases through Instant Checkout, roughly 7% once card fees and taxes are included. Nikhil Pahwa rightly [argued](https://www.reasoned.live/p/why-commerce-isnt-ready-for-ai-yet#:~:text=MDR\)%20and%20taxes.-,Why,-will%20merchants%20sign) that why would a businessman pay extra while the alternative (the traditional buying methods) already exists and works. 

Similarly, the users also might not have an incentive to rely on the purchases via Google’s Gemini or AI mode unless the AI systems takes away all the user’s shopping friction away and make the purchase successful despite the hurdles and keep the whole process transparent and trustable. 

The price a user sees is not always the price they may pay: For instance, in July, when Pahwa asked his AI agent to [buy a pack of Pears soa](https://www.reasoned.live/p/what-i-learned-when-my-ai-agent-tried)p. It showed showed a base price of Rs 411, with promotional messaging suggesting an effective price of around Rs 315. That lower number depended on payment instruments (like credit cards, preferred UPI app etc) and conditional offers, including UPI or wallet cashbacks. "An agent cannot optimise for 'lowest price' unless it knows which offers apply to that user," he wrote. Eventually, the lowest price was available on other platform, despite the discounted price shown on initial listing.

So, what are the safety and transparent frameworks that Google and Flipkart can employ that instill trust among the users?

Why Google-Flipkart AI-buying deal might raise competition concerns: In December 2025, [IndiaMART moved the Calcutta High Court against OpenAI](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-explained-indiamart-openai-lawsuit-visibility-ai-driven-web/), alleging that ChatGPT selectively excluded it from AI-generated responses while surfacing rival B2B marketplaces.  Later, the [HC ruled in the favour of OpenAI](https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-calcutta-hc-rejects-indiamart-plea-against-chatgpt-link-exclusion/)

Since, Flipkart is a marketplace, [similar concerns could be raised by businesses](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-google-agentic-commerce-protocol-e-commerce-user-behaviour/) who might feel/expererience the possible selective discrimination. 

What we don’t know yet:

- The tech behind the AI-shopping. TechCrunch says it is unclear what powers this potential Google-Flipkart deal. We do not know whether it runs on UCP/ACP.
    
- We also do not know whether the checkout happens entirely inside Gemini/AI Mode or sends the user to Flipkart's site or app with less/no human intervention.
    
- The mode of payments. Recently, Swiggy added wallet money for agentic transactions via its MCP. How would Google design the payment system?
    
- The commercial terms between Google and Flipkart, including any fee or commission, given [Google's investment and stake in Flipkart.](https://www.medianama.com/2024/05/223-google-becomes-minority-investor-in-flipkart/)
    
- Whether other marketplaces like Amazon, Meesho, Myntra and IndiaMart will be integrated later and if other retailers can get the equal treatment. 
    
- How would the returns/refunds be handled in case of disputes? Who will handle returns, refunds, cancellations and complaints for a purchase made inside Gemini or AI Mode.
    

MediaNama has written to Google and Flipkart with these questions and will update this article when they respond.
