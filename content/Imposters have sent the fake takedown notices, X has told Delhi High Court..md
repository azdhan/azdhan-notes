   **# Impostors sent fake Delhi Police takedown notices against IFF, SFLC.in; X tells Delhi HC 

X Delhi High Court that their internal investigation has found someone impersonating Delhi Police to send takedown notices against the Internet Freedom Foundation (IFF) and Software Freedom Law Center (SFLC.in). It also said that it has filed a complaint with Delhi Police against the impostor, according to the court proceedings, attended by MediaNama.

What X told the court:

Appearing before Justice Amit Mahajan, X's counsel said the platform had not acted on the notices. "We received the notice. In terms of transparency, we told them that we have received this. We didn't take action," the counsel said.

"It turns out this was somebody playing an impostor, somebody impersonating Delhi Police who has sent this… We are not actioning this request because it's an illegal request. It's not from Delhi Police. We filed a complaint last night with Delhi Police."

X's counsel asked the court to also note that X had approached Delhi Police to investigate the impersonation.An advocate representing Delhi Police also attended the hearing but hasn't made any submissions.

What IFF and SFLC.in argued:

Senior Advocate Akhil Sibal appeared for both organisations and told the court they had doubted the notices' authenticity and had written to the authorities for confirmation. "But if it is genuine, My Lords, it's most troubling," he said.

He read out X's message to the petitioners, dated November 3 that read: "In the interest of transparency, we are writing to inform you that X has received requests from the Delhi Police regarding your X account."

The attached "Receipt of illegal content report" named the Special Commissioner of Police (Crime), Delhi Police, as the sender. Sibal said SFLC.in had emailed the address on the notice asking whether it was genuine, and the email "bounced back".

He also said the legal notice sent in the name of the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) carried no details of a lawyer and no signature. The notices accused the organisations of defaming the RSS under Section 356 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), which covers criminal defamation. Sibal told the court that defamation is "non-cognizable", and rhetorically asked how the police could send such a notice.

Sibal told the court that Delhi Police had not said anything in the matter. Before the hearing, as noted by MediaNama, IFF and SFLC said that they filed the writ petition at Delhi HC considering the no response from Delhi Polcie. 

After X stated that he notices were infact fake and impersonated, Sibal told the court that X's statement could be recorded and the petitions disposed of. The court recorded X's statement and closed the petitions. According to [IFF](https://x.com/internetfreedom/status/2118582685694406744), the court gave both organisations liberty to approach it afresh if any action is taken.

The online impersonation and the free speech problem: 

While the use of email identities that appear to belong to Delhi Police is threatening and bizarre, this impersonation issue also pertains to the misuse of the copyright tools of these intermediary social media platforms. Some entities impersonate creators, either claiming that content uploaded by the original creator belongs to them or claiming it belongs to someone else. 

The [Delhi High Court is examining the copyright tools of intermediaries](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-metas-copyright-delhi-hc-examine/). MediaNama has also [documented](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-metas-copyright-delhi-hc-examine/) instances of fake copyright notices issued to content creators, especially concerning critical speech, political speech, and speech about billionaire Anant Ambani.
