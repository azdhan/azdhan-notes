# Iran's cyberattack on UK's power plant is a warning India shouldn't ignore


Reportedly Iran-linked hackers shut down a small British power plant for four days, the first confirmed case of Tehran-affiliated attackers forcing a UK facility to close, reported UK's Telegraph. UK Officials called it the most successful cyberattack of its kind in the country.

UK's Telegraph reported that the power plant’s name was withheld for security reasons. However, the UK government seems to have clarified that Its size meant the shutdown did not affect the UK’s broader power supply or energy production. Later, the government briefed power company leaders and sent guidance to businesses on how to respond.

The incident was reported to the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), part of GCHQ (Government Communications Headquarters) is a British intelligence and security agency. 

**Why attacker attribution is extremely difficult in cyber warfare:** "Attributing cyber attacks to specific entities or nation states is extremely difficult, because cyber attacks can be launched by compromised devices spread across various countries, as well as via proxies. Cyber attacks are something that no one claims credit for. An attack on a nuclear installation, would be an act of war under normal circumstances, but what what if no one knows where the cyber attack came from?" wrote Nikhil Pahwa in his 2019's piece on ["How India should deal with cyber attacks on critical infrastructure"](https://www.medianama.com/2019/10/223-editorial-cyber-warfare-critical-infrastructure/)
Some of the instances when India's Critical infrastructure was cyber-attacked: 
- In 2020, China-linked cyber-group named RedEcho reportedly [targeted](https://www.recordedfuture.com/research/redecho-targeting-indian-power-sector) India’s critical power and maritime infrastructure during the 2020 border standoff. While s[ome media reports](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/mumbai-power-outage-in-october-may-have-had-chinese-hand-study-2380925) suggested a link to the October 2020 Mumbai blackout, the linkage is still unproven officially. Read its full report here: [PDF](https://assets.recordedfuture.com/insikt-report-pdfs/2021/cta-2021-0228.pdf)
- In October 2019, a malware attack on the internet-connected network of the Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant exposed severe vulnerabilities in India’s power infrastructure, a situation that initially prompted [denials and delayed disclosures](https://www.medianama.com/2019/10/223-kundankulam-nuclear-power-plant-cyber-attack/) from the concerned officials. 
- In July 2026, data belonging to Reliance, a contractor associated with the Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant, was [reportedly leaked](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-reliance-data-breach-kudankulam-files-dark-web/) on the dark web. Later, Reliance confirmed a “partial breach” of data on a server hosted by third-party provider Mumbai-based Yotta.





**Why private sector is also vulnerable than the government-controlled critical infrastructure:** "Most importantly, the private sector plays a major role in cybersecurity at multiple levels. Many of designated critical sectors such as transport that covers airports and airlines, shipping, trains, and roadways are manned by private corporations. The increasing privatisation of many utility services in banking, power and others has led to expanding the threat landscape much beyond government entities," [wrote](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/opinion/digital-india-act-heres-how-it-should-fix-indias-cybersecurity-weaknesses-11038701.html) the trio of NANDKUMAR SARAVADE & SAIKAT DATTA & ANAND VENKATANARAYANAN for MoneyControl in 2023, commenting on then proposed Digital India Act. \

"You can't use digital technology supremacy, it's just cyber, in order to defeat a regime, a nation-state, should have humongous technological capability and people capability," said Anand VENKATANARAYANAN in a small webinar ahead the release of his co-authored book, The Power of UncertAInty.  He also stated that "the Indian state cybersecurity capabilities are non-existent at the best." 

"You have to stop thinking sovereignty as moments. Digital sovereignty, AI sovereignty, all that stuff that you hear about in newspapers and big business guys and stuff people talking about is actually a mirage. They don't exist. So instead you have to start thinking about sovereignty as capabilities. Unless you think about sovereignty as capabilities, unless you think sovereignty as supply chain that you have control over, the damn thing doesn't mean anything. That's the core hypothesis of the book that sovereignty is supply chain capability. You do not own the supply chain, you don't have sovereignty." - Anand V. 

**Why Public-private partnership might solve this problem:** "Given the mutual distrust and vulnerability of public and private sector, the solution involves taking a shared responsibility approach through a public-private partnership for critical infrastructure protection. These should focus on building an institutional framework, expanding and deepening capacity, creating security standards and strict audits and evolving a cybersecurity incident reporting framework," [wrote](https://www.gatewayhouse.in/cyber-attacks-on-critical-infrastructure-is-india-ready/) Sameer Patil, the current Director for the Centre for Security, Strategy and Technology at the Observer Research Foundation. Hindustan Times published his op-ed first.
