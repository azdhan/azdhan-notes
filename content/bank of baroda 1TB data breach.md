India's second-largest public sector bank, Bank of Baroda (BOB), had a data breach of 1 Terabite (TB), and the compromised data is still publicly accessible on the Tor network. This was first [reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-bank-of-baroda-forensic-probe-customer-data-dark-web/) on July 27. 

Why does the data leak still matter?

- While the 1 TB database is still publicly accessible, MediaNama could access the database through Tor network in less than three minutes.
    
- The onion website is not password protected and doesn't have a paywall.
    
- Therefore, as of now, the data is publicly available to anyone who has access to the onion URL. MediaNama will not expose the URLs of such data leaks to prevent further spread of the data that has already leaked. 
    
- Interestingly, the threat group that leaked the data hasn't demanded any ransom from BoB.
    

What was Bank of Baroda's response after the breach? In a statement to the media, BoB [said](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-bank-of-baroda-forensic-probe-customer-data-dark-web/) that the breach resulted in the “unauthorised access to certain data” and claimed that attackers did not access its core banking systems. It also said that it had '​started a forensic investigation and was working with relevant ​authorities.' The full statement is at the end of this article. 

Passwords and sensitive data ‘may’ not have leaked: Srikanth L, who runs [Cashless Consumer](https://bankin-report.cashlessconsumer.in/), also said in his [report](https://bobbreach.cashlessconsumer.in/pages/overview.html#overview) that "the dump does NOT appear to include direct access to Finacle (core banking system)" He further said that "account passwords and sensitive transaction credentials may not be directly exposed." Explore Srikanth’s detailed report [here](https://bobbreach.cashlessconsumer.in/pages/overview.html#overview). 

Bank of Baroda's statement in full: "The Bank has robust information security protocols in place. The incident involved compromise of an employee's email account, resulting in unauthorised access to certain data. The matter was promptly identified, and immediate containment measures were implemented. The Bank's core banking systems were not accessed and continue to remain secure.

A comprehensive forensic investigation has beeninitiated, and the Bank is working closely with the relevant authorities in accordance with applicable regulatory requirements.

The Bank remains committed to maintaining the highest standards of information security and to safeguarding the trust of its customers and stakeholders." [This is the URL](https://x.com/bankofbaroda/status/2081688535766368613?s=20) of their post. 

  
  
  
  
  
  
  

The leaked data also included the invoice/bills. For instance, MediaNama accessed one of such bills that belonged to Hyderabad’s Maredpalley regional branch. The bill had the expenses of air conditioners, refrigerators, two-wheelers and cars etc totalling upto 85 lakh rupees. 

  
  

- Sensitive users data enrolled in government schemes: We found two folders with 950 and 9759 plain text files that had the data related to people’s eligibility status for Jansuraksha a.k.a Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY). It is a government-backed, one-year term life insurance scheme in India. The data included names, phone numbers, region, branch names, and their eligibility status. 
    
- Sensitive user data who enrolled in “UIDAI Certification” course: The leaked database exposed the sensitive PII data of candidates enrolled in “Introduction to Aadhaar Authentication and Seeding” course at various companies, like Pune-based VisionIndia, Gujrat InfoTech, Center for Development Orientation and training (NGO) and other 21 entities. All the data in this folder belongs to the Gujrat region across various districts. 
    
- NPCI letter levying fine on Bank of Baroda for non-compliance of device binding: MediaNama accessed a NPCI’s letter dated June 5 2026, communicating the penalty of Rs. 3,78,957 levied for “non-compliance to device binding guidelines” at com.bankofbaroda.upi app ID that reportedly resulted in the ‘fraudulent transactions.’ According to the letter the amount lost to fraud is similar to the penalty levied. A simple google search regarding this didn’t show any reveant reporting. 
    
- Manappuram home finance data: PAN details and passport numbers of Manappuram Home Finance ltd’s directors in Kerala’s Ernakulam were exposed through a 2025’s document. MHFL’s few communications between Bajaj Finserv were also part of leaked data among others. 
    
- Tax invoices and bills: For instance, we accessed a nine-page tax voice billed to Pune-based VisionIndia. Some of the contents included “charges for SMS alert, NEFT Transcations, Aadhaar and mobile number seeding” among various other contents. We also found an invoice to Indore-based NICT technologies for similar purposes. 
    
- Everyday operations realted data: For instance, we found access to 2024 HR department letters to install QR codes for consumer feedback. The leaked database also had photoshop files (.psd formats) and design creatives.
