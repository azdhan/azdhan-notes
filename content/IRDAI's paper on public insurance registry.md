# Lowdown: Inside IRDAI's consultation paper on the Public Insurance Registry

India's insurance regulator, Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) has called for a "Public Insurance Registry” in its recent  [consultation paper](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%A3-%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%A7%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%82-%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%B0-%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B6-%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0-%E0%A4%AD%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%97-1-_-Consultation-paper-on-Distribution-Reforms-Part-1.pdf), titled “Recalibrating Economics of Insurance Distribution.” It referred to this as Digital Public Infrastrcutre (DPI) body as an interoperable business-facing digital infrastructure for insurance. 

Based on the Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025, IRDAI released a [consultation paper](https://iib.gov.in/pir) on "Public Insurance Registry” in August 2026 whose consultation period is ending on September 30, 2026. This shared DPI registry for insurance data aims to make policies portable, claims faster, and the insurance market transparent.

How would a PIR would look and function in real life? The IRDAI says that PRI will work as a shared data registry that can run through the [Insurance Information Bureau (IIB),](https://iib.gov.in/) an autonomous independent body established in Hyderabad in [2009](https://joinditto.in/articles/general/insurance-information-bureau-of-india-iib/). It also said that there is a plan to turn it into a not-for-profit company under IRDAI with different regulations.

The document listed over 50 scenarios where the PIR could be useful. A few exerpts from that list are: 

- Insurance Risk Score for risk underwriting: “PIR may enable an Insurance Risk Score (IRS) to be a unified, consent-based risk score synthesizing insurance history (IIB), credit (Credit Information Companies (CICs)), and other permitted external data sources — as a decision-support input at underwriting.” 
    
- Auto-deactivation of Aadhaar for the deceased: “PIR can enable structured sharing of validated death events, assisting in timely updating of UIDAI and civil registration records more efficiently, improve data quality, and reduce the risk of misuse of Aadhaar and other identity-linked services.”
    
- Data could be shared with law enforcement authorities: “PIR may connect directly to public transport and law enforcement databases to track recovered property. The moment such vehicles or assets reappear in a government registry where the claim has been paid by an insurer, PIR can flag it and update the insurer, cutting down on financial losses.” 
    
- Data could be shared with Minsitry of Health, Family and Welfare: “PIR can enable collation and analysis of anonymized claims data linked to disease and treatment classifications and unique lives, providing complementary insights including incidence of disease outbreaks in specific regions to the Ministry.”
    
- As a fundatuional back-end service to the insurers: “PIR is envisaged to provide a common foundation and back-end service to insurers, Bima Bharosa, and other public grievance redressal platforms like Centralized Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS) for standardized and integrated grievance management, besides root cause analytics.”
    

What are the problems that the Public Insurance Registry is trying to solve? 

The Public and consumers: 

- The discovery issue increases intermediary dependence: The paper claims that due to asymmetrical information for "like-for-like comparison difficult and increases dependence on intermediaries, particularly for complex products that combine protection, savings, annuity or investment features."
    
- Lack of information access may lead to falling prey to sales narratives: The paper argues that selecting a suitable product according to the user requirement may be being shaped by the sales narratives and not from a informed data-backed decisions. 
    
- Lack of eagle view of all insurances: It further says that the policyholders lack the "consolidated view of their insurance relationships across life, health, motor and other products" and therefore increases the friction. 
    

“PIR will facilitate transparency in insurance product offerings and performance of insurers, intermediaries and salespersons by placing this information as a public good. By promoting such clarity and responsiveness, the PIR will enhance overall customer experience and confidence in the insurance ecosystem.” 

#### Government Departments & Agencies

- No eagle view of insurance coverage: It says that government and its agencies has "limited visibility into insurance coverage, gaps and programme outcomes across farmers, MSMEs, rural communities, unorganised workers, productive assets and priority sectors."
    
- Insurance data is not structured for policy use: "claims, losses, disease burden, hospitalization patterns, crop losses, asset protection and catastrophe outcomes are not available in a structured and usable form."
    
- Poor coordination with public registries: "Limited coordination between insurance systems and public registries also affects areas such as uninsured vehicle identification, mandatory motor insurance enforcement, public programme monitoring and disaster-risk planning"
    

“PIR will reduce data silos, improve interoperability, and lowers integration costs, enabling faster product development and more efficient operations. Standardized insurance data infrastructure would encourage innovation across the insurance ecosystem. This supports the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and advanced analytics paving the way for responsible innovation.”

For Regulators/IRDAI: 

- Asymmetrical and inconsistent information in whole insurance markets: "Data on coverage, protection gaps, customer outcomes, market conduct and emerging risks is dispersed across insurers, products, distribution channels, regulatory returns, public disclosures, grievance systems, Ombudsman records and consumer-dispute forums. Differences in definitions, formats, reporting frequency and data quality reduce comparability and make it difficult to identify underserved segments, recurring customer issues, systemic conduct concerns and areas requiring market-development intervention."
    
- Lack of cross-market data visibility impacts the risk assessments: "Data on coverage, protection gaps, customer outcomes, market conduct and emerging risks is dispersed across insurers, products, distribution channels, regulatory returns, public disclosures, grievance systems, Ombudsman records and consumer-dispute forums. Differences in definitions, formats, reporting frequency and data quality reduce comparability and make it difficult to identify underserved segments, recurring customer issues, systemic conduct concerns and areas requiring market-development intervention."
    

“PIR will simplify regulatory reporting. The registry will provide regulators with near realtime visibility into market activity, enabling more effective supervision and evidence-based regulation. Automated reporting, standardized data formats, and improved data quality would reduce compliance costs for insurers while strengthening regulatory monitoring.”

For Insurers and intermediaries: 

- Insurers, reinsurers and intermediaries lack of market-wide visibility into "unserved and underserved segments, protection gaps, product performance, customer needs, emerging risks and claims experience" and suchh incomplete risk-related information leading to their underwriting, pricing, claims and portfolio decisions.
    

- Know Your Customer (KYC) and customer verification might be triggered multiple times causing operational costs for insurers and impacting customer experience. 
    

“PIR will improve understanding of unserved and underserved markets by making available standardized market-wide information. It will also help insurers assess and price risks better. Thus, the PIR supports financial sustainability for insurers and intermediaries by helping them grow the business, sell efficiently and underwrite better. “

#### Financial Institutions (Banks, NBFCs, Credit Bureaus, Rating Agencies) 

- Insurance rarely usable as collateral: "Life insurance policies are not widely used as collateral because lenders lack a standardized mechanism to verify ownership, policy status, assignment, encumbrance and enforceability. Similarly, lenders have limited visibility into whether financed assets, MSME businesses, projects or borrowers are adequately insured against relevant risks."
    
- Insurance information is also underused in credit assessment and risk monitoring: Since insurance information is not in standarized form, it argues that "this can reduce the ability of lenders, investors and rating agencies to assess borrower resilience, collateral protection, risk transfer and portfolio vulnerability." 
    

#### “PIR will assist in better underwriting and risk pricing. Significant improvements in risk assessment and underwriting would accrue from the availability of verified historical policy and claims information. PIR would strengthen fraud prevention and risk reduction.”

For Researchers, Academia, Public Policy Institutes: It states that there is no research-ready anonymised, aggregated, standardised insurance data for evidence-based policy studies.  

“PIR can enable access to anonymized data with appropriate aggregation as a public good for research and policy analysis. Researchers can generate deeper insights into protection gaps, risk patterns, customer outcomes, financial resilience, and emerging trends.”

Guiding data principles: The consultation paper also lists nine guiding principles, which include’s PIR’s operational and data handling obligations. Some of them are: 

- No data centralisation; original owners holds the data, not PIR. Insurers, distributors and other authorities keep owning their records. It states that PIR keeps no full source documents centrally
    
- Data minimization: It states that data sets will onlyu have what the user needs require.. Data is held at the coarsest useful level: a broad category instead of a clinical code, a banded amount instead of an exact figure.
    
- Tokenizing personal identifiers in a secured vault: PIR tokenizes every personal identifier when it arrives. A vault for the encrypted data has been contemplated.
    
- Privacy risk assessment for anonymized data: Before each data release, PIR proposes to check that policyholders and insurers cannot be re-identified from the output. It would review the assessment periodically as data volumes and techniques change.
    
- Standardised formats for data submissions: PIR would publish submission standards for each repository, covering format, mandatory fields and internal consistency.
    

Why it matters: The paper floats the Insurance Risk Score that would combine insurance history, credit bureau data and "other permitted external data sources" into a single consent-based score for underwriting purposes. It can be algorithmic profiling of citizens through their insurance/financial records.

Every critique that applies to a CIBIL score applies here too, like opaque methodology, weak dispute mechanisms, one figure determining a complicated financial behaviour. Hence, atleast the Insurance Risk Score shuld have more in-built transparency.

- Who exactly computes the score, and who audits them? 
    
- Can a consumer demand an explanation or challenge the number? What is the transparency mechanism?
    
- What are "other permitted external data sources"?
    
- Will this score be made mandatory for insurers and users? Will be there be alternative routes if a user doesn’t consent for this score?
    
- Can it be used/misused beyond underwriting by insurers/financial institutions, like by general lenders, employers, landlords?
    

These questions are important because without a clearly defined use-case limitation, the IRS becomes a general-purpose financial-behaviour signal for anyone with access to the registry. 

Moreover, the same architecture that makes PIR useful is what makes it potentially tool that can be misused because of the nature of data consolidation of variety of types. For instance, IRDAI proposes that the PRI will hold: a tokenised identity for every policyholder, cross-insurer policy and claim references, consent records, grievance logs, intermediary conduct records, and an anomaly-signal engine. 

It further dates that PRI could be linked to other databases like VAHAN, e-Detailed Accident Report (e-DAR), CKYC, UIDAI and natural catastrophe databases. And, PIR may surface "anomaly signals" and share them with law enforcement authorities like prevention of money laundering (PMLA) authorities.  

Moreover, once such a centralised database-level architecture exists, there could be [pressure](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/national-intelligence-grid-slowly-gathers-pace-receives-45k-requests-a-month/article70369184.ece/amp/) to use it for tax or law enforcement, welfare or policy eligibility, and everything that might be related to the financial health might be relying on this score and data. 

The usage of settled death claims as a trigger for Aadhaar deactivation sits in a grey zone because it depends on based on who requests/calls for it, under what authority/proof and the validated authenticity of the proof. 

The paper asks stakeholders, in Section 15, what approach PIR should adopt "to progress towards formal notification as a Significant Data Fiduciary." Considering the vastness of the data that will be collected, the data fiduciary obligations are worthy to look it as it gives certain legally mandated rights as well as few more additional data protection obligations.
