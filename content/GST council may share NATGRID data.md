# GST Council may consider letting NATGRID agencies access taxpayer data

The GST Council is likely to consider a proposal at its upcoming meeting that would let central intelligence and investigative agencies, and State and Union Territory police forces that use NATGRID, access specified GST-related taxpayer information, an anonymous senior government official told [BusinessLine](https://claude.ai/chat/ADD_BUSINESSLINE_STORY_URL).

Under the proposal, GST data could be shared online through application programming interfaces (APIs) that would give agencies on NATGRID a more structured way to get disaggregated GST data. 

Five categories of data that could be shared:

- “registration details, including proprietors and date of commencement of business;
    
- e-way bill details (document [required](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/electronic-way-bill-050819.pdf) for movement of goods); 
    
- inward and outward supplies over a specified period; 
    
- branch mapping through PAN for multi-branch companies; and 
    
- supplies made through e-commerce platforms.”
    

Without specifying in detail, the Business Line copy noted that this system could have some safeguard limitations. Separately, the Council may also consider allowing the Statistics Ministry to access certain taxpayer data.  

What is NATGRID: The National Intelligence Grid ([NATGRID](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-natgrid-new-hirings-surveillance-evolution-timeline/)) is a union government platform that lets security agencies pull data from multiple government and private databases. Formulated after the 26/11 Mumbai attacks, it is meant for a single-point mega database to deal with counter-terrorism and national security, which includes internal and economic security. 

NATGRID is also outside the ambit of the RTI Act. It was [added to the Act's second schedule](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-natgrid-npr-database-privacy-law-enforcement/) in 2011, which exempts intelligence and security organisations. 

Some of the recent NATGRID-related developments: 

- In December 2025, the Ministry of Home Affairs [told](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-natgrid-npr-database-privacy-law-enforcement/) the Lok Sabha that an Organised Crime Network Database is being developed on NATGRID’s IT platform for intelligence-sharing between the National Investigative Agency (NIA) and State Anti-Terror Squads.
    
- In February 2026, the Enforcement Directorate [asked its zonal heads](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-ed-natgrid-chargesheet-target-fy26-500-prosecution-complaints/) to increase the use of NATGRID and Financial Intelligence Networ. 
    
- In June 2026, the CISF [proposed a data fusion centre](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-cisf-facial-recognition-cameras-airports-natgrid/) to connect facial recognition cameras at major airports with NATGRID.
    

Officials are already accessing sensitive and non-sensitive citizen data with around 45,000 requests per month: The Hindu also [reported](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/national-intelligence-grid-slowly-gathers-pace-receives-45k-requests-a-month/article70369184.ece) that NATGRID was receiving around 45,000 requests a month. The data agencies can request is classified as non-sensitive, sensitive and highly sensitive, with tax records, bank statements, financial transactions and export-import data in the highly sensitive category, according to the same report.
