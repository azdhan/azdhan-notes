# Why an ISP-level ban on ‘obscene content’ cannot work the way Uday Mahurkar proposes

  

Former Central Information Commissioner Uday Mahurkar said he has written to Prime Minister Narendra Modi seeking the enactment of three new laws to curb what he described as a "horrendous situation" created by obscene online content.

  

Releasing the letter at a press conference, Mahurkar said the proliferation of such content was linked to crimes against women and required legislative intervention. These are the three laws he proposed: 

  

- “Immediate ban on Pornography for minors as in 16 countries.”
    
- “A law disallowing internet service providers from allowing obscene content from their internet connections using AI, on the pain of punishment.”
    
- “Amendment of Indecent Representation of Women (Prohibition) Act, 1986, & increasing the jail term to 10 years with no bail for 3 years & stringent implementation of the law.”
    

  

Why this matters: The attempts to regulate online content, [especially pornographic content have always been an issue](https://www.medianama.com/2015/09/223-porn-ban-sc-womens-lawyer-association-moves-apex-court-to-block-all-porn-sites/). What makes Mahurkar’s comments worth noting is the nature and the impossibility of such a ask, when viewed with what is happening around content and platform regulation in India.. 

  

While the ban on pornography on [certain websites](https://www.medianama.com/2022/10/223-india-dot-orders-blocking-porn-websites/) and platforms ([like X](https://www.deccanherald.com/technology/it-rules-2021-x-geo-blocks-porn-content-in-india-after-govt-heat-3918781)) is already in place, irrespective of age of the user, the ask for a law to stop an ISP from “allowing obscene content from their internet connections using AI” would be impossible to implement, unless they had [“actual knowledge”](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/110813550/) of the existence of such content, the standard that governs intermediary liability under Section 79 of the IT Act. If not, Uday Mahurkar’s idea of AI-powered ISP-level content regulation would lead to:

- Blocking the whole website/domain and thereby depriving the other legitimate services that are available on the same AI platform. Or, 
    
- Breaking the encrypted internet and thereby exposing all the information/content that is travels through the data packets over ISPs.  
    

  

Speaking to MediaNama, Karan Saini, a network researcher said that the internet service providers (ISPs) wouldn’t know that the user is transferring through their networks and hence it can’t moderate/block what it doesn’t know/see. 

  

“No ISP or anyone with network access, where the prompts or data either go through the URL or in the post-data, and all of that is [mostly] encrypted. It goes against the basic idea. How would the ISP even know what exactly you're transferring at the moment? They won't. So, because the ISP doesn't know, it can't block.” – Karan Saini. 

  

So, at what layer the obscene content is seen/known? Where can it be regulated/moderated?  

- Websites and platforms, including social media platforms and unencrypted messaging platforms. 
    
- Distribution and discovery platforms, like search engines and app stores.
    
- Hosting services and content delivery networks (CDNs).
    

  

They further asked for age-based restrictions and platform-level accountability: Vishnu Jain, who is the lawyer in a petition filed in the Supreme Court by Mahurkar and two others also sought strict laws to ban pornographic content, said India needed "a clear and enforceable framework that makes age verification meaningful and ensures that children cannot simply bypass safeguards."

  

"The objective should be straightforward: protect minors while creating accountability across the digital ecosystem," said the advocate.

  

This aligns with the on-going discussions around social media restrictions for children. Recently, Solicitor General Tushar Mehta [told](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-sc-social-media-rules-minors/) the Supreme Court that the Centre will amend the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules to stop social media platforms from allowing users under 18 to create accounts.

  

Alternately, several Indian states like [Karanataka](https://www.medianama.com/2026/03/223-karnataka-cm-social-media-ban-under-16/), [Andhra Pradesh](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-andhra-pradesh-explores-digilocker-age-tokens-social-media-curbs-children-aged-13-16/) and [Goa](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-goa-under-16-social-media-ban-australia-law/) are also considering social media restrictions for children. Read MediaNama stories from the discussions around these discussions [here](https://www.medianama.com/tag/nama-age-verification-and-restricting-social-media-for-children/).  

  

Concluding the press conference, Mahurkar said he had stressed two points that he specified in the letter to PM Modi: “While becoming a developed country Bharat can’t afford to become a culturally bankrupt nation. If Bharat wants to realise its goal of becoming a Vishwaguru it will have to take a pledge that it will become the world’s first perverted content free nation.”

A CBFC-like body for OTT would lead to pre-censorship for private viewing: Along similar lines of online content regulation, recently, the  Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) has reportedly [formed](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-rss-ott-regulation/) an informal group to examine OTT content and propose measures to address what it calls “cultural pollution” on Indian OTT platforms. It further said that it held its first meeting and will submit its recommendations to the Union Ministry of Culture and other government stakeholders. This could lead to the pre-censorship bodies like Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC) even for private viewing avenues like OTTs.
