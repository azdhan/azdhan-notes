

# SC to direct CBSE on opt-out in Aadhaar-linked APAAR ID consent forms

The Supreme Court on July 20 said it will direct the Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) to implement an Orissa High Court order requiring the APAAR consent form to explicitly give parents the option to refuse or opt out of the scheme, which mandates Aadhaar linking to APAAR ID. LiveLaw reported this first. Read the original reporting [here](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/will-ask-cbse-to-give-opt-out-option-in-apaar-consent-forms-for-students-supreme-court-in-plea-against-aadhaar-linkage-541982).  

A bench of Chief Justice of India Surya Kant, Justice Joymalya Bagchi, and Justice V Mohana was hearing a writ petition filed by parents of four students challenging the constitutional validity of APAAR (Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry), on the ground that it compels students to obtain Aadhaar IDs and does not comply with the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023.

**What the SC said:** The bench said it will pass a direction to CBSE "to implement this judgment on pan-India basis" since the Orissa High Court's order was unchallenged, and will also direct CBSE "to examine the issues as well." 

However, the CJI pushed back on the petitioners saying: "Let us not doubt everything in the country with a suspicious eye; it is a welcome step." He said a unique student ID helps CBSE headquarters track enrolment, maintain the teacher-student ratio, and support curriculum implementation.

SC also noted that CBSE's circulars are subject to the DPDP Act regardless of its own rules. 

Senior Advocate Indira Jaising, appearing for the petitioners, cited the Supreme Court's 2019 _Puttaswamy_ ruling that children cannot be compelled to enter their Aadhaar number, and argued that since APAAR IDs are linked to Aadhaar and required for exam registration, students are effectively and indirectly forced into Aadhaar linking system despite the APAAR ID being described as voluntary.

>  "Right to education is not a targeted service. The Right to education is a Constitutional right. Therefore, asking a child to get an Aadhaar and an APAAR for being sent up to exams is against the Constitution." -- Indira Jaising 

she further argued: "We are concerned with the micro-level data. It is my right to be forgotten, to withdraw consent. I don't want people to know when I am a professional whether I failed in any subject while at school."

As an alternative to striking down the scheme, Jaising sought court-mandated guidelines on consent and data safeguards, and asked that the Orissa High Court's opt-out direction be declared applicable across India.

**The Orissa High Court order:** The Supreme Court's direction stems from a December 2025 ruling by Justice Sashikanta Mishra of the Orissa High Court in _Rohit Anand Das & Anr. v. State of Odisha & Ors._, filed by the father of a kindergarten student after his school sought Aadhaar details and consent for an APAAR ID.

The Orissa High Court then held that the model consent form did not reflect the government's own position that APAAR is voluntary, since it allowed only withdrawal of consent after enrolment rather than an upfront option to refuse and therefore this gap raised legitimate privacy concerns. It relied on the Supreme Court's _Puttaswamy_ judgment to hold that children have heightened privacy protections and that education cannot be conditioned on Aadhaar.
