# Union Home Secretary defends but also apologises for internet shutdowns

Union Home Secretary defends the [recent internet shutdowns](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-internet-shutdown-new-delhi-jantar-mantar/) while 'unconditionally apologizing' for defying the Supreme Court's order that said: [“Internet facilities should function unhindered.”](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-supreme-court-internet-shutdown-delhi/)

Despite the SC order to not shut down, the internet was shut down, as [documented](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-cjp-protests-jantar-mantar-censorship-live-tracker/#:~:text=Internet%20shutdown-,continued,-despite%20Supreme%20Court) by MediaNama.  “There is no mobile Internet connectivity at ITO, around 5 kms away from Jantar Mantar. Most newspaper offices are located here including The Hindu and TOI,”  [wrote](https://x.com/vijaita/status/2108832384778940554?s=20) The Hindu’s reporter Vaijaita Singh on X. She posted this on October 10 around 1pm, when the internet shutdown was active. As the order noted, the non-cellular internet activity, like Wi-Fi, was not shutdown. 

  

[https://x.com/vijaita/status/2108832384778940554?s=20](https://x.com/vijaita/status/2108832384778940554?s=20) 

The affidavit filed by the Home Secretary also revealed that Delhi Police had requested the internet shutdowns. These are the following arguments made by the Home Secretary:

1. To 'maintain peace and tranquility': "The measures were necessitated by the call given by an organization to hold a protest without obtaining the requisite permission and in defiance of the refusal of permission, contrary to law declared by this Hon'ble Court in the judgment of Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan v. Union of India and Ors. (2018) 17 SCC 324 and in light of the recent experience of the Delhi Police on 20.07.2026."
    

He was referring to the July's Jantar Mantar protests that also witnessed [internet shutdowns](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-digital-developments-second-day-internet-shutdown-jantar-mantar/), [censorship](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-censorship-tracker-live-list-jantar-mantar-protests-new-delhi/), [surveillance](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-delhi-police-rti-reply-live-cctv-surveillance/), police violence among other things. Read MediaNama's coverage of the Day-2 developments of protests [here](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-digital-developments-second-day-internet-shutdown-jantar-mantar/).

2. ‘Top secret’ internet shutdown order was found 'proportionate': "The said request was duly processed and considered in accordance with law and was found to be a proportionate measure, having regard to the magnitude of the situation and the need to prevent any disturbance of public order in the national capital."
    

It is important to know that the internet shutdown order was not public. The public got to know about the internet shutdown only because news agency [ANI](https://x.com/ANI/status/2108549578735919394?s=20) published the so-called "top secret" order. 

Ironically, in the Anuradha Bhasin judgement, the Supreme Court has said in 2020 that the internet shutdown orders should be made public and judicially reviewable:

“It must be noted that although the Suspension Rules does not provide for publication or notification of the orders, a settled principle of law, and of natural justice, is that an order, particularly one that affects lives, liberty and property of people, must be made available …. We are therefore required to read in the requirement of ensuring that all the orders passed under the Suspension Rules are made freely available, through some suitable mechanism.

The above requirement would further the rights of an affected party to challenge the orders, if aggrieved. Judicial review of the orders issued under the Suspension Rules is always available, although no appellate mechanism has been provided, and the same cannot be taken away or made ineffective. An aggrieved person has the constitutional right to challenge the orders made under the Suspension Rules, before the High Court under [Article 226](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1712542/) of the Constitution or other appropriate forum.” - [Supreme Court (2020)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/82461587/)

3. Defined geographical area and limited time period: "The suspension of internet services for a limited period and within a defined geographical area, subject to the applicable statutory safeguards, is a recognized measure that may be considered for maintaining public order in appropriate circumstances."
    

According to the internet shutdown orders that were leaked through the media, internet was shutdown atleast twice during the October’s Jantar Mantar protests. So, there were two separate orders from the Home Minsitry. 

While the first shutdown order was for 10 pm on October 9 to 10 pm on October 10 for the 4-km radius of the Janpath–Kartavya Path, the second order was from October 10th, from 12 pm to 10 pm around the 500 meter region in eight different regions.
