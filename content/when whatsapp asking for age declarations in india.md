**# WhatsApp tests age declaration. What is ahead as per the DPDP Act?

"Add your date of birth; Upcoming laws in India require us to ask for your age. Add now," read a screenshot of WhatsApp's notice posted by [@yabhishekhd on X.](https://x.com/yabhishekhd) 

https://x.com/yabhishekhd/status/2083384904138768547?s=20 

Confirming this recent development to independent journalist Aditi Agarwal, WhatsApp spokesperson said that Meta is testing "privacy-protective ways for people to confirm their age" to comply with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP). WhatsApp didn't send this notice to everybody as it is in the testing phase. The spokesperson reportedly affirmed that this will not change how users might experience WhatsApp and will not share the users' sensitive age-related information to other users. 

Read Aditi's full report and commentary [here](https://thetechtrace.substack.com/p/edition-32-whatsapp-tests-age-self-declaration-in-india), on her Substack. 

What the DPDP Act says should happen next: The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, which is commonly referred to as the DPDP Act’s Section 9 specifically governs how platforms handle the personal data of children. The Act defines a child as any person who has not yet turned 18 years old. 

However, WhatsApp is not obligated to implement this right now as this obligation comes into force on only 13 May 2027, as per the implementation timeframe, notified on November 13, 2026. 

A few key dates:

- 13 November 2025: The Data Protection Board of India was formally established and a few certain foundational provisions took effect.
    
- 13 November 2026: Rules related to Consent Managers are supposed to take effect.
    
- 13 May 2027: The major operative provisions of the Act are scheduled to come into force, including Section 9 and the obligations around children's data processing covered in this article.
    

This is why WhatsApp is acting now, by prompting the users to declare their age. Howeve, it has over nine months before the full DPDP Act obligations will come into effect and therefore making the platform liable for its violations. The current age-check prompt is a step taken in anticipation of a legal obligation, not as a response to an obligation that is currently in force.

Before the processing any child's data, the platforms should get a parent's verifiable consent: "The Data Fiduciary shall, before processing any personal data of a child or a person with disability who has a lawful guardian, obtain verifiable consent of the parent of such child or the lawful guardian, as the case may be, in such manner as may be prescribed," reads Section 9(1) of the DPDP Act. 

It means that before WhatsApp can collect or use/process the personal data of any under-aged user who is under 18, it must obtain consent from that user's parent/legal guardian. This consent must be verifiable, meaning WhatsApp must be able to show that it actually confirmed the identity of the adult providing the consent and that the legal relationship is established and verified between the child and the adult. .

How parental consent must be obtained under the DPDP Rules: The Act says consent must be "verifiable" and "in such a manner as may be prescribed." The manner is prescribed in Rule 10 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025.

Rule 10 requires platforms to take "appropriate technical and organisational measures" to ensure verifiable parental consent is obtained. Specifically, before processing a child's data, the platform must:

- Confirm that the adult’s age, who might be claiming to be the parent/guardian has turned 18 years. 
    
- Confirm the adult’s idenity 
    
- Keep a record of these verification details for legal compliance under various relevant laws. 
    

The law offers two ways for the age verification:

- WhatsApp’s own database: If the parent is already a registered and verified user of the platform, WhatsApp can use those details to identity and age if the platform already has verified the adult’s idenity. 
    
- Through DigiLocker: The parent can submit their identity and age by submitting the government-issued valid ID themselves, or through a virtual age token from an authorised entity, like government-owned Digi Locker. 
    

DPDP Act mandated prohibits processing child data for tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted ads: 

What the law says: 

- Section 9(3): "A Data Fiduciary shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children."
    
- Section 9(2): "A Data Fiduciary shall not undertake such processing of personal data that is likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of a child."
    

Under these sections, a Data Fiduciary must not:

- Tracking a child user's activity on the platform
    
- Behavioural monitoring of the child, like building a profile of a child's habits, preferences, or patterns
    
- Targeted advertising directed at children, including personalised ads based on their user data
    
- If processing impacts the well being of the child, it shouldn’t be processed. 
    
- Shouldn’t process data that might impact the well being of the child. 
    

Therefore, by 13 May 2027, the DPDP Act will require WhatsApp to have:

- A technical mechanism to identify whether a user is under 18
    
- A technical verifiable parental consent flow that confirms the parent's identity and age 
    
- No tracking, behavioural monitoring, or targeted advertising directed at child users
    
- No processing of a child's data that could harm their well-being
    

When this applies: Both these sections are scheduled to come into force on 13 May 2027.

What happens If a platform gets this wrong: If platforms violate obligations related to children’s data breach under Section 9 of the Act can attract a penalty of up to Rs. 250 crore. This is among the highest penalty brackets in the entire Act.
