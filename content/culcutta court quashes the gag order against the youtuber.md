 **# Calcutta HC quashes 'gag order' against YouTuber who flagged high lead, mercury levels in Emami's Ashwagandha 

“The nature of a gag order stifling such free speech in the larger interest of the public," said the Calcutta High Court has set aside a trial court's against nutrition YouTuber and product-testing channel [Trustified](https://www.youtube.com/@Trustified-Certification) from publishing any content about Emami Limited's product  "Zandu Ashwagandha Gold Plus". 

The Youtuber had found that Zandu Ashwagandha Gold Plus had higher amount of lead and mercury which can endanger the health of individuals. 

The HC was referring to the trial court's May 3, 2025 order, which had restrained Mangal and his company from "displaying, airing in any manner… or publishing in writing or speaking or in any other form any statement with regard to the product… in any manner in any media including electronic media." A Division Bench of Justices Sabyasachi Bhattacharyya and Sandip Kumar De delivered the judgment on September 22, 2026. 

No commercial motive found, says HC: The Bench found no evidence of commercial motive or advertiser "material connection" that would trigger ASCI's influencer-disclosure rules, and held that Mangal's stated qualifications were enough for him to comment on nutrition and health matters. 

Applying the Bonnard standard, under which a pre-trial injunction against speech should be granted only where a defence of truth is certain to fail at trial, the Bench concluded Emami had not cleared that bar and set the injunction aside. 

"The learned Trial Judge completely overlooked the Bonnard standard in granting ad interim injunction mechanically, which is in the nature of a gag order stifling such free speech in the larger interest of the public." – Calcutta HC. 

Who is the YouTuber:  Arpit Mangal is a social media content creator who runs a product testing YouTube channel, Trustified. As the court order notes, he is also reportedly a certified sports science nutritionist and personal trainer and part of American Council of Exercise. 

What is the YouTube video about? On March 26, 2025, Trustified uploaded a YouTube video on Zandu Ashwagandha Gold Plus, a product manufactured by Emami. The video used the phrase "poison in a bottle" and called the product "cancerous". 

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bove10TKqEY&pp=0gcJCS8MAYcqIYzv](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bove10TKqEY&pp=0gcJCS8MAYcqIYzv) 

Citing the scientific findings, which are also recorded by the Culcutta HC, he flagged low levels of the active compound Withanolide, which is reportedly esssentiual for quality sleep, and dangerously high levels of lead and mercury. He had found that: 

- On Withanolide: the active compound in Ashwagandha, the product was found to contain only 0.60g per 100g, well below the levels expected for meaningful health benefit.
    
- On heavy metals, lead and mercury were found "well in excess" of the limits set by the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India (API), 
    

Trustigied used Eurofins Analytical Services India Pvt. Ltd., an FSSAI-notified, NABL and IAS-accredited laboratory, for two rounds of testing.

How did the company respond to the YouTube video:  After this video, Emami issued a legal notice to the content creator on April 17, 2025 calling the video “defamatory.” It asserted that Zandu Ashwagandha Gold Plus is a licensed Ayurvedic medicine under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940. 

The company also alleged that the YouTuber had breached the Advertising Standards Council of India's (ASCI) guidelines for health influencers, which require a medical degree or nursing qualification. 

What is the procedure followed for testing: Later, he replied stating their testing process and standing by the video. The High Court's order sets out Trustified's testing methodology in some detail, since it went to the heart of the "defence of truth" argument.

- Products are shortlisted for testing based on polls on Trustified's own website, consumer complaints, or requests from manufacturers themselves. 
    
- Two units of a product are then ordered under pseudonyms, with the ordering and unboxing recorded on screen, to preserve chain-of-custody and match batch numbers. 
    
- Sealed samples go to accredited third-party labs, and results are published regardless of outcome. The court noted this as "result agnostic". 
    
- The content creator then sends the findings and gives an opportunity to respond with in 24 hours before publication. 
    
- If they respond, the creator said they will include those comments and if they want to challenge the order, they can.
    

Why it matters: A trial court gag order against a video reporting lab-verified findings about heavy metal contamination in a consumer health product, especially without hearing the person being restrained, is a huge concern to the online free speech.

Since freedom of the press in India is not a separate right but flows from, and is exercised through, the same Article 19(1)(a) guarantee of speech and expression. The trail court’s gag order agaist Youtuber is not just a issue of free speech, but also free press, especially considering the fact that the YouTuber had followed the due diligence precautions that traditional journalism follows.
