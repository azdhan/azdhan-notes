# Inside Petition: Anti-gambling-addiction  app Gamban challenges MeitY's Section  69A blocking order  

UK-based  nine-year-old app Gamban has moved the Delhi High Court against the Ministry of Electronics and IT (MeitY), challenging a Section 69A blocking order that has kept its website and app inaccessible in India since last three years despite the app's core functionality being the blocking of access to gambling application and not enable gambling itself. MediaNama had access to the writ petition filed with the Delhi HC. 

The petition names the Union of India, through MeitY's Secretary, as the sole respondent, and is likely to be listed on July 13, 2026, or any date after, as per the petition's notice of motion. 

At the time of writing this report, the website remains inaccessible. You will require a VPN to access the website. **What is the Gamban app, and why was it blocked?**

Gamban is a UK company founded in 2017 that builds a self-exclusion tool used by people recovering from gambling addiction. 
A few key points mentioned in the petition: 
- It blocks access to gambling, betting, casino and online money-gaming apps and websites on a user's device, rather than offering any such services itself. 
- The petition claims that the app is available in 197 countries, has over six lakh registrations across more than eight lakh devices, and is ISO-certified. 
- It also reportedly has tie-ups with regulators including Norway's gambling company, [Norsk Tipping](https://www.norsk-tipping.no/spillevett/responsible-gaming/gamban), the [Welsh Government,](https://gamban.com/announcements/gamban-partners-with-wales) and gaming control boards in [Ohio](https://casinocontrol.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/cac/responsible-gambling/05-gamban) and [Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/mgcb/news/2026/04/23/free-gamban-licenses-available-for-michigan-residents).
- The petition claims that Gamban was mistakenly blocked by MeitY on February 7, 2023, while blocking a series of gambling applications under the same Section 69A of the IT Act.

**Why did MeitY blocked Gamban?** MeitY has not filed a counter-affidavit yet as the petition was only filed on July 9, and the Delhi HC is yet to hear the matter. However, as per the MeitY's response, which[ Medianama reported earlier](https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-meitys-section-69a-blocked-anti-gambling-app-gamban-3-years-no-reasons-shared/) and cited in the petition, the Ministry has responded as follows: 

- Gamban received MeitY's first response after over three years on May 6, 2025. in a short email response MeitY stated that had, "after following due process," ordered the app blocked on the following three grounds: 
	- **Data privacy concerns** for Android users, 
	- **The Super app concerns with alleged extra access:** The app allegedly operating as a "super app" capable of monitoring browser history and scanning other apps including banking apps, and 
	- **Serving more gambling ads to users:** MeitY's response claims that users are allegedly facing more gambling ads if they doesn't continue the subscription plan.
- **MeitY asks Gamban to undergo security audit without sharing the blocking order:** Following these accusations, MeitY asked Gamban to submit a response against these practices and undergo a security audit by a government-approved agency, but did not attach a copy of the actual blocking order.
- Nearly a year later, on May 5, 2026, MeitY sent a further email saying Gamban's request to be unblocked had been "forwarded to the concerned nodal agency" and that it would "revert shortly." Since thenm according to the petition, there is no response, including after Gamban's follow-up email on May 13, 2026.

MeitY has, at no point in this correspondence, shared a copy of the blocking order, complying with the rule 8 and Rule 9 of the IT Rules and it also said that the Ministry didn't offered a hearing to Gamban. In May 2021, MeitY refused to provide Section 69A blocking orders citing [‘strict confidentiality.’](https://www.medianama.com/2021/05/223-meity-section-69a-confidential/)

**What is Gamban's response to MeitY's allegations?** The petition counters  each of the three grounds MeitY cited through following points:

- **On data privacy:** Gamban says the app is GDPR-compliant, collects only data essential to its function, processes most of it on-device, and doesn't send tracking or analytics data to third parties, including crash reports and diagnostic data are auto-deleted after 14 days.
- **On the "super app" allegation:** Gamban says it does not access or scan banking or financial apps, only requests admin/accessibility permissions to detect and block gambling domains, and that Google's Play Store policy team reviewed the app and granted it a permission reserved for apps meeting the highest privacy standards.
- **On increased gambling ads:** Gamban says this is factually impossible since it shares no data with third parties and uses no tracking cookies or analytics.

The petition also argues that none of these three grounds, even if true, fall within the limited categories under which Section 69A. It further argues MeitY had no statutory basis to direct a government-approved security audit as a precondition for unblocking.

**What is the relief sought?** Gamban is asking the Delhi HC to:

- **Produce blocking order:** Ask MeitY to produce all records on the blocking, as required under Rule 15 of the Blocking Rules;
- **Present Review Committee finding after blocking:** Ask MeitY to produce the Review Committee's findings under Rule 7 on the blocking decision;
- **Revoke all blocking orders:** Quash all blocking orders issued against Gamban's website and app under Section 69A;
- **Restore access at ISPs and app stores:** Seeks restoration of access to Gamban's website and app in India, including directing Google Play Store, Apple App Store and ISPs to unblock it;

The petition also asked for the compensation of the costs because of the arbitrary blocking and any other relief the court deems fit.
