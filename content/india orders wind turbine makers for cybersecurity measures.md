# India orders wind turbine makers to submit reports on data localisation to tighten cybersecurity

The Ministry of New & Renewable Energy reportedly has sent an order to all the wind turbine manufacturers enlisted under the [Approved List](https://mnre.gov.in/en/document/1wind-turbine-models-included-in-the-rlmm-i-e-renamed-as-approved-list-of-models-and-manufacturer-almm-wind-as-per-amendment-to-rlmm-procedure-dated-31-07-2025-as-on-03-03-2026/) of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM) to submit the status report on cybersecurity compliance protocols by month-end. 

  

As per [The Hindu Businesline](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/economy/wind-turbine-makers-face-tighter-cybersecurity-norms-as-mnre-seeks-compliance-status/article71395192.ece) report these are the mandatory requirements: 

- Data centres and servers should be located in India.
    
- All wind turbine data must be stored and maintained inside India.
    
- Real-time operational data cannot be transferred outside India.
    
- Operational control of wind turbines must be conducted exclusively from a facility in India.
    
- R&D centres must be set up in India within one year from 31 July 2025.
    

  

The Ministry may have passed this order under the [notified](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/45390/mnre-notified-regarding-the-amendment-to-procedure-for-inclusionupdati/) “Procedure for inclusion/ updating Wind Turbine Model in the Revised List of Models and Manufacturers of Wind Turbines (RLMM)”: 

According to the draft amendment dated April 17, 2025, in order to strengthen the cybersecurity standards, the ministry mandated the following compliances: 

- “mandatorily locating data centre and/or servers in India. All data pertaining to wind turbine must be stored and maintained within India.
    
- transferring real-time operational data outside India shall be prohibited. Operational control of wind turbine must be conducted exclusively from a facility located within India
    
- mandatorily locating R&D centre in India within six months from issuance of this OM” 
    

  

Why it matters: Recently, on August 24, [Iran cyber-attacked](https://www.medianama.com/2026/08/223-irans-cyberattack-uks-power-plant/) one of the minor power plants in the UK and it remained shut for four days. Electricity grids, telecom infrastructure or data centers are part of very critical infrastructure that runs almost every nation's activity. If there are adverse times like wars, then these can be the potential targets, especially in the age where the wars may not happen with the real people on the real grounds. The doctrine of cyber warfare dates back to the very [origins of the internet](https://www.cfr.org/education/learn/timelines/origins-internet) itself. 

  

Thus, it is very important to have the cyber-strength to defend and as well as attack in the adverse times. For that to function, this critical infrastructure is the core. How long can a military base camp defend itself if the electricity and the communion systems are hijacked? 

  

For instance, in 2007, during the very transition days for the internet, a small Baltic country, Estonia, faced a very [major cyber attack by Russia](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2007/may/17/topstories3.russia). This incident is generally credited as the [first](https://www.cfr.org/cyber-operations/estonian-denial-of-service-incident#:~:text=2007%20was%20the-,first,-time%20that%20a) instance of a nation threatening another nation entirely through the internet. Alongside government and ministries websites, Russia reportedly attacked the banks and communication firms too. This incident happened  decade before the launch of UPI in India. Now with the advancing technologies powered by AI-capabilities, the risk factors of threats are only going to only increase. 

  

Apart from the need to strengthen the cyber-capacities, the push for data localisation also means the need and demand for more data centers. Meanwhile, while the central [DPDP Act doesn’t mandate data localisation](https://www.medianama.com/2023/08/223-summary-india-digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023/), the [sectorial regulator’](https://www.medianama.com/2023/03/223-sebi-mandates-data-loclisation-regulated-entities/)s mandatory compliances are now [trying](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-economic-survey-cross-border-ai-data/) to make data localliston requirements a standard. 

  

What did NITI Ayog say about the cyber security of power plants? In this context of the importance of electricity grids and infrastructure like wind turbines, in 2024, Indian government’s think tank NITI Ayog highlighted the cybersecurity risks of wind turbines as they are connected to the electric grids, making them potentially vulnerable if they don’t have solid cyber-defence sytems: 

  

“Wind turbines' capability to exchange information through Power Plant Controllers poses a significant cybersecurity threat. The PPC (Power Plant Controller) software is of critical importance and associated with risks- used in the device which connects the wind farm directly to the national/state grid. PPC OEMs of foreign origin especially neighboring countries, need to be examined and call needs to be taken for their suspension while not adhering to the protocol. The report emphasizes the potential risks associated with cyberattacks on wind turbines, including the compromise of grid operations, especially when managed remotely by owners stationed outside India. The need for robust security measures is crucial to safeguard national infrastructure.” – NITI Ayog (2024)
