
SEBI cautions against Influencers offering real-time trading strategies

India's stock markets regulator cautioned investors against relying on so-called "live trading strategies" being promoted on social media platforms, warning that the individuals behind such sessions are not registered to provide investment advice. 

The Securities and Exchange Board of India said in a press release that it had found certain persons on social media offering real-time trading tips and guiding viewers like when to enter or exit positions in the stock market. 

SEBI also noted that individuals to broadcast their so-called strategies or investment advise also has the live chat features that allows unregistered advisors to be exchange information in real time.

The individuals portray themselves as market experts, providing detailed analysis of market indices, entry and exit points, trading strategies and expected price targets, SEBI said. Some claim to trade live while displaying the performance of their own positions and patterns they say the market is forming based on real-time data.

SEBI advised investors not to trust such claims and to make investment decisions only through SEBI-registered intermediaries.

SEBI said market price data may be shared for investor education and awareness purposes only after 30 days and without offering monetary incentives to participants. A person engaged solely in education may not use price data from the preceding 30 days for any security, indicate future prices, or provide advice or recommendations related to securities, the circular stated. This relates to the SEBI's [Norms for sharing of real time price data to third parties](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/may-2024/norms-for-sharing-of-real-time-price-data-to-third-parties_83572.html) issued in May 2024. 

Live market data is not permitted to be shared by any entity except for the orderly functioning of the securities market or to fulfill regulatory requirements, the regulator said.

 "The real challenge is further exacerbated as it comes with the platforms themselves, especially since they can't effectively monitor all content, such as on platforms like Telegram," [said](https://www.medianama.com/2024/11/223-can-sebis-regulation-on-digital-platforms-be-justified-under-the-sebi-act-nama/) Deepak Shenoy, founder and CEO of CapitalMind, during one of MediaNama's discussion. 

> "I simply don't understand the reason why we should ban people from influencing or talking about stocks. It's like banning people influencing elections. These are things that people should decide and learn on their own at some point. It's the educational framework that allows people to learn." -- [Deepak Shenoy, CapitalMind.](https://www.medianama.com/2024/11/223-alternatives-to-sebi-online-content-regulation-approach-nama/) 

During the same discussion, Shenoy pointed out that YouTube has begun taking down videos from unregistered influencers. He added that these creators may argue that their content is no different from what TV channels do, telling viewers to buy or sell securities. They’re confused about why their videos, which are often free and don’t include affiliate links, are being targeted when their only income comes from YouTube ad revenue. "Why are you taking on my videos, which are both free and I don't get any affiliate revenue. I just make ad revenue from YouTube," he further commented.
