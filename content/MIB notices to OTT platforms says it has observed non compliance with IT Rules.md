**# MIB obsered ‘instances of noncompliance’ with IT Act; issues nine-point advisory to OTTs, online news and intermediaries

Access a copy of the advisory here: ([pdf](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/advisory-dated.pdf)) 

Without specifying which provisions were violated, the Ministry of Informaton and Broadcasting issued an [advisory](https://mib.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-11/advisory-dated.pdf) on November 2 saying that it "has observed instances of noncompliance" with the Information Technology Act, 2000 (IT Act) and its Rules by digital news publishers and OTT platforms. 

In this ambiguous context, MIB issued an advisory addressing OTT platforms, online news publshers and online intermediaries "to ensure strict adherence to the statutory provisions governing content published on online platforms, in letter and spirit."

This MIB advisory, whose copy was copied to all the self regulated bodies under the IT Rules, 2021, has asked news websites and streaming platforms to follow nine content practices, from time-stamping visuals to disclosing AI-generated media  "in letter and spirit" of the IT Act. It is signed by MIB’s Deputy Secretary Arpitha S.

The advisory matters because most of its tasks are phrased as advice, not obligation. Yet it tells publishers to follow them "in letter and spirit", and it comes weeks after two advisories from the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY). 

The advisory is addressed to:

- publishers of online curated content, which it calls "OTT Platforms";
    
- publishers of news and current affairs content on online platforms, called "Digital News Publishers"; and
    
- Intermediaries, which can include social media platofrms. 
    

What MIB has asked for

1. Time-stamping of older visuals. The ministry says "unrelated and old videos and clippings" have been "circulated in a misleading manner as being from recent events of public interest." It advises "particular care" with archival or illustrative visuals. As a "best practice", publishers should time-stamp "the original date, location, and context" on all visuals. Where visuals come from external sources, "including social media", they should also name the source.  
      
    
2. Live coverage of anti-terrorist operations to be discouraged. The advisory says "live coverage of anti-terrorist operations by the security forces is discouraged." Publishers should avoid real-time disclosure of "specific tactical positions, deployment or movement of security personnel, access routes or operational details" that may compromise an operation or endanger lives. They should report "lawful information based on the periodic briefings by an authorised officer till such operation concludes." Date- and time-stamped visuals are "encouraged" to avoid misrepresentation as live footage.  
      
    In 2025, both MietY and MIB issued notices to interemediaries and news channels after the New Delhi’s Red Fort blast. Read more about it [here](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-meity-social-media-platforms-red-fort-blast-content-penalties/).  
      
    
3. Violent or disturbing visuals should be masked. Entities addressed in the advisory should exercise "due caution, responsibility and editorial discretion." Blurring or masking is "suggested wherever warranted". The advisory also asks for "duly date and time-stamped visuals, wherever necessary."  
      
    
4. Depiction of crime and violence. This item names no addressee. It says visuals "should be based on verified facts and truth." Headlines, thumbnails and captions should not "materially misrepresent the reported event." Blurring or masking is suggested, and "requisite due diligence and sensitivity" should apply to content involving women and children.  
      
    
5. Natural calamities, disasters and major accidents. The advisory says that visuals "should be duly date and time-stamped, wherever necessary, to avoid unnecessary panic among the users." It is "encouraged to report verified facts only."  
      
    
6. Authentic map of India. OTT platforms, intermediaries and online news publishers are advised "to use only authentic maps of India, as published by the Survey of India, in all their content." For context, in 2025,  the Indian government [told](https://www.medianama.com/2025/04/223-india-govt-banned-ablo-app-over-showing-wrong-map/) Google to remove the video chat app Ablo from its Play Store after finding that the app showed an incorrect map of India.  
      
    
7. Communal harmony and debate programmes. They should "exercise due caution and discretion" when depicting religious, linguistic or other communities. Quotations, visuals, translations and allegations should be "presented in their proper context." The advisory also advises "adherence to applicable laws related to incitement to violence and unlawful promotion of enmity."  
      
    
8. Identity of victims of sexual offences . This is the only item that says "shall". The two groups "shall comply with the applicable statutory protections and directions" of the Supreme Court. It cites W.P. No. 565 of 2012, Nipun Saxena & Another v. Union of India & Others. This covers any "direct or indirect particulars, remote facts, photographs, family details, locality or other such information that may lead to identification of the victim", unless law or a court order permits it.  
      
    
9. Artificial intelligence and synthetic content: Digital news publishers are advised "to take reasonable steps to verify AI generated or materially altered photographs, videos and audio recordings". They should also "make appropriate disclosures where synthetic content is presented in connection with actual persons or events". Moreover, apparently they should also "guard against misleading representations of authentic news footage or statements." This aligns with the [MeitY’s amendments to IT Rules in February 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-meity-amendments-it-rules-synthetic-media-deepfakes/).  
      
    

Sensitise workers to IT Rules and conduct workshops if needed: It also asks the OTT platforms and digital news publishers to "strengthen their editorial and content-review mechanisms". It also asks them to ensure people who might cover these events are "adequately sensitized" to the IT Act and Rules. It says "conduct of workshops, training programmes and sensitization sessions is encouraged."

What we don't know about this MIB advisory: 

- which publishers MIB found non-compliant;
    
- which rules it considers breached;
    
- whether MIB/MietY will act on the alleged non-adherence to IT Rules; and
    
- why MIB has copied intermediaries, which MeitY normally addresses.
    

The MIB advisory follows two from MeitY in October.

- October 8: MeitY asked all social media intermediaries to exercise "enhanced diligence" over false or manipulated content that could affect public order. It also asked them to "accord priority" to complaints from "authorised Government agencies". The advisory did not define either term. Read more about it [here](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-meity-labelling-social-media-synthetic-content/). 
    
- October 10: MeitY sent an advisory only to Meta, asking it to ensure its recommendation systems do not expose users to "a single or limited set of narratives". The Internet Freedom Foundation (IFF) called it "a censorship directive in all but name". Read more about it [here](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-meity-asks-meta-diversify-narratives-cjp-protests/). 
    

Similarly, a few notices from MietY and MIB in 2025:

- November 2025: After the Red Fort blast, MeitY [asked](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-meity-social-media-platforms-red-fort-blast-content-penalties/) intermediaries of their due diligence duties under the IT Rules. It warned that non-compliance "may attract penal consequences under BNSS".
    
- November 2025: MIB issued [another notice](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-meity-social-media-platforms-red-fort-blast-content-penalties/) asking digital news publishers and private news channels for "the highest level of discretion and sensitivity" in reporting the blast.
    
- December 2025: MeitY has [asked](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-meity-advisory-social-media-curb-obscene-content/) intermediaries, including social media platforms, to curb the hosting and circulation of obscene, pornographic, vulgar, and other unlawful content on their platforms, through an advisory dated December 29, 2025
