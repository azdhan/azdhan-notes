**# Lowdown: Bihar Gambling Prohibition Act 2026. How it differs from the Centre's real-money gaming ban

By repealing the 159-year-old Public Gambling Act (1867), Bihar passed and notified Bihar Gambling (Prohibition) Act, 2026 on August 13, 2026. It means Bihar now has two laws governing online gambling alongside Center’s the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 ([PROG Act](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/LSPapersAdmin_Papers_PC_English1_11154-1.pdf)), whose rules were [notified](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-online-gaming-rules-notified-may-1-major-changes/) on April 24 2026. Here are key provisions from the Bihar Gambling (Prohibition) Act, 2026: 

Central PROG Act to prevail if there is a conflict: A person engaged in online money gaming or online gambling within Bihar could face prosecution under both the PROG Act and the Bihar Act. However, in case of inconsistency, the PROG Act will have an overriding effect over the Bihar Act. Because:  

- The PROG Act explicitly states its overriding effect over other laws under the Section 18 of the PROG Act. It further states that its provisions are in addition to, and not repeal of, any other existing law.
    
- The Section 17 of the Bihar Act states thatt it only claims overriding effect over other State laws, not the central PROG Act and it doesn’t intend to ‘derogation of any existing laws.’ This means that in any conflict between the two, the PROG Act would prevail. 
    

How does Bihar define an online game: The Bihar Act takes a broader approach, covering all gambling, including both physical and online forms. Section 2(1)(d) defines "gambling" as:

"wagering or betting, whether conducted physically or through online, electronic, digital, or virtual means, for the purpose of financial gain or reward; and any act or omission which is intended to aid, abet, facilitate, or promote wagering or betting."

So, the PROG Act and the Bihar Act define and prohibit online gaming and gambling differently. The PROG Act classifies online games into three categories:

- E-sports, which are recognised and promoted.
    
- Online social games, which are also recognised and allowed.
    
- Online money games, which are prohibited.
    

PROG Act defines an "online money game" in Section 2(1)(g) as:

"an online game, played by a user by paying fees, depositing money, or other stakes in expectation of winning monetary or other enrichment; but shall not include any e-sports."

It is important to note that the central PROG Act’s definition of online game does not distinguish between games of skill and games of chance. Whereas, the Bihar Act defines the game of chance as: 

“Game of chance” means a game in which the outcome is not predominantly determined by the knowledge, training, expertise or experience of the participant but is predominantly determined by chance or luck 

The Bihar Act lists the following games as game of change under the Schedule A: Baccarat, Big Six Wheel/wheel of fortune, Chemin-de-fer, Craps, Flush/Brag/Three Card Game, Keno, Pontoon 21, Roulette, Slots, Super Pen 9. 

The Bihar Act didn’t define and clarify games of skill: If a game's result depends mostly on skill, it may not be legally considered as gambling or as a game of chance under the Bihar Act. 

Unlike PROG Act, the Bihar Act doesn't have any provisions for “promoting” or recognising e-sports or social games.  

Promoting and sharing of gambling material is an offence: Anyone who prints, publishes, circulates, or shares digits, symbols, pictures, or any other material related to gambling, such as worli or matka, to promote or assist betting will face punishment upto 2-3 years along with financial penalties too. 

The Bihar Act flips the liability of financial services involving gambling: If someone willingly allows their bank account, mobile app, wallet, or any other financial or digital account for gambling or online gambling and earns any profit from it, they may face up to six months in prison, a fine of up to Rs 10,000, or both. Whereas PROG Act flips this liability onto the financial service providers. For instance, Section 7 of PROG Act prohibits any bank, financial institution, or person from facilitating financial transactions from permitting or aiding any transaction towards payment for an online money gaming service

Under The Bihar Act, companies are exempt from liability if they can prove that they didn’t know about gambling and followed due diligence. Additionally, if a company breaks the law under this Act, both the company and the person in charge of its business at the time of the offense will be held responsible and punished. 

Unlike OGAI, The Bihar Act relies on existing police machinery for enforcement of this Act. The Section 13 of the Bihar Act, empowers police officers to enter, search, seize, and freeze bank accounts and digital accounts used for gambling. However, under PROG Act, [Online Gaming Authority of India (OGAI),](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-online-gaming-authority-registration-form-no-submission-portal/) a dedicated regulatory body is established. OGAI is responsible for determining whether an online game is an online money game, registering e-sports and online social games, maintaining a list of prohibited online money games, and addressing user grievances.

Mandatory identity disclosure to police or Magistrate: If a person is found in a common gambling house entered by the police and, after arresting or before a Magistrate, if the individual refuses to disclose his/her name and address or gives fake details, then they may face imprisonment for up to six months, or a fine of up to Rs 5,000, or both.

If you have gambling app, you are guilty by default until proven innocent: If any mobile phone, computer, other electronic device has a gambling or betting app downloaded, the Bihar Act’s explanation states that it will be presumed for gambling or betting unless proven otherwise. This is a classic reversal of the burden of proof onto the public. 

Any register, record, electronic record, script, laptop, mobile phone, computer, other electronic or digital device containing digits, figures, signs, symbols, pictures, any combination thereof relating to gambling, other betting, shall be presumed a gambling instrument, 

No blocking powers under the Bihar Act: While the PROG Act empowers the blocking of entire computer resources (like websites, apps, platforms) used for online money gaming services, the Bihar Act has no such provision. 

The penalty structures for the both laws: While the PROG Act imposes higher fines, the Bihar Act imposes a higher maximum imprisonment for repeat offences up to 7 years, compared to the PROG Act's maximum of 5 years. 

For Online gambling, the Bihar Act’s Penalties under Section 6 are: 

- First offence: 1 to 3 years in prison and a fine of Rs 50,000–Rs 5 lakh.
    
- Subsequent offence: 2 to 7 years in prison and a fine of Rs 1–10 lakh.
    

PROG Act Penalties, under Section 9: 

- For Offering online money gaming services: 
    

-  First offence: up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to Rs 1 crore. 
    
- Subsequent offence: 3 to 5 years in prison and a fine of Rs 1-2 crore.
    

- For advertising online real-money gambling: 
    

- First offence: up to 2 years in prison or a fine of up to Rs 50 lakh. 
    
- Subsequent offence: 2 to 3 years in prison and a fine of Rs 50 lakh to Rs 1 crore.
    

- For facilitating fund transfers: 
    

- First offence: up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to Rs 1 crore. 
    
- Subsequent offence: 3 to 5 years in prison and a fine of Rs 1-2 crore.
    

Both central and Bihar laws treat a few of above-mentioned offences as cognizable and non-bailable, meaning police can arrest without a warrant, and bail is not a legally mandated right.
