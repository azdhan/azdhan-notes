Lowdown: Inside IRDAI’s consultation paper on the Public Insurance Registry [Azdhan]

IRDAI has proposed a [Public Insurance Registry](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-irdais-public-insurance-registry/) (PIR), whose consultation period ended on September 30. It is pitched as a shared Digital Public Infrastructure for insurance data that would run through the Insurance Information Bureau (IIB). The consultation paper lists over 59 scenarios where it could be useful, from portable policies to faster claims. The 

Among various other use cases presented, one of thm is an Insurance Risk Score (IRS), a "consent-based" score combining insurance history, credit bureau data and "other permitted external data sources", used as an input at underwriting or risk scoring process. The paper also proposes that the PIR could be linked to databases like VAHAN, CKYC and UIDAI, and share "anomaly signals" with law enforcement authorities, such as those under PMLA.

The paper leaves open several questions about the score itself:

- Who computes the score, and who audits it? What is the process behind scoring?
    
- Can a consumer ask for an explanation or challenge the assigned risk score?
    
- What counts as "other permitted external data sources"?
    
- Will the score be mandatory? What happens if a user doesn't consent to it?
    
- Can it be used beyond underwriting, by lenders, employers or landlords?
    

Every critique of a CIBIL score, like opaque methods, weak dispute mechanisms, reducing financial behaviour to a few financial figures, also applies here too. The paper also asks stakeholders how the PIR should progress towards notification as a Significant Data Fiduciary, which suggests that the scale of data involved is not lost on IRDAI.

If the score does not come with a defined use-case limitation, does it remain an insurance tool, or become a general indicator of financial behaviour for anyone with access to the registry? And once a registry like this exists, how long before there is pressure to use it for taxation, law enforcement or welfare eligibility?
